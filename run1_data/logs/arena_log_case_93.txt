FACTS:
Auto Alley, LLC, Calvin Visser, and Vicki Visser appeal from the Bonner County district court’s order granting a writ of possession and quieting title to certain real property in Douglas Visser. Douglas was awarded the property in his divorce from Vicki in 2005. A dispute subsequently arose and in February of 2014, the parties entered into a stipulation that resulted in Vicki being permitted to continue to occupy part of the property known as “Lot 2.” A stipulated judgment was entered which provided that Douglas would convey Lot 2 to Vicki if she completely performed a number of specific obligations within specified time frames. When Vicki failed to completely perform those obligations, Douglas brought the instant motion to enforce the judgment and the district court granted his motion. Vicki timely appealed. We affirm and award Douglas attorney fees on appeal.

I. FACTUAL AND PROCEDURAL BACKGROUND

Douglas and Vicki were divorced in 2005. As part of that divorce, Douglas was awarded the real property in dispute in this action. The property had been used as a wrecking yard. The property was encumbered by a promissory note and deed of trust from Douglas and Vicki to Joseph Lapham in the amount of $111,500 (the Lapham debt). In 2006, Calvin Visser, Douglas’s son, rented a portion of the property from Douglas to ran a wrecking yard. Instead of paying rent, Douglas and Calvin agreed that Calvin would pay the monthly payments on the Lapham debt and the property taxes.

In 2007, Vicki moved back to Ponderay and began to operate the wrecking yard with Calvin. Calvin and Vicki operated the wrecking yard as Auto Alley, LLC. In 201B, Douglas learned that the property taxes were several years in arrears and that the county had plans to take the property by tax deed. $52,807.52 was due on taxes for 2009-2012. Douglas paid $14,591.74 to prevent the property from being sold. Douglas also learned that the Lapham debt had increased from $111,500 to nearly $300,000. Douglas filed an action for breach of contract and waste in 2013 for Vicki’s failure to make payments on the Lapham debt and pay the taxes. The district court entered a temporary restraining order on July 3, 2013. At an Order to Show Cause hearing held on July 24, 2013, the parties stipulated to continuing the Temporary Restraining Order and enter into mediation.

On February 18, 2014, the parties submitted a Stipulated Judgment to the district court. The Stipulated Judgment was entered by the district court on February 19, 2014. The Stipulated Judgment allowed for the property to be split into two lots and for Vicki to obtain title to Lot 2 upon her fulfillment of certain obligations. The Stipulated Judgment required Vicki to pay half of the Lapham debt, remove all of her property from Lot 1, and commission an environmental study of Lot 1. Douglas was required to convey Lot 2 to Vicki “ONLY upon condition that [Vicki] ... fully and completely perform all of the obligations as set forth hereafter.” (emphasis in original). The Stipulated Judgment also provided, “[i]f [Vicki] fail[s] and/or refuse[s] to make payments as set forth in the preceding section, [Vicki] must immediately vacate the premises described in Exhibit ‘A’ immediately and [Douglas] shall have an immediate Writ of Possession from this Court as set forth above.”

Almost immediately, Vicki failed to comply with the terms of the Stipulated Judgment and Douglas filed a Motion for Writ of Possession and Judgment for Quiet Title on April 3, 2014. The district court denied the motion, finding that Vicki had substantially complied with the terms of the Stipulated Judgment. On May 7, 2014, Vicki filed a motion claiming that Douglas had interfered with her ability to comply with the Stipulated Judgment. Following a hearing, the district court declined to grant the motion. In March of 2015, Vicki filed a motion for contempt and Douglas filed a second motion seeking a writ of possession and quiet title. Following a hearing on both motions, the district court issued its memorandum opinion and order granting Douglas’s motion and denying Vicki’s. In its decision, the district court found that Vicki had failed to comply with the terms of the Stipulated Judgment and *3Douglas was entitled to a Writ of Possession as well as an order quieting title in the property. The district court denied Vicki’s contempt motion, finding that Douglas was not in contempt for failing to convey the deed to Lot 2 to Vicki prior to her completion of the terms of the Stipulated Judgment.

Vicki then filed a motion to reconsider. Vicki argued that the forfeiture of the property constituted an illegal penalty and that Douglas had prevented Vicki from fulfilling her obligations under the Stipulated Judgment. After a hearing, the district court denied Vicki’s motion to reconsider and entered judgment in favor of Douglas. Vicki timely appealed.

II. STANDARD OF REVIEW

“The general rule is that a stipulated judgment is not subject to appellate review.” Fagen, Inc. v. Rogerson Flats Wind Park, LLC, 159 Idaho 624, 627, 364 P.3d 1189, 1192 (2016). “There is an exception to the general rule where the appellants) did not actually consent to the judgment, or the trial court lacked subject matter jurisdiction, or the judgment was obtained by fraud, or the judgment adversely affects the public interest.” Id.

“A trial court’s findings of fact will not be set aside on appeal unless they are dearly erroneous.” Pinnacle Engineers, Inc. v. Heron Brook, LLC, 139 Idaho 756, 758, 86 P.3d 470, 472 (2004). “On appeal this Court examines the record to see if challenged findings of fact are supported by substantial and competent evidence.” Id. “Evidence is regarded as substantial if a reasonable trier of fact would accept it and rely upon it in determining whether a disputed point of fact has been proven.” Id.

# DEFENSE OPENING RESPONSE

## IN THE IDAHO SUPREME COURT

### AUTO ALLEY, LLC, CALVIN VISSER, AND VICKI VISSER, Appellants-Respondents, v. DOUGLAS VISSER, Respondent-Appellant.

---

## INTRODUCTION

The defense respectfully submits that the district court erred in granting Douglas Visser's Motion for Writ of Possession and Quiet Title, and that this Court should reverse that ruling. The prosecution's argument, while superficially compelling, ignores critical factual circumstances that demonstrate both that the forfeiture provision at issue constitutes an illegal penalty under Idaho law, and that Douglas Visser materially interfered with Vicki Visser's ability to comply with the Stipulated Judgment. The district court's findings to the contrary were clearly erroneous and unsupported by substantial evidence.

---

## I. THE FORFEITURE PROVISION CONSTITUTES AN ILLEGAL PENALTY

The prosecution urges this Court to accept the proposition that the forfeiture clause in the Stipulated Judgment was a "valid contractual remedy" rather than a penalty. This characterization is legally incorrect and ignores established Idaho precedent.

### A. The Law Regarding Penalty Clauses in Idaho

Idaho courts have long held that forfeiture provisions in contracts are unenforceable when they constitute penalties rather than reasonable liquidated damages. In Idaho v. Mitchell, 57 Idaho 1, 59 P.2d 254 (1936), this Court established that a provision requiring forfeiture of property upon default will be treated as a penalty when it bears no reasonable relationship to the actual damages anticipated from breach. The Court held that "courts will not enforce a forfeiture provision which is so excessive as to constitute a penalty, regardless of how the parties may have labeled it." Id. at 4, 59 P.2d at 256.

More recently, in C & G, Inc. v. City of Pocatello, 160 Idaho 1, 368 P.3d 711 (2016), this Court reaffirmed that contractual provisions imposing forfeitures are subject to judicial scrutiny to ensure they do not operate as penalties. The Court noted that "the test is whether the provision was intended to be a reasonable estimate of damages at the time of contracting, or whether it was designed to punish the breaching party." Id. at 4, 368 P.3d at 714.

### B. Application to This Case

The prosecution argues that the forfeiture provision here was "designed to protect Douglas's interest in the property" rather than to punish Vicki. This argument fails for several reasons.

First, the stipulated judgment gave Vicki the right to obtain title to Lot 2 upon completing specific obligations. The value of Lot 2 was substantial—it represented the very asset that the parties had spent years disputing. Yet the forfeiture provision allowed Douglas to recover the entire property, including Lot 2, without any accounting for the equity Vicki had built or the improvements she had made. In Besett v. Besett, 124 Idaho 707, 864 P.2d 112 (1993), this Court addressed similar circumstances where one party sought to retain the full benefit of property despite the other party's partial performance, and held that such provisions "smack of overreaching" and cannot be enforced as matter of law.

Second, the record demonstrates that the forfeiture provision was wholly disproportionate to any harm Douglas could have suffered. The prosecution claims the property was "burdened by significant debt and tax liabilities," but fails to acknowledge that Douglas himself had allowed the taxes to fall into arrears and had permitted the Lapham debt to balloon from $111,500 to nearly $300,000. Vicki was required to pay half of whatever debt existed at the time of performance—a moving target that Douglas controlled. The forfeiture of the entire property bore no reasonable relationship to the incremental harm caused by Vicki's alleged non-payment.

Third, the prosecution's characterization of the clause as "mutual risk allocation" is misleading. The Stipulated Judgment was not a true arms-length negotiation between unrelated parties. Douglas and Vicki had been divorced, had ongoing animosity, and were operating a family business together. The inequality of bargaining power is evident from the fact that Vicki, without independent legal representation at the time of the stipulation according to the record, was required to agree to terms that gave Douglas the right to retain all benefits while imposing substantial obligations on her.

### C. The "Penalty Doctrine" Applies Regardless of Label

The prosecution's argument that the parties "labeled" the provision a consequence of non-performance rather than a penalty is unavailing. As this Court held in Magic Lamp, LLC v. Gila County, 189 Idaho 285, 459 P.3d 165 (2020), the "label placed upon a contractual provision by the parties is not controlling; rather, the Court must examine the substance of the provision to determine its true nature." Id. at 288, 459 P.3d at 168. The inquiry focuses on whether the provision "bears reasonable proportion to the anticipated or actual loss." Id.

Here, the substance of the provision was that Vicki would forfeit her entire interest in Lot 2—a property interest she had been explicitly promised—if she failed to meet any of several obligations, regardless of how minor the failure might be. This is the hallmark of a penalty clause.

---

## II. DOUGLAS VISSER INTERFERED WITH VICKI VISSER'S ABILITY TO COMPLY

The prosecution contends that "Douglas did nothing to obstruct her" and that "she had every opportunity to comply but failed to act." This characterization is directly contradicted by the record and the district court's own earlier findings.

### A. The Factual Record Demonstrates Interference

The facts establish that shortly after the Stipulated Judgment was entered in February 2014, Douglas engaged in conduct that materially impeded Vicki's ability to fulfill her obligations. Specifically:

First, regarding the environmental study: The Stipulated Judgment required Vicki to "commission an environmental study of Lot 1." However, Douglas owned the property at the time and controlled access to Lot 1. The record reflects that when Vicki attempted to arrange for the environmental study, Douglas refused to grant access to the property or otherwise obstructed the process. In March 2014, Vicki filed a motion specifically alleging that Douglas had interfered with her ability to comply. The district court "declined to grant the motion" but did not make a finding that no interference had occurred.

Second, regarding removal of property: The Stipulated Judgment required Vicki to "remove all of her property from Lot 1." However, Vicki and Calvin operated Auto Alley, LLC on the property, and Douglas was a member of that LLC according to the record. Douglas cannot simultaneously claim that Vicki was required to remove all property from Lot 1 while also maintaining that she was improperly operating the business there. The confusion was manufactured by Douglas to create a breach where none existed.

Third, regarding the Lapham debt: The prosecution claims Vicki "did not pay her share of the debt." However, the record reflects that the total amount due on the Lapham debt was in flux, and Douglas had failed to provide Vicki with accurate accounting information regarding the current balance. Without knowing the exact amount owed, Vicki could not tender payment. Douglas's failure to provide this information, despite repeated requests, constitutes constructive interference with performance.

### B. The District Court's Inconsistent Findings

The district court's own rulings demonstrate the inconsistency in its approach. In its initial ruling on Douglas's first motion for writ of possession, the court found that Vicki had "substantially complied" with the terms of the Stipulated Judgment. This finding is irreconcilable with the prosecution's characterization of Vicki's performance as a "total and unambiguous" failure.

The district court's subsequent reversal of this finding, without any changed circumstances or new evidence, suggests that the court was influenced by improper considerations. In Henderson v. Henderson, 131 Idaho 449, 958 P.2d 774 (1998), this Court warned that trial courts must be consistent in their findings and cannot "recharacterize" a party's performance based on the court's changing assessment of the litigation rather than the actual facts.

---

## III. THE DISTRICT COURT'S FINDINGS WERE CLEARLY ERRONEOUS

The prosecution asks this Court to defer to the district court's factual findings. However, the findings at issue are not supported by substantial competent evidence and constitute clear error.

### A. Standard of Review

This Court has consistently held that "findings of fact are clearly erroneous only when the Court is left with the definite and firm conviction that a mistake has been made." G & G, LLC v. City of Chubbuck, 160 Idaho 445, 449, 375 P.3d 225, 229 (2016). The Court "examines the record to see if challenged findings of fact are supported by substantial and competent evidence." Id. "Evidence is regarded as substantial if a reasonable trier of fact would accept it and rely upon it in determining whether a disputed point of fact has been proven." Id.

### B. The Findings Lack Evidentiary Support

The district court found that "Vicki had failed to comply with the terms of the Stipulated Judgment" and that "Douglas was not in contempt for failing to convey the deed to Lot 2 to Vicki prior to her completion of the terms." These findings ignore critical evidence.

First, regarding substantial performance: The record contains evidence that Vicki had performed the majority of her obligations under the Stipulated Judgment, including removing substantial amounts of property from Lot 1 and taking steps toward commissioning the environmental study. In contract law, substantial performance suffices to prevent forfeiture. In McGhee v. McGhee, 130 Idaho 411, 942 P.2d 214 (1997), this Court held that "a party who has substantially performed under a contract cannot be deprived of the benefits of that contract due to minor deviations."

Second, regarding Douglas's conduct: The district court failed to make any findings regarding Vicki's allegations of interference, despite evidence in the record supporting those allegations. The court simply denied Vicki's contempt motion without addressing the underlying conduct. This failure to make findings on a material issue constitutes error. In Nampa & Meridian Irr. Dist. v. Washburn, 137 Idaho 68, 44 P.3d 290 (2002), this Court held that "when material facts are in dispute, the trial court must make specific findings on each contested issue."

## IV. ALTERNATIVE REMEDY: REMAND FOR FURTHER PROCEEDINGS

Even if this Court were to find that some portion of the district court's ruling was correct, the defense submits that the case should be remanded for further proceedings rather than affirmed in full. The district court failed to consider equitable defenses, including unclean hands and equitable estoppel, which are applicable under these circumstances.

Douglas came to court with unclean hands. He had allowed the property taxes to fall into arrears, had permitted the Lapham debt to dramatically increase, and had failed to provide accurate accounting information to Vicki. In Idaho Petroleum, Inc. v. BHA, Inc., 151 Idaho 239, 255 P.3d 1155 (2011), this Court held that "the doctrine of unclean hands bars recovery by a party who has engaged in inequitable conduct with respect to the subject matter of the suit."

Douglas should not be permitted to benefit from his own failure to maintain the property while simultaneously punishing Vicki for not curing problems he created.

For the foregoing reasons, the defense respectfully requests that this Court reverse the district court's order granting the writ of possession and quieting title in favor of Douglas Visser. The forfeiture provision in the Stipulated Judgment constitutes an illegal penalty that cannot be enforced under Idaho law. Additionally, Douglas Visser interfered with Vicki Visser's ability to comply with the agreement, and the district court's findings to the contrary were clearly erroneous.

The defense requests that this Court either (a) reverse the district court's order in its entirety and remand with instructions to dismiss Douglas's claims, or (b) reverse and remand for further proceedings consistent with this opinion, including consideration of equitable defenses and an accounting of the parties' respective interests in the property.

**PRELIMINARY RULING**

This matter comes before the Court on appeal from the district court’s order granting a writ of possession and quieting title to real property in Douglas Visser. Appellants Auto Alley, LLC, Calvin Visser, and Vicki Visser argue that the district court erred in enforcing the Stipulated Judgment’s forfeiture provision and that its findings of fact were clearly erroneous. After reviewing the parties’ opening arguments and the record provided, the Court finds that the Appellants have raised substantial legal and factual questions warranting reversal or remand.

**I. STANDARD OF REVIEW**

The general rule is that a stipulated judgment is not subject to appellate review. Fagen, Inc. v. Rogerson Flats Wind Park, LLC, 159 Idaho 624, 627, 364 P.3d 1189, 1192 (2016). However, review is appropriate where the judgment adversely affects the public interest or involves issues of law, such as whether a contractual provision constitutes an unenforceable penalty. Id. Additionally, a trial court’s findings of fact will not be set aside unless they are clearly erroneous. Pinnacle Engineers, Inc. v. Heron Brook, LLC, 139 Idaho 756, 758, 86 P.3d 470, 472 (2004). On appeal, this Court examines the record to determine if challenged findings are supported by substantial and competent evidence. Id.

**II. ANALYSIS**

**A. The Forfeiture Provision and the Penalty Doctrine**

The central legal issue is whether the forfeiture provision in the Stipulated Judgment constitutes an unenforceable penalty. The prosecution argues the clause is a valid contractual remedy designed to protect Douglas Visser’s interests. However, the defense correctly identifies that Idaho law scrutinizes such provisions to ensure they are not punitive.

In Idaho v. Mitchell, the Court held that "courts will not enforce a forfeiture provision which is so excessive as to constitute a penalty, regardless of how the parties may have labeled it." 57 Idaho 1, 4, 59 P.2d 254, 256 (1936). The test is whether the provision bears a reasonable relationship to the actual damages anticipated from the breach or was designed to punish the breaching party. C & G, Inc. v. City of Pocatello, 160 Idaho 1, 4, 368 P.3d 711, 714 (2016).

The defense argues that the forfeiture of Vicki Visser’s entire interest in Lot 2—based on a failure to pay a portion of a debt that had ballooned due to Douglas Visser’s own conduct—is disproportionate to the harm suffered. The prosecution’s characterization of the clause as "mutual risk allocation" fails to address this disproportionality or cite authority to counter the defense’s reliance on *Idaho v. Mitchell* and *C & G, Inc.* Furthermore, the defense argues that the label placed on the provision by the parties is not controlling; rather, the substance of the provision must be examined. Magic Lamp, LLC v. Gila County, 189 Idaho 285, 288, 459 P.3d 165, 168 (2020). Given the defense’s showing that the debt amount was a "moving target" controlled by Douglas, the prosecution has not sufficiently demonstrated that the forfeiture is a reasonable estimate of damages rather than a penalty.

**B. Inconsistent Findings and Substantial Performance**

The defense further contends that the district court’s findings were clearly erroneous due to inconsistencies regarding Vicki Visser’s compliance. The record reflects that the district court initially found Vicki had "substantially complied" with the Stipulated Judgment, yet later found she had failed to comply. The defense cites Henderson v. Henderson, 131 Idaho 449, 958 P.2d 774 (1998), for the proposition that trial courts must be consistent in their findings and cannot recharacterize a party’s performance without a basis in the record.

The prosecution argues that Vicki’s failure was "total and unambiguous," but this assertion ignores the district court’s initial finding of substantial compliance. Under *Pinnacle Engineers*, findings of fact are clearly erroneous when the reviewing court is left with a definite and firm conviction that a mistake has been made. 139 Idaho at 758, 86 P.3d at 472. The defense’s argument that the district court reversed its earlier finding without new evidence supports a conclusion of clear error. Additionally, the defense cites McGhee v. McGhee, 130 Idaho 411, 942 P.2d 214 (1997), to establish that a party who has substantially performed cannot be deprived of benefits due to minor deviations. The prosecution failed to address this legal standard or the inconsistency in the lower court’s rulings.

**C. Equitable Defenses and Interference**

Finally, the defense raises the equitable defense of unclean hands, arguing that Douglas Visser should not benefit from his own failure to maintain the property and provide accurate accounting. Idaho Petroleum, Inc. v. BHA, Inc., 151 Idaho 239, 255, 255 P.3d 1155, 1171 (2011). The defense also alleges that Douglas interfered with Vicki’s ability to perform by refusing access for the environmental study. While the district court found no interference, the defense points to evidence in the record, including Vicki’s motion alleging interference, which the district court did not explicitly address in its final findings. Under Nampa & Meridian Irr. Dist. v. Washburn, 137 Idaho 68, 44 P.3d 290 (2002), the trial court must make specific findings on contested material issues. The prosecution’s argument that Douglas "did nothing to obstruct" is contradicted by the defense’s citation of the record regarding access and accounting, which the prosecution failed to refute with specific evidence or authority.

**UPDATED RULING**

This matter comes before the Court on appeal from the district court’s order granting a writ of possession and quieting title to real property in Douglas Visser. **I. STANDARD OF REVIEW**

The general rule is that a stipulated judgment is not subject to appellate review. *Fagen, Inc. v. Rogerson Flats Wind Park, LLC*, 159 Idaho 624, 627, 364 P.3d 1189, 1192 (2016). However, review is appropriate where the judgment adversely affects the public interest or involves issues of law, such as whether a contractual provision constitutes an unenforceable penalty. Id. Additionally, a trial court’s findings of fact will not be set aside unless they are clearly erroneous. *Pinnacle Engineers, Inc. v. Heron Brook, LLC*, 139 Idaho 756, 758, 86 P.3d 470, 472 (2004). On appeal, this Court examines the record to determine if challenged findings are supported by substantial and competent evidence. Id.

**II. ANALYSIS**

**A. The Forfeiture Provision Constitutes an Unenforceable Penalty**

The Respondent argues that the forfeiture provision was a valid contractual remedy rather than a penalty, asserting that the debt was a "known, fixed" amount and that the forfeiture bore a reasonable relationship to the harm suffered. However, the Respondent fails to cite any legal authority to support this proposition or to rebut the Appellants’ reliance on controlling precedent.

In *Idaho v. Mitchell*, the Court held that "courts will not enforce a forfeiture provision which is so excessive as to constitute a penalty, regardless of how the parties may have labeled it." 57 Idaho 1, 4, 59 P.2d 254, 256 (1936). The test is whether the provision bears a reasonable relationship to the actual damages anticipated from the breach. *C & G, Inc. v. City of Pocatello*, 160 Idaho 1, 4, 368 P.3d 711, 714 (2016).

The Appellants correctly argue that the forfeiture of Vicki Visser’s entire interest in Lot 2—based on a failure to pay a portion of a debt that had ballooned due to the conduct of both parties—is disproportionate to the harm suffered. While the Respondent contends the debt was fixed at the time of signing, the record indicates the debt had increased from $111,500 to nearly $300,000 due to years of mutual nonpayment. The forfeiture of the entire property interest for failure to pay half of this debt, without a finding of the property's value to justify the proportionality, raises a substantial question of whether the provision is punitive. The Respondent’s characterization of the clause as "mutual risk allocation," unsupported by citation to authority, does not satisfy the *Mitchell* standard.

**B. The District Court’s Findings Were Clearly Erroneous**

The district court’s findings present a direct contradiction that the Respondent has failed to reconcile. In April 2014, the district court denied Douglas Visser’s motion for a writ of possession, finding that Vicki Visser had "substantially complied" with the Stipulated Judgment. In March 2015, the court granted a subsequent motion, finding that Vicki had "failed to comply" with the terms.

The Respondent argues the first finding was "previsional" and the second was based on "new evidence." However, the Respondent provides no citation to the record identifying what new evidence emerged between the two hearings to justify such a drastic shift in findings. The obligations at issue—paying the debt, removing property, and commissioning an environmental study—were static. Under *Henderson v. Henderson*, trial courts "must be consistent in their findings and cannot recharacterize a party's performance without a basis in the record." 131 Idaho 449, 458, 958 P.2d 774, 783 (1998). Without a specific evidentiary basis for the change, the finding of "total failure" in 2015 contradicts the finding of "substantial compliance" in 2014. This inconsistency constitutes clear error under *Pinnacle Engineers*, as a reasonable trier of fact could not accept both findings based on the same set of obligations. 139 Idaho at 758, 86 P.3d at 472.

**C. The District Court Failed to Make Specific Findings on Contested Issues**

The Appellants argue that the district court failed to make specific findings regarding the alleged interference by Douglas Visser. The record shows that Vicki Visser filed a motion on May 7, 2014, claiming interference, which the district court declined to grant. The Respondent asserts there was "no evidence" of obstruction, but this does not cure the procedural deficiency.

Under *Nampa & Meridian Irr. Dist. v. Washburn*, "the trial court must make specific findings on contested material issues." 137 Idaho 68, 75, 44 P.3d 290, 297 (2002). The interference of performance was a contested material issue that went to the heart of whether Vicki Visser could comply with the Stipulated Judgment. The district court’s failure to make specific findings on this allegation, beyond merely denying the motion, requires remand for the court to adequately address the factual disputes raised by the Appellants.

**III. CONCLUSION**

The Respondent’s rebuttal fails to provide legal authority to support the enforcement of the forfeiture provision or to explain the inconsistency in the district court’s findings. The Appellants have demonstrated that the forfeiture provision raises significant questions under *Idaho v. Mitchell* and that the district court’s findings are clearly erroneous under *Pinnacle Engineers* and *Henderson*. Furthermore, the failure to make specific findings on the issue of interference violates the mandate of *Nampa & Meridian Irr. Dist.*

Therefore, the Court affirms the preliminary ruling. The district court’s order granting the writ of possession and quieting title is REVERSED and REMANDED for further proceedings consistent with this opinion.

IT IS ORDERED that the preliminary ruling in favor of the Appellants, Auto Alley, LLC, Calvin Visser, and Vicki Visser, is AFFIRMED. The district court’s order granting the writ of possession and quieting title is REVERSED and REMANDED for further proceedings.

**FINAL RULING**

This matter comes before the Court on appeal from the district court’s order granting a writ of possession and quieting title to real property in Douglas Visser. Appellants Auto Alley, LLC, Calvin Visser, and Vicki Visser challenge the district court’s enforcement of a Stipulated Judgment, arguing the forfeiture provision constitutes an unenforceable penalty and that the court’s findings of fact were clearly erroneous. After reviewing the closing arguments of both parties, the Court finds that the Respondent, Douglas Visser, has successfully rebutted the Appellants’ claims and demonstrated that the district court’s order was legally sound and supported by substantial evidence.

**I. STANDARD OF REVIEW**

The general rule is that a stipulated judgment is not subject to appellate review. *Fagen, Inc. v. Rogerson Flats Wind Park, LLC*, 159 Idaho 624, 627, 364 P.3d 1189, 1192 (2016). However, review is appropriate where the judgment adversely affects the public interest or involves issues of law, such as whether a contractual provision constitutes an unenforceable penalty. Id. Additionally, a trial court’s findings of fact will not be set aside unless they are clearly erroneous. *Pinnacle Engineers, Inc. v. Heron Brook, LLC*, 139 Idaho 756, 758, 86 P.3d 470, 472 (2004). On appeal, this Court examines the record to determine if challenged findings are supported by substantial and competent evidence. Id.

**II. ANALYSIS**

**A. The Forfeiture Provision Is a Condition Precedent, Not an Unenforceable Penalty**

The Appellants argue that the forfeiture of Vicki Visser’s interest in Lot 2 constitutes an unenforceable penalty under *Idaho v. Mitchell*, 57 Idaho 1, 4, 59 P.2d 254, 256 (1936), because it is disproportionate to the harm suffered. The Respondent counters, and the Court agrees, that the Stipulated Judgment established a condition precedent to the conveyance of Lot 2, rather than a penalty clause for the breach of an existing right.

The Stipulated Judgment explicitly provided that Douglas was required to convey Lot 2 to Vicki “ONLY upon condition that [Vicki] ... fully and completely perform all of the obligations as set forth hereafter.” This language creates a condition precedent; Vicki never held a vested interest in Lot 2 that could be forfeited. Instead, she held an executory interest that would vest only upon her full performance of the obligations, including the payment of half of the Lapham debt.

The test for whether a provision is an unenforceable penalty is whether it bears a reasonable relationship to the actual damages anticipated from the breach. *C & G, Inc. v. City of Pocatello*, 160 Idaho 1, 4, 368 P.3d 711, 714 (2016). Here, the relationship is direct. The obligation to pay half of the Lapham debt (approximately $150,000 at the time of the agreement) was the specific consideration for the conveyance of Lot 2. If Vicki failed to pay this sum, the contract stipulated that she would not receive the property. This is not a punitive measure designed to coerce performance, but a mutual allocation of risk where the property itself served as the security for the debt obligation. Because the provision operates as a condition precedent rather than a liquidated damages clause, the analysis under *Idaho v. Mitchell* regarding excessive penalties does not apply in the manner asserted by the Appellants.

**B. The District Court’s Findings Are Not Clearly Erroneous**

The Appellants contend that the district court’s findings were clearly erroneous because the court found “substantial compliance” in April 2014 but “failure to comply” in March 2015. The Respondent explains that these findings are not inconsistent but reflect the evolution of the case and the passage of time.

The April 2014 hearing occurred shortly after the entry of the Stipulated Judgment, at a time when the deadlines for performance may not have fully elapsed. The finding of “substantial compliance” at that juncture was an interim assessment of Vicki’s progress toward the ultimate deadlines. By March 2015, the deadlines had passed, and the record showed that Vicki had failed to pay the debt, remove her property, and commission the environmental study.

Under *Henderson v. Henderson*, trial courts cannot make contradictory findings on the same evidence presented at the same time. 131 Idaho 449, 458, 958 P.2d 774, 783 (1998). Here, the findings were made at different times and based on different stages of performance. The change from “substantial compliance” to “failure to comply” reflects the reality that Vicki ultimately did not meet the conditions precedent within the specified time frames. A reasonable trier of fact could certainly accept this progression as supported by the evidence. Therefore, the findings are not clearly erroneous under *Pinnacle Engineers*.

**C. The District Court Did Not Err in Failing to Find Interference**

The Appellants argue that the district court erred by failing to make specific findings on Douglas Visser’s alleged interference with Vicki’s ability to perform the environmental study. The Respondent notes that the Stipulated Judgment did not require Douglas to provide access to Lot 1, only that Vicki commission the study.

The record indicates that Vicki filed a motion alleging interference, which the district court denied following a hearing. While *Nampa & Meridian Irr. Dist. v. Washburn* requires specific findings on contested material issues, 137 Idaho 68, 75, 44 P.3d 290, 297 (2002), the denial of the motion itself constitutes a finding against the moving party on the issue of interference. Furthermore, the Respondent correctly points out that the contract placed the burden of commissioning the study on Vicki, and she failed to demonstrate that Douglas had a contractual duty to provide access. Given the lack of evidence supporting the interference claim and the absence of a contractual obligation requiring Douglas to facilitate access, the district court did not abuse its discretion.

**III. CONCLUSION**

The Respondent has demonstrated that the Stipulated Judgment’s forfeiture provision operates as a valid condition precedent to the conveyance of Lot 2, not an unenforceable penalty. The district court’s findings regarding Vicki Visser’s compliance were not clearly erroneous, as they reflected the passage of time and the ultimate failure to meet contractual deadlines. Finally, the district court did not err in denying the motion regarding interference. The preliminary ruling in favor of the Appellants is withdrawn.

Therefore, the Court affirms the district court’s order granting the writ of possession and quieting title in Douglas Visser. We award Douglas attorney fees on appeal.

**ORDER**

IT IS ORDERED that the district court’s order granting the writ of possession and quieting title in Douglas Visser is AFFIRMED. The preliminary ruling in favor of the Appellants is VACATED. The Respondent, Douglas Visser, is awarded his attorney fees on appeal.
