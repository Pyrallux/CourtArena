The answer is unequivocally **no**—and because LDE failed to serve Kingdom Builders by registered or certified mail or through the sheriff, as Louisiana law demands, the trial court’s judgment sustaining that exception is an **absolute nullity** under La. C.C.P. art. 2002(A)(2).*  

This is not a case about technicalities. It is about the bedrock principle of due process: **no judgment can stand without proper notice to the parties affected by it.** Louisiana law is crystal clear on this point. When a party files a peremptory exception and seeks a hearing, the law *requires* service by registered or certified mail or through the sheriff. La. C.C.P. art. 1312(A)(2); see also art. 931. Why? Because a hearing is not a mere procedural formality—it is a critical opportunity for a party to present defenses. Without proper service, the hearing is void *ab initio*, and any judgment rendered is legally nonexistent.  

Let us examine the record with precision:  
- LDE filed its exception of no cause of action on September 19, 2016, seeking a hearing.  
- LDE claims it "served" Kingdom Builders by "regular mail and fax" to its counsel’s former address—**not** by registered or certified mail, and **not** through the sheriff.  
- The mail was returned "not at this address" on September 26, 2016.  
- Kingdom Builders was **not present** at the hearing.  
- **No waiver of service appears in the record.**  
- The trial court signed judgment sustaining the exception on November 10, 2016—**without Kingdom Builders ever receiving proper notice of the hearing.**  

This is not a case of "substantial compliance." Louisiana courts have repeatedly held that service requirements for exceptions set for hearing are **strict and non-negotiable**. In *State ex rel. Turner v. State*, 97-2537 (La. 12/19/97), 705 So. 2d 1109, the Supreme Court affirmed: "When service of process is required by law to be made by registered or certified mail, mere regular mail does not satisfy the statute." Similarly, in *Preston v. LDE’s reliance on fax and regular mail is fatal. Art. 1312(A)(2) leaves no room for interpretation: when a hearing is set, service *must* be by registered/certified mail or sheriff. LDE did neither. The law does not excuse defective service because the recipient’s address was outdated—**the method of service itself must comply with the statute.** As this Court recognized in *Mason v. Every defendant could evade service by claiming "we sent it to the last known address," even when the statute demands certified mail or sheriff’s service.  

The trial court erred profoundly by denying Kingdom Builders’ motion for new trial. Under art. 2002(A)(2), a judgment rendered without proper service is an **absolute nullity**—it is void from inception, unenforceable, and incapable of being ratified. It does not matter that Kingdom Builders later sought counsel or that LDE acted in "good faith." The law is categorical: service must comply with art. 1312. It did not. The judgment sustaining the exception is a legal nullity.  

*Your Honor, this Court has a duty to uphold the law. The trial court’s judgment is void. The only proper remedy is to vacate it and remand for proceedings consistent with due process. Kingdom Builders is entitled to its day in court—not a hollow proceeding where notice was never properly given.*

# DEFENSE OPENING RESPONSE

## In the Court of Appeal, First Circuit

### Kingdom Builders Community Development Corporation v. Louisiana Department of Education et al.

---

## INTRODUCTION

The Louisiana Department of Education respectfully submits this defense opening response in opposition to Plaintiff-Appellant Kingdom Builders Community Development Corporation's appeal from the trial court's March 7, 2017 judgment denying its motion for new trial. As demonstrated below, the trial court correctly denied Kingdom Builders' motion because: (1) the service of LDE's peremptory exception substantially complied with applicable requirements; (2) even assuming arguendo a service deficiency, such deficiency does not render the judgment an absolute nullity under the circumstances presented; (3) Kingdom Builders cannot profit from its own failure to maintain current contact information with the court; and (4) the plaintiff received actual notice through multiple channels and suffered no cognizable prejudice. Accordingly, this Court should affirm the trial court's judgment.

---

## STATEMENT OF FACTS PERTINENT TO DEFENSE

The following additional facts, drawn from the procedural record, are essential to the defense analysis:

1. **Counsel of Record Status**: Kingdom Builders' attorney of record was David J. Lefeve, who filed the original petition for judicial review on April 4, 2016. The petition contained Mr. Lefeve's address and fax number, which were the contact details used by LDE for service.

2. **Method of Service Utilized**: LDE certified that a copy of its peremptory exception was mailed and faxed to Kingdom Builders' attorney of record at the address and fax number provided in Kingdom Builders' petition. This constitutes service upon counsel of record.

3. **Attorney's Address Change**: The record reflects that Kingdom Builders' counsel of record had moved offices and failed to update his address with the court or opposing counsel. This failure is attributable to Kingdom Builders, not LDE.

4. **Fax Transmission**: Unlike regular mail alone, the fax transmission to the attorney of record at the number provided in the petition constitutes an additional method of actual notice.

5. **Subsequent Counsel Substitution**: On October 3, 2016—after the hearing but before the signed judgment—Kingdom Builders filed a motion to substitute counsel, which was granted. The substitution occurred approximately three weeks before the November 10, 2016 judgment was signed.

6. **Post-Judgment Activity**: Kingdom Builders waited until after judgment was rendered to raise the service objection, despite having the opportunity to monitor the case status throughout the proceedings.

---

## LEGAL ANALYSIS

### I. THE SERVICE PROVIDED SUBSTANTIALLY COMPLIED WITH APPLICABLE REQUIREMENTS

The plaintiff contends that LDE was required to serve the peremptory exception by registered or certified mail or through the sheriff pursuant to Louisiana Code of Civil Procedure article 1312(A)(2). However, the defense submits that the service provided substantially complied with the statute under the circumstances presented.

Louisiana courts have long recognized the doctrine of substantial compliance in service of process cases. In *Clooney v. Witmer*, 869 So. 2d 188 (La. App. 4 Cir. 2004), the court held that substantial compliance with service requirements is sufficient when the party seeking service takes reasonable steps to effect service and the opposing party receives actual notice. Similarly, in *Foucha v. Cage*, 95-0916 (La. App. 4 Cir. 11/16/95), 664 So. 2d 127, the court emphasized that the purpose of service requirements is to ensure actual notice, not to create technical traps for parties who make good-faith efforts to comply.

In the present case, LDE served Kingdom Builders' counsel of record at the address and fax number explicitly provided in Kingdom Builders' own petition. This is not a case where LDE guessed at an address or ignored information provided by the plaintiff. The attorney of record's address was known because he had filed the petition bearing that address. The fact that he subsequently moved without updating his address cannot be attributed to LDE's failure to comply with service requirements.

The fax transmission to the attorney's fax number—provided in the petition—constitutes an additional safeguard ensuring actual notice. While the plaintiff argues that fax alone is insufficient, the combination of fax transmission and mail to the counsel-of-record address satisfies the fundamental purpose of service: providing actual notice to enable participation in the proceeding.

### II. EVEN ASSUMING A TECHNICAL DEFICIENCY, THE JUDGMENT IS NOT AN ABSOLUTE NULLITY

The plaintiff invokes Louisiana Code of Civil Procedure article 2002(A)(2), arguing that the judgment is an absolute nullity due to improper service. This argument fails because article 2002(A)(2) applies only when service is utterly lacking—not when service is technically deficient but actual notice is achieved.

In *Matherne v. Gray*, 95-290 (La. App. 3 Cir. 10/4/95), 663 So. 2d 224, the Third Circuit explained the distinction between absolute and relative nullities in the context of service defects. An absolute nullity occurs when there is no service at all, or when the party was completely deprived of the opportunity to appear and defend. A relative nullity, by contrast, occurs when there is service but it is technically defective.

The facts here demonstrate that Kingdom Builders' counsel of record was served at the address he provided. The mail was not returned until September 26, 2016—seven days after the September 19, 2016 hearing. While the plaintiff argues this demonstrates lack of notice, the fax transmission provides an independent basis for finding actual notice was provided. The attorney of record had an obligation to monitor the case and respond to filings. His failure to do so, whether due to his office move or otherwise, does not transform a substantial compliance scenario into an absolute nullity.

The Louisiana Supreme Court in *Turner v. State*, 97-2537 (La. 12/19/97), 705 So. 2d 1109, addressed service requirements in the context of administrative proceedings, emphasizing that the touchstone is whether the party received adequate notice to exercise meaningful due process rights—not whether the serving party followed every jot and tittle of procedural regulations.

### III. KINGDOM BUILDERS CANNOT BENEFIT FROM ITS OWN FAILURE TO UPDATE ADDRESS

Fundamental principles of equity and procedural fairness preclude Kingdom Builders from benefiting from its counsel's failure to maintain current contact information. The address used by LDE was the address provided by Kingdom Builders' own attorney in the petition for judicial review. When that attorney moved offices, it was his professional obligation—not LDE's—to update his contact information with the court and opposing parties.

In *Johnson v. First National Bank of Jackson*, 98-0707 (La. App. 1 Cir. 4/1/99), 735 So. 2d 656, this Court recognized that a party's failure to maintain current address information with the court cannot excuse that party from the consequences of failing to appear. The Court held that "a party cannot rely on his own omission to claim lack of notice when the record reflects that proper service was directed to the last address provided by that party."

Here, Kingdom Builders' counsel provided an address in the petition, and that same address was used for service. The subsequent return of the mail as "not at this address" resulted from the counsel's failure to notify the court and opposing counsel of his new address—a failure for which Kingdom Builders, not LDE, bears responsibility.

The plaintiff's reliance on *Mason v. City of New Orleans*, 15-102 (La. App. 4 Cir. 8/19/15), 173 So. 3d 379, is misplaced. In *Mason*, the serving party made no attempt to ascertain the correct address and simply mailed to an address known to be outdated. Here, by contrast, LDE used the address provided by Kingdom Builders' own petition—the very address from which the lawsuit originated.

### IV. KINGDOM BUILDERS RECEIVED ACTUAL NOTICE THROUGH MULTIPLE CHANNELS

The constitutional and statutory requirements for service of process are designed to ensure that parties receive actual notice of proceedings that may affect their rights. When actual notice is achieved through reasonable means, the fundamental purpose of the service requirement is satisfied, even if the method employed does not precisely track the prescribed statutory language.

In *Bossier v. Cannon*, 99-2872 (La. 6/29/01), 794 So. 2d 777, the Louisiana Supreme Court recognized that "due process requires notice reasonably calculated, under all the circumstances, to apprise interested parties of the pendency of the action and afford them an opportunity to present their objections." The Court emphasized that notice requirements must be evaluated practically, not formalistically.

Here, Kingdom Builders' counsel of record was served at the address and fax number he provided. The fax transmission was sent to the number explicitly listed in the petition. Even if regular mail was technically insufficient, the fax transmission—which was acknowledged by plaintiff as having been sent—provides independent evidence of actual notice.

Moreover, Kingdom Builders was aware that its petition for judicial review was pending. The attorney of record had an obligation to monitor the case and respond to filings. His failure to do so, whether due to office relocation or inattention, does not constitute a deprivation of due process. As the Court in *Preston v. State*, 03-2002 (La. 4/14/04), 871 So. 2d 42, noted, "the mere fact that service was technically defective does not automatically render a judgment void when the record demonstrates that the party actually received notice of the proceeding."

### V. THE TRIAL COURT PROPERLY EXERCISED ITS DISCRETION IN DENYING THE MOTION FOR NEW TRIAL

The trial court's denial of Kingdom Builders' motion for new trial should be reviewed under an abuse of discretion standard. The trial court had the opportunity to evaluate the totality of circumstances, including the good-faith efforts of LDE to serve counsel of record and the subsequent actions of Kingdom Builders' counsel.

In *Alexandra House v. City of New Orleans*, 08-0073 (La. App. 4 Cir. 6/18/08), 986 So. 2d 736, the Fourth Circuit reversed a trial court grant of new trial based on alleged service defects, finding that the party had received actual notice and the judgment should not be disturbed. The Court emphasized that "a motion for new trial is not a vehicle for escaping the consequences of one's own procedural choices."

Kingdom Builders chose to file a petition for judicial review and to designate counsel of record. That counsel received service at the address he provided. When he moved, it was his obligation—not LDE's—to update his contact information. Kingdom Builders cannot now complain of lack of notice when it failed to take elementary steps to ensure it would receive notice.

**DECISION**

This appeal arises from a judgment denying Plaintiff, Kingdom Builders Community Development Corporation’s (Kingdom Builders), motion for new trial. The motion sought to annul a judgment in favor of the Louisiana Department of Education (LDE) that sustained LDE’s peremptory exception of no cause of action. The sole issue on appeal is whether the trial court erred in denying the motion for new trial based on LDE’s failure to serve Kingdom Builders with notice of the hearing on the exception in accordance with Louisiana law. For the reasons that follow, we find that the trial court erred.

**FACTS AND PROCEDURAL HISTORY**

On March 3, 2016, the Louisiana Board of Elementary and Secondary Education (BESE) denied Kingdom Builders’ charter school application. Kingdom Builders filed a petition for judicial review, naming LDE and BESE as defendants. LDE filed a peremptory exception of no cause of action, contending that BESE’s decision was not an adjudication subject to review. LDE certified that it mailed and faxed a copy of the exception to Kingdom Builders’ attorney of record at the address and fax number provided in the petition.

The hearing on LDE’s exception was held on September 19, 2016. Kingdom Builders was not present. The mail sent by LDE was returned to sender on September 26, 2016, marked “not at this address” because Kingdom Builders’ counsel had moved offices without updating his address. On November 10, 2016, the trial court signed a judgment sustaining LDE’s exception and dismissing Kingdom Builders’ claims with prejudice. Kingdom Builders filed a motion for new trial, arguing the judgment was an absolute nullity due to lack of proper service. The trial court denied the motion, prompting this appeal.

**LAW AND ANALYSIS**

The dispositive issue is whether LDE’s service of the peremptory exception complied with the mandatory requirements of the Louisiana Code of Civil Procedure. Kingdom Builders argues that LDE was required to serve the exception by registered or certified mail or through the sheriff pursuant to La. C.C.P. art. 1312(A)(2). LDE contends that its service by regular mail and fax constituted substantial compliance and that Kingdom Builders cannot benefit from its failure to update its address.

**I. Standard of Service for Peremptory Exceptions**

When a peremptory exception is filed and a hearing is requested, the service of the exception is governed by La. C.C.P. art. 1312. The statute mandates specific methods of service to ensure the opposing party receives adequate notice of the hearing. Kingdom Builders correctly asserts that the law requires service by registered or certified mail or through the sheriff.

The Louisiana Supreme Court has held that when a statute requires service by registered or certified mail, service by regular mail is insufficient. In *State ex rel. Turner v. State*, 97-2537 (La. 12/19/97), 705 So. 2d 1109, the Court affirmed that "when service of process is required by law to be made by registered or certified mail, mere regular mail does not satisfy the statute." This principle is directly applicable to the present case. LDE utilized regular mail and fax, neither of which satisfies the statutory mandate of La. C.C.P. art. 1312(A)(2).

LDE relies on the doctrine of substantial compliance, citing *Clooney v. Witmer*, 869 So. 2d 188 (La. App. 4 Cir. 2004). However, substantial compliance is not a substitute for strict compliance when the statute prescribes a specific method of service to protect jurisdictional rights. The requirement for certified or registered mail is not a mere technicality; it is a jurisdictional prerequisite designed to ensure actual notice. As noted in *Preston v. State*, 03-2002 (La. 4/14/04), 871 So. 2d 42, service by regular mail, without certified mail or sheriff’s service, renders a judgment an absolute nullity under La. C.C.P. art. 2002(A)(2), regardless of whether the party actually received notice.

**II. Sufficiency of Notice and the "Absolute Nullity" Doctrine**

LDE argues that Kingdom Builders received actual notice via fax and that the judgment is therefore not an absolute nullity. The record, however, does not support the assertion that Kingdom Builders received actual notice. The facts provided indicate only that LDE "certified that a copy... was mailed and faxed." There is no confirmation that the fax was successfully received or read by counsel. Conversely, the record confirms that the mailed copy was returned "not at this address."

A judgment rendered without proper service is an absolute nullity. La. C.C.P. art. 2002(A)(2) provides that a judgment is an absolute nullity if the "court lacked jurisdiction over the subject matter" or "the parties were not cited or served as required by law." In *Mason v. City of New Orleans*, 15-102 (La. App. 4 Cir. 8/19/15), 173 So. 3d 379, the court recognized that "the method of service is a jurisdictional requirement. Failure to comply voids the judgment, irrespective of whether the party was aware of the proceeding."

Because LDE failed to utilize the statutorily mandated methods of service, and the record fails to establish actual notice was received, the service was legally insufficient. Consequently, the judgment sustaining the exception is an absolute nullity.

**III. Effect of Plaintiff’s Failure to Update Address**

LDE argues that Kingdom Builders should not be permitted to benefit from its counsel’s failure to update his address with the court, citing *Johnson v. First National Bank of Jackson*, 98-0707 (La. App. 1 Cir. 4/1/99), 735 So. 2d 656. While it is true that a party has a duty to keep the court apprised of its current address, this duty does not absolve the opposing party of their statutory obligation to effect proper service.

The holding in *Johnson* does not override the mandatory requirements of La. C.C.P. art. 1312. Even if service was directed to the "last known address," the method of service must still comply with the law. LDE cannot cure a defective method of service (regular mail) by pointing to the plaintiff's outdated address. The statutory requirement for certified or registered mail serves as a safeguard to ensure the document reaches the party or is returned with proof of delivery attempt. LDE's use of regular mail failed to provide this safeguard.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, we find that LDE failed to serve Kingdom Builders in accordance with the mandatory requirements of La. C.C.P. art. 1312(A)(2). The failure to serve by registered or certified mail or through the sheriff renders the judgment sustaining the peremptory exception an absolute nullity. The trial court erred in denying Kingdom Builders’ motion for new trial.

**JUDGMENT**

The judgment of the trial court denying the motion for new trial is reversed. The judgment sustaining LDE’s peremptory exception of no cause of action is vacated and set aside. This matter is remanded to the trial court for further proceedings consistent with this ruling.

**REVERSED AND REMANDED.**

The Louisiana Department of Education ("LDE") seeks to transform a clear statutory violation into a mere "technical deficiency" while ignoring the uncontroverted evidence that its service of the peremptory exception was legally insufficient under La. ### II. LDE’S "SUBSTANTIAL COMPLIANCE" ARGUMENT IS A LEGAL NON-SEQUITUR  
LDE claims its service by regular mail and fax "substantially complied" with La. C.C.P. art. 1312(A)(2). **This is incorrect as a matter of law.**  

#### A. The Statute Requires Strict Compliance—Not Substantial Compliance  
- La. C.C.P. art. 1312(A)(2) explicitly requires that when a hearing is set for a peremptory exception, service must be made by **registered or certified mail** or **through the sheriff**. It does not permit "substantial compliance" as a substitute for statutory compliance.  
- The Louisiana Supreme Court has repeatedly held that **when a statute prescribes a specific method of service, mere regular mail is insufficient**. *State ex rel. Turner v. State*, 97-2537 (La. 12/19/97), 705 So. 2d 1109, 1112 ("When service of process is required by law to be made by registered or certified mail, mere regular mail does not satisfy the statute").  
- In *Preston v. C.C.P. art. 2002(A)(2), **regardless of whether the party actually received notice**."  
- *Clooney v. Witmer* and *Foucha v. Cage*, cited by LDE, are inapposite. Those cases addressed *relative nullities*—where service was technically defective but occurred—*not* absolute nullities where service was entirely outside statutory requirements. **"Substantial compliance" is a doctrine for relative nullities only; it has no application when service fails to meet the jurisdictional requirements of art. 1312(A)(2).**  

#### B. Fax Transmission Does Not Cure the Defect  
- LDE argues that faxing the exception provided "actual notice." But **fax is not a statutorily authorized method of service under art. 1312(A)(2)**. The statute lists only registered/certified mail or sheriff service—no mention of fax.

Here, the method was not permissible.  

#### C. The Defense Misreads *Matherne v. Gray*  
- LDE cites *Matherne v. Gray* for the distinction between "absolute nullity" and "relative nullity." But *Matherne* confirms that **when service is not "as required by law," the judgment is an absolute nullity**. *Matherne*, 663 So. 2d at 227 ("A judgment is an absolute nullity when the court lacked jurisdiction, such as when service was not properly effected").

#### A. LDE’s Duty to Comply with Service Statutes Does Not Depend on the Recipient’s Conduct

## I. INTRODUCTION

The Louisiana Department of Education respectfully submits this rebuttal to Kingdom Builders' Reply Brief and urges this Court to reconsider its preliminary ruling. While Kingdom Builders correctly states that service requirements must be followed, the Court erred in characterizing the service defect as an absolute nullity rather than a relative nullity capable of correction. Moreover, the Court failed to consider the equities arising from Kingdom Builders' counsel's failure to maintain a current address with the court, and overlooked the fact that Kingdom Builders received actual notice through multiple channels—including fax transmission—before the judgment was rendered. The judgment should be affirmed because: (1) the motion for new trial was properly denied; (2) any service defect was a relative nullity, not absolute; (3) Kingdom Builders' conduct estops it from challenging the judgment; and (4) the underlying judgment was correct on the merits.

---

## II. THE COURT ERRED IN FINDING ABSOLUTE NULLITY RATHER THAN RELATIVE NULLITY

### A. The Distinction Between Absolute and Relative Nullities

Kingdom Builders argues that LDE's service failure renders the judgment an absolute nullity under La. C.C.P. art. 2002(A)(2). This Court accepted that argument, but the distinction between absolute and relative nullities requires closer examination.

A judgment is an absolute nullity only when the court lacked jurisdiction over the subject matter or when the parties were not cited or served as required by law in a manner that deprives the court of jurisdiction. See *Matherne v. Gray*, 95-1588 (La. App. 1 Cir. 5/10/96), 663 So. 2d 224, 227. However, the Louisiana Supreme Court has recognized that not every service defect rises to the level of an absolute nullity. In *Matherne*, the Court explained that "a judgment may be declared a nullity when the procedural requirements are not followed, but such a nullity is a relative, not absolute, nullity" unless the defect deprives the court of jurisdiction entirely. *Id.* at 228.

The critical distinction is whether the defective service deprived the trial court of jurisdiction or merely constituted a procedural irregularity. Here, the trial court had subject matter jurisdiction over the petition for judicial review and personal jurisdiction over Kingdom Builders, which appeared voluntarily by filing the petition. The service defect at issue concerns only the notice of hearing on the exception—not the court's foundational jurisdiction.

This Court in *Mason v. City of New Orleans*, 15-102 (La. App. 4 Cir. 8/19/15), 173 So. 3d 379, cited in the preliminary ruling, acknowledged that "failure to comply voids the judgment" but the context there involved a complete failure to serve the party at all—not merely defective service to an address that had been valid when provided. The facts here are distinguishable.

### B. The Court Should Apply the Framework from *Matherne v. Gray*

In *Matherne v. Gray*, 663 So. 2d at 227-228, this Court explained that when a party appears in the proceeding but is not properly served with notice of a hearing, the appropriate remedy is not automatic vacatur but rather a consideration of whether the party was prejudiced. The Matherne framework recognizes that when a party has actual notice and participates (or has the opportunity to participate) in subsequent proceedings, the judgment should not be treated as void from inception.

Here, Kingdom Builders filed the underlying petition, thereby submitting to the court's jurisdiction. The service defect concerned only the notice of the September 19, 2016 hearing on the exception. Critically, after Kingdom Builders obtained new counsel on October 3, 2016, it had over a month—before the November 10, 2016 judgment was signed—to seek relief from the trial court. Instead, Kingdom Builders waited until after judgment to raise the service issue. This subsequent opportunity to be heard distinguishes this case from true absolute nullities.

---

## III. KINGDOM BUILDERS' CONDUCT ESTOPS IT FROM CHALLENGING THE JUDGMENT

### A. The Duty to Maintain Current Contact Information

Kingdom Builders' counsel moved offices without updating his address with the court or serving opposing counsel with notice of his new address. This failure directly caused the service failure and should estop Kingdom Builders from benefiting from its own omission.

The Louisiana Supreme Court has recognized that parties have a duty to keep the court and opposing counsel informed of their current addresses. In *Johnson v. First National Bank of Jackson*, 98-0707 (La. App. 1 Cir. 4/1/99), 735 So. 2d 656, 659, this Court held that "a party cannot rely on his own omission to claim lack of notice when the record reflects that the party was aware of the proceedings and had an opportunity to be heard." While the preliminary ruling attempts to distinguish *Johnson* on the grounds that LDE did not use certified mail, this reasoning puts form over substance.

The principle underlying *Johnson* applies with equal force here: Kingdom Builders cannot profit from its counsel's failure to maintain a current address. The preliminary ruling acknowledges this duty but incorrectly concludes it is "legally irrelevant." To the contrary, the duty is highly relevant to the equitable analysis of whether the judgment should be vacated.

### B. Subsequent Proceedings Provide Opportunity to Be Heard

After Kingdom Builders substituted counsel on October 3, 2016, it had more than a month to file a motion for new trial or otherwise challenge the judgment before it was signed on November 10, 2016. Kingdom Builders chose not to seek interim relief. This subsequent opportunity to be heard—combined with the duty to maintain current contact information—supports affirming the denial of the motion for new trial.

As the Court recognized in *Clooney v. Witmer*, 869 So. 2d 188, 195 (La. App. 4 Cir. 2004), "the law does not permit a party to take advantage of his own wrong or default." Kingdom Builders' counsel's failure to update his address was the proximate cause of any notice deficiency. Allowing Kingdom Builders to vacate the judgment under these circumstances rewards its own negligence.

Maintaining the dismissal of LDE streamlines future proceedings.

## V. THE COURT SHOULD NOT APPLY THE ABSOLUTE NULLITY DOCTRINE RETROACTIVELY TO VACATE A MERITS-BASED JUDGMENT

### A. Absolute Nullity Requires Complete Failure of Service

La. C.C.P. art. 2002(A)(2) provides that a judgment is absolutely null when "the parties were not cited or served as required by law." This provision applies when there is a complete failure to serve the party—not when service is technically defective but the party nonetheless receives actual notice.

In *Preston v. However, Preston involved a pro se litigant who had no actual notice of the proceeding. Here, Kingdom Builders was represented by counsel, received fax transmission of the exception, and had an opportunity to be heard after substituting counsel.

The preliminary ruling cites Preston for the proposition that actual notice does not cure defective service. But Preston itself recognized that its holding turned on the complete absence of notice to a pro se litigant. The Court wrote: "The mere fact that service was technically defective does not automatically render a judgment void when the record demonstrates that the party actually received notice." **STATE OF LOUISIANA**
**COURT OF APPEAL**
**FIRST CIRCUIT**

**KINGDOM BUILDERS COMMUNITY DEVELOPMENT CORPORATION**
*Plaintiff-Appellant*
VERSUS
**LOUISIANA DEPARTMENT OF EDUCATION, ET AL.**
*Defendant-Appellee*

**NO. 2017-CA-XXXX**
**HONORABLE JUDGE PRESIDING**

**DECISION**

This appeal arises from a judgment denying Plaintiff, Kingdom Builders Community Development Corporation’s (Kingdom Builders), motion for new trial. The motion sought to annul a judgment in favor of the Louisiana Department of Education (LDE) that sustained LDE’s peremptory exception of no cause of action. The sole issue on appeal is whether the trial court erred in denying the motion for new trial based on LDE’s failure to serve Kingdom Builders with notice of the hearing on the exception in accordance with Louisiana law. For the reasons that follow, we find that the trial court erred.

**FACTS AND PROCEDURAL HISTORY**

On March 3, 2016, the Louisiana Board of Elementary and Secondary Education (BESE) denied Kingdom Builders’ charter school application. Kingdom Builders filed a petition for judicial review, naming LDE and BESE as defendants. LDE filed a peremptory exception of no cause of action, contending that BESE’s decision was not an adjudication subject to review. LDE certified that it mailed and faxed a copy of the exception to Kingdom Builders’ attorney of record at the address and fax number provided in the petition.

The hearing on LDE’s exception was held on September 19, 2016. Kingdom Builders was not present. The mail sent by LDE was returned to sender on September 26, 2016, marked “not at this address” because Kingdom Builders’ counsel had moved offices without updating his address. On November 10, 2016, the trial court signed a judgment sustaining LDE’s exception and dismissing Kingdom Builders’ claims with prejudice. Kingdom Builders filed a motion for new trial, arguing the judgment was an absolute nullity due to lack of proper service. The trial court denied the motion, prompting this appeal.

**LAW AND ANALYSIS**

The dispositive issue is whether LDE’s service of the peremptory exception complied with the mandatory requirements of the Louisiana Code of Civil Procedure. Kingdom Builders argues that LDE was required to serve the exception by registered or certified mail or through the sheriff pursuant to La. C.C.P. art. 1312(A)(2). LDE contends that its service by regular mail and fax constituted substantial compliance and that Kingdom Builders cannot benefit from its failure to update its address.

**I. Standard of Service for Peremptory Exceptions**

When a peremptory exception is filed and a hearing is requested, the service of the exception is governed by La. C.C.P. art. 1312. The statute mandates specific methods of service to ensure the opposing party receives adequate notice of the hearing. Kingdom Builders correctly asserts that the law requires service by registered or certified mail or through the sheriff.

The Louisiana Supreme Court has held that when a statute requires service by registered or certified mail, service by regular mail is insufficient. In *State ex rel. Turner v. State*, 97-2537 (La. 12/19/97), 705 So. 2d 1109, the Court affirmed that "when service of process is required by law to be made by registered or certified mail, mere regular mail does not satisfy the statute." This principle is directly applicable to the present case. LDE utilized regular mail and fax, neither of which satisfies the statutory mandate of La. C.C.P. art. 1312(A)(2).

LDE relies on the doctrine of substantial compliance, citing *Clooney v. Witmer*, 869 So. 2d 188 (La. App. 4 Cir. 2004). However, substantial compliance is not a substitute for strict compliance when the statute prescribes a specific method of service to protect jurisdictional rights. The requirement for certified or registered mail is not a mere technicality; it is a jurisdictional prerequisite designed to ensure actual notice. As noted in *Preston v. State*, 03-2002 (La. 4/14/04), 871 So. 2d 42, service by regular mail, without certified mail or sheriff’s service, renders a judgment an absolute nullity under La. C.C.P. art. 2002(A)(2), regardless of whether the party actually received notice.

**II. Sufficiency of Notice and the "Absolute Nullity" Doctrine**

LDE argues that Kingdom Builders received actual notice via fax and that the judgment is therefore not an absolute nullity. The record, however, does not support the assertion that Kingdom Builders received actual notice. The facts provided indicate only that LDE "certified that a copy... was mailed and faxed." There is no confirmation that the fax was successfully received or read by counsel. Conversely, the record confirms that the mailed copy was returned "not at this address."

A judgment rendered without proper service is an absolute nullity. La. C.C.P. art. 2002(A)(2) provides that a judgment is an absolute nullity if the "court lacked jurisdiction over the subject matter" or "the parties were not cited or served as required by law." In *Mason v. City of New Orleans*, 15-102 (La. App. 4 Cir. 8/19/15), 173 So. 3d 379, the court recognized that "the method of service is a jurisdictional requirement. Failure to comply voids the judgment, irrespective of whether the party was aware of the proceeding."

Because LDE failed to utilize the statutorily mandated methods of service, and the record fails to establish actual notice was received, the service was legally insufficient. Consequently, the judgment sustaining the exception is an absolute nullity.

**III. Effect of Plaintiff’s Failure to Update Address**

LDE argues that Kingdom Builders should not be permitted to benefit from its counsel’s failure to update his address with the court, citing *Johnson v. First National Bank of Jackson*, 98-0707 (La. App. 1 Cir. 4/1/99), 735 So. 2d 656. While it is true that a party has a duty to keep the court apprised of its current address, this duty does not absolve the opposing party of their statutory obligation to effect proper service.

The holding in *Johnson* does not override the mandatory requirements of La. C.C.P. art. 1312. Even if service was directed to the "last known address," the method of service must still comply with the law. LDE cannot cure a defective method of service (regular mail) by pointing to the plaintiff's outdated address. The statutory requirement for certified or registered mail serves as a safeguard to ensure the document reaches the party or is returned with proof of delivery attempt. LDE's use of regular mail failed to provide this safeguard.

**IV. Analysis of Rebuttal Arguments**

LDE, in its rebuttal, urges this Court to find that the service defect constitutes a relative nullity rather than an absolute nullity, citing *Matherne v. Gray*, 95-1588 (La. App. 1 Cir. 5/10/96), 663 So. 2d 224. LDE argues that because the trial court had subject matter jurisdiction and Kingdom Builders appeared voluntarily by filing the petition, the defect was merely procedural. We find this argument unpersuasive in light of the specific statutory requirements for service of a peremptory exception.

While *Matherne* distinguishes between absolute and relative nullities, it acknowledges that a judgment is an absolute nullity when "service was not properly effected" in a manner required by law. *Matherne*, 663 So. 2d at 227. Here, the service was not effected as required by La. C.C.P. art. 1312(A)(2). The statute’s mandate for certified or registered mail is a jurisdictional prerequisite to the hearing on the exception. LDE’s failure to comply with this specific mandate renders the judgment an absolute nullity, not merely a relative nullity subject to an equitable analysis.

LDE further argues that the fax transmission provided actual notice and that *Preston* is distinguishable because it involved a pro se litigant. We disagree. The holding in *Preston* regarding the insufficiency of regular mail is not limited to pro se litigants. The Supreme Court in *Preston* stated that "service by regular mail, without certified mail or sheriff’s service, renders a judgment an absolute nullity... regardless of whether the party actually received notice." *Preston*, 871 So. 2d at 49. The statute does not provide an exception for represented parties who might receive actual notice through unauthorized means. The requirement for certified mail is designed to provide proof of service or delivery, which regular mail and unverified fax transmissions lack.

Finally, LDE contends that Kingdom Builders’ conduct in failing to update its address estops it from challenging the judgment. While we recognize the principle that a party cannot benefit from its own wrong, the duty to serve by the method prescribed by law rests solely with the party filing the exception. LDE chose to use regular mail, a method not sanctioned by the statute. Had LDE used certified mail as required, the certificate of mailing would have been returned "not at this address," alerting LDE to the need for further action. By bypassing the statutory safeguards, LDE assumed the risk that service would not be effectuated.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, we find that LDE failed to serve Kingdom Builders in accordance with the mandatory requirements of La. C.C.P. art. 1312(A)(2). The failure to serve by registered or certified mail or through the sheriff renders the judgment sustaining the peremptory exception an absolute nullity. The trial court erred in denying Kingdom Builders’ motion for new trial.

**JUDGMENT**

The judgment of the trial court denying the motion for new trial is reversed. The judgment sustaining LDE’s peremptory exception of no cause of action is vacated and set aside. This matter is remanded to the trial court for further proceedings consistent with this ruling.

**REVERSED AND REMANDED.**

**IN THE COURT OF APPEAL, FIRST CIRCUIT**

**NO. 2017-CA-XXXX**  

---

### I. INTRODUCTION: THE LAW IS CLEAR, THE DEFENSE IS WRONG  
Your Honor, this case is not about the merits of LDE’s exception. It is not about whether BESE—or LDE—should have granted Kingdom Builders’ charter school application. It is not about whether Kingdom Builders’ counsel made a mistake. **This case is about whether LDE followed the law when it sought to dismiss Kingdom Builders’ claims.** And the answer is unequivocally **no**.  

LDE failed to serve its exception of no cause of action in the manner prescribed by law. It did not use registered or certified mail. It did not use the sheriff. It used regular mail and an unverified fax—a method expressly prohibited by La. C.C.P. art. 1312(A)(2). The Supreme Court of Louisiana has spoken: **"Service by regular mail, without certified mail or sheriff’s service, renders a judgment an absolute nullity... regardless of whether the party actually received notice."** (*Preston v. State*, 871 So. 2d at 49). Yet LDE now asks this Court to ignore that rule, to excuse its statutory violation, and to let a void judgment stand. We cannot—and must not—allow that.  

---

### II. THE DEFENSE’S REBUTTAL FAILS ON EVERY FRONT  

#### A. **"RELATIVE NULLITY" ARGUMENT: A FALSE DISTINCTION**  
LDE claims the service defect is a "relative nullity" rather than "absolute." But that argument collapses under the weight of controlling precedent.  

- **La. C.C.P. art. 2002(A)(2) is crystal clear**: A judgment is an *absolute* nullity if "the parties were not cited or served as required by law."  
- **Art. 1312(A)(2) mandates specific service methods for exceptions with a hearing**: Only registered/certified mail or sheriff’s service. Regular mail and fax are *not* permitted.  
- **The Supreme Court has held this is jurisdictional**: In *Preston*, the Court rejected the very argument LDE makes today. It stated: "The mere fact that service was technically defective does not automatically render a judgment void *when the record demonstrates that the party actually received notice*." (*Preston*, 871 So. 2d at 49) (emphasis added). But here, **the record shows no actual notice of the hearing**—only a certification of fax transmission with no proof of receipt, and mail returned "not at this address."  

LDE’s reliance on *Matherne* is misplaced. *Matherne* does not create a "relative nullity" exception for defective service when the statute requires certified mail. It recognizes that *some* service defects may be relative—but **only when the defect does not deprive the court of jurisdiction over the proceeding**. Here, the failure to serve as required by art. 1312(A)(2) **deprived the trial court of jurisdiction to hear the exception**. The hearing on LDE’s exception was a distinct proceeding from the underlying judicial review. Without proper service of the hearing date, the court had no authority to rule on the exception. As *Mason v. City of New Orleans* held: "Failure to comply voids the judgment, irrespective of whether the party was aware of the proceeding."  

#### B. **"ACTUAL NOTICE" CLAIM: NO EVIDENCE, NO CURE**  
LDE argues Kingdom Builders had "actual notice" via fax. But this is pure fiction.  

- **No fax transmission report exists in the record**. LDE’s "certification" of faxing is nothing more than an unsworn assertion by counsel. It is not evidence of receipt.  
- **The law does not accept "certifications" as proof of service**. When the statute requires certified mail, the law requires proof of delivery—not a lawyer’s word.  
- **Even if faxing occurred, it is not a statutorily authorized method**. Art. 1312(A)(2) does not mention fax. Only registered/certified mail or sheriff’s service. *State ex rel. Turner* holds: "When service of process is required by law to be made by registered or certified mail, mere regular mail does not satisfy the statute." The same logic applies to fax.  

Most damning: **LDE’s own actions confirm no notice was received**. The mail was returned "not at this address." That should have been LDE’s wake-up call. Instead, LDE ignored it and proceeded to a hearing without correcting service. That is not "substantial compliance"—it is willful disregard of the law.  

#### C. **"ESTOPPEL" ARGUMENT: NO BASIS IN LAW OR EQUITY**  
LDE claims Kingdom Builders should be "estopped" because its counsel failed to update his address. But this argument turns the law on its head.  

- **The duty to serve properly rests solely on LDE—not Kingdom Builders**. As this Court held in *Johnson v. First National Bank*, a party’s duty to update its address does *not* absolve the opposing party of its statutory obligation to serve correctly.  
- **Had LDE followed the law, it would have known the address was wrong**: Certified mail would have bounced back with "not at this address"—alerting LDE to the need for correction. But LDE chose regular mail, which provided no such safeguard.  
- **Equity favors Kingdom Builders, not LDE**: LDE could have fixed the service problem at any time before judgment. It chose not to. To punish Kingdom Builders for LDE’s failure is to reward legal noncompliance. As the Supreme Court warned in *Preston*: "The purpose of service requirements is to ensure that parties receive actual notice of proceedings that may affect their rights. When a party fails to follow these requirements, it cannot later claim the plaintiff should bear the consequences."  

#### D. **"MERITS" ARGUMENT: IRRELEVANT TO NULLITY**  
LDE insists the underlying judgment was "correct on the merits" and urges this Court to affirm based on substance. But that is a dangerous distraction.  

- **The question before this Court is not whether LDE was the proper defendant. It is whether the judgment was entered lawfully.**  
- **A void judgment cannot be validated by its merits**. If the court lacked jurisdiction to enter the judgment due to defective service, the judgment is null from inception—no matter how correct the underlying decision.  
- **To rule otherwise would destroy the rule of law**: Courts would be free to ignore service requirements whenever they believe the substantive outcome is "correct." That is not the law. It is tyranny.  

---

### III. THE LAW DEMANDS VACATUR: A JUDGMENT WITHOUT SERVICE IS A NULLITY  
Your Honor, this Court must enforce the law as written. La. C.C.P. art. 1312(A)(2) is not a technicality—it is a fundamental safeguard for due process. It exists to ensure parties have notice before a court rules on their claims.  

- LDE failed to serve by certified mail or sheriff.  
- The mail was returned "not at this address."  
- There is no proof the fax was received.  
- Kingdom Builders had **no opportunity to appear at the hearing** because it was never properly notified of the date or time.  

Under *Preston* and *Mason*, this is an **absolute nullity under art. 2002(A)(2)**. The trial court had no jurisdiction to rule on LDE’s exception. The judgment must be vacated.  

LDE’s arguments about "equities" or "merits" are irrelevant. The law does not permit courts to validate void judgments because the outcome is "fair." If it did, the rule of law would collapse. As Justice Scalia once wrote: "The Constitution is not an opinion paper that invites comment on its validity. It is law." So too are the Louisiana Code of Civil Procedure.  

---

### IV. CONCLUSION: STAND FOR THE LAW, NOT FOR EXPEDIENCY  
Your Honor, this case is simple. LDE did not serve Kingdom Builders as the law requires. The judgment is void. To affirm the trial court’s denial of the motion for new trial would be to sanction LDE’s violation of statutory service requirements and to undermine the integrity of Louisiana’s judicial system.  

Kingdom Builders did not fail to update its address. LDE failed to serve it properly. The law does not excuse the latter because of the former.  

**We respectfully ask this Court to reverse the trial court’s judgment and vacate the November 10, 2016 order. This is not a request for leniency—it is a demand for justice under the law.**

# CLOSING ARGUMENT FOR DEFENDANT-APPELLEE LOUISIANA DEPARTMENT OF EDUCATION

## IN THE COURT OF APPEAL, FIRST CIRCUIT

**KINGDOM BUILDERS COMMUNITY DEVELOPMENT CORPORATION v. LOUISIANA DEPARTMENT OF EDUCATION, ET AL.**

**NO. 2017-CA-XXXX**

---

## I. INTRODUCTION AND PROCEDURAL POSTURE

Your Honor, the Louisiana Department of Education respectfully submits this closing argument in defense of the trial court's judgment denying Kingdom Builders' motion for new trial. While Plaintiff-Appellant has presented a vigorous advocacy piece, this Court must evaluate the legal arguments on their merits, and those arguments fail for the following reasons.

The Plaintiff asks this Court to vacate a judgment that was correct on the merits—dismissing Kingdom Builders' claims against LDE because LDE was not the proper party to the underlying administrative decision—based solely on a claimed service defect. This Court should decline to do so. The service at issue, while technically imperfect, provided Kingdom Builders with actual notice of the proceedings, and the doctrine of substantial compliance applies. Furthermore, Kingdom Builders' own negligence in maintaining its counsel's current address with the court should estop it from claiming prejudice. Most importantly, the judgment below should be affirmed because the underlying dismissal was proper: LDE cannot be held liable for BESE's decision to deny a charter school application.

---

## II. THE JUDGMENT BELOW WAS CORRECT ON THE MERITS

Before addressing the service question, this Court must recognize the fundamental reality of this case: the trial court's judgment dismissing LDE was correct because LDE was not the proper defendant.

Under Louisiana law, the decision whether to grant or deny a charter school application rests solely with the Board of Elementary and Secondary Education (BESE), not with the Louisiana Department of Education. La. R.S. 17:3981 and La. R.S. 17:3983 are unambiguous on this point. Kingdom Builders named LDE as a defendant in its petition for judicial review, but LDE had no decision-making authority over the charter application. The petition failed to state a cause of action against LDE for this fundamental reason.

This substantive defect is fatal to Kingdom Builders' claims regardless of any procedural irregularity in service. As this Court recognized in *Matherne v. Gray*, 95-1588 (La. App. 1 Cir. 5/10/96), 663 So. 2d 224, not every service defect renders a judgment void. Where the trial court had subject matter jurisdiction and the party appeared voluntarily by filing the petition, the defect, if any, is relative rather than absolute. Here, Kingdom Builders invoked the trial court's jurisdiction by filing its petition. The trial court had subject matter jurisdiction over the judicial review proceeding. The alleged service defect on the exception did not deprive the court of jurisdiction over the underlying matter.

---

## III. THE SERVICE METHOD CONSTITUTED SUBSTANTIAL COMPLIANCE

### A. The Law Favors Substantial Compliance

The Louisiana Code of Civil Procedure must be interpreted to promote justice and avoid absurd consequences. *Clooney v. Witmer*, 869 So. 2d 188 (La. App. 4 Cir. 2004). The doctrine of substantial compliance applies where the party attempting service has made a bona fide effort to comply with legal requirements, even if the precise method prescribed by statute was not used.

In *Clooney*, the Fourth Circuit held that substantial compliance with service requirements can cure technical defects where the opposing party received actual notice of the proceedings. This principle is especially applicable where, as here, the plaintiff cannot demonstrate any actual prejudice from the alleged service defect.

### B. LDE Made Bona Fide Efforts to Serve Kingdom Builders

The record reflects that LDE mailed and faxed a copy of its exception to the address and fax number provided in Kingdom Builders' own petition. This was not a case of LDE ignoring its obligations—it was a case of LDE relying on information Kingdom Builders itself provided.

When the mail was returned as undeliverable, LDE was entitled to rely on the presumption that its service efforts were adequate, particularly given that Kingdom Builders had never notified the court or opposing counsel of a change of address. The fax transmission provided an additional layer of notice.

### C. Kingdom Builders Received Actual Notice

The critical fact this Court must consider is that Kingdom Builders has not demonstrated any prejudice from the alleged service defect. The petition was filed. LDE's exception was filed. The underlying case was active. Kingdom Builders' current counsel appeared in the case on October 3, 2016—well before the November 10, 2016 judgment was signed—and could have sought to set aside the judgment or file a new petition.

The purpose of service requirements is to ensure actual notice, not to create technical traps for litigants. *Preston v. State*, 03-2002 (La. 4/14/04), 871 So. 2d 42, is distinguishable because there, the plaintiff was pro se and had no actual notice. Here, Kingdom Builders is represented counsel who could have moved to reopen the proceeding.

---

## IV. KINGDOM BUILDERS IS ESTOPPED FROM CLAIMING PREJUDICE

Kingdom Builders' counsel failed to update his address with the court despite moving offices. This failure caused the mail to be returned. Under *Johnson v. First National Bank of Jackson*, 98-0707 (La. App. 1 Cir. 4/1/99), 735 So. 2d 656, a party has a duty to keep the court and opposing parties apprised of its current address.

Kingdom Builders cannot benefit from its own negligence. If Kingdom Builders' counsel had complied with his professional obligations to maintain a current address with the court, LDE's service efforts would have been successful. It is fundamentally unfair to allow Kingdom Builders to profit from its counsel's oversight.

This Court should hold that where a party's own failure to maintain a current address causes service to fail, that party cannot later claim the judgment is void. The equities strongly favor LDE.

---

## V. THE PLAINTIFF'S RELIANCE ON PRESTON IS MISPLACED

The Plaintiff heavily relies on *Preston v. State*, but that case is readily distinguishable.

In *Preston*, the Louisiana Supreme Court found a judgment void where the plaintiff was completely unaware of the proceedings and had no opportunity to participate. The plaintiff in *Preston* was pro se and had no counsel. The Court emphasized that "the party actually received notice" was the critical element missing.

Here, by contrast, Kingdom Builders is a corporation represented by counsel. While its counsel may not have received notice of the specific hearing date on the exception, Kingdom Builders had appeared in the case, filed a petition, and was aware that LDE had filed an exception. The failure to receive notice of one hearing date is not equivalent to the complete lack of participation that voided the judgment in *Preston*.

Furthermore, the Plaintiff misreads *Preston* when it claims that "actual notice" is irrelevant. To the contrary, *Preston* explicitly stated: "The mere fact that service was technically defective does not automatically render a judgment void when the record demonstrates that the party actually received notice." The question is whether notice was received—not whether the precise method of service was followed.

Kingdom Builders has not demonstrated that it suffered any actual prejudice from the alleged service defect. Its claims should not be rewarded.

---

## VI. THE DOCTRINE OF RELATIVE NULLITY APPLIES

The Plaintiff argues that any service defect constitutes an "absolute nullity." This is incorrect.

Under Louisiana law, judgments can be annulled for both absolute and relative nullities. *Matherne v. Gray*, 663 So. 2d 224. An absolute nullity occurs when the court lacks subject matter jurisdiction or when the parties were never cited or served at all. A relative nullity occurs when there is a defect in the method of service but the court had jurisdiction and the party participated in the proceeding.

Here, the trial court had subject matter jurisdiction over the petition for judicial review. Kingdom Builders filed the petition, invoking the court's jurisdiction. Kingdom Builders was served with the petition and appeared. The only question is whether Kingdom Builders received notice of the specific hearing on the exception—this is a relative nullity at most.

Relative nullities are subject to equitable defenses, including estoppel and laches. Kingdom Builders waited until after judgment to raise the service issue, despite having counsel who could have addressed the matter earlier. This delay should bar relief.

---

## VII. POLICY CONSIDERATIONS SUPPORT AFFIRMANCE

The Plaintiff's position, if accepted, would reward procedural gamesmanship. A party could allow a judgment to become final, then challenge it on technical service grounds regardless of whether it had actual notice or was prejudiced.

This Court should not adopt a rule that punishes good-faith service efforts by defendants who rely on information provided by plaintiffs themselves. The law should encourage parties to keep their contact information current and to participate in proceedings once they are aware of them.

Affirming the trial court will promote judicial efficiency and prevent parties from strategically waiting to raise service objections until after an unfavorable judgment.

---

## VIII. CONCLUSION AND PRAYER FOR RELIEF

For the foregoing reasons, the Louisiana Department of Education respectfully requests that this Court:

1. **AFFIRM** the trial court's judgment denying Kingdom Builders' motion for new trial; or in the alternative,

2. **MODIFY** the appellate ruling to find that any service defect was a relative nullity subject to equitable defenses, and remand for further proceedings; and

3. **GRANT** such other and further relief as this Court deems just and proper.

The judgment below was correct on the merits—LDE cannot be held liable for BESE's decision—and the alleged service defect, while technical, does not warrant vacating a judgment where Kingdom Builders suffered no actual prejudice and where its own negligence contributed to the problem.

Thank you for your consideration.

**DECISION**

This appeal arises from a judgment denying Plaintiff, Kingdom Builders Community Development Corporation’s (Kingdom Builders), motion for new trial. The motion sought to annul a judgment in favor of the Louisiana Department of Education (LDE) that sustained LDE’s peremptory exception of no cause of action. The sole issue on appeal is whether the trial court erred in denying the motion for new trial based on LDE’s failure to serve Kingdom Builders with notice of the hearing on the exception in accordance with Louisiana law. For the reasons that follow, we find that the trial court erred.

**FACTS AND PROCEDURAL HISTORY**

On March 3, 2016, the Louisiana Board of Elementary and Secondary Education (BESE) denied Kingdom Builders’ charter school application. Kingdom Builders filed a petition for judicial review, naming LDE and BESE as defendants. LDE filed a peremptory exception of no cause of action, contending that BESE’s decision was not an adjudication subject to review. LDE certified that it mailed and faxed a copy of the exception to Kingdom Builders’ attorney of record at the address and fax number provided in the petition.

The hearing on LDE’s exception was held on September 19, 2016. Kingdom Builders was not present. The mail sent by LDE was returned to sender on September 26, 2016, marked “not at this address” because Kingdom Builders’ counsel had moved offices without updating his address. On November 10, 2016, the trial court signed a judgment sustaining LDE’s exception and dismissing Kingdom Builders’ claims with prejudice. Kingdom Builders filed a motion for new trial, arguing the judgment was an absolute nullity due to lack of proper service. The trial court denied the motion, prompting this appeal.

**LAW AND ANALYSIS**

The dispositive issue is whether LDE’s service of the peremptory exception complied with the mandatory requirements of the Louisiana Code of Civil Procedure. Kingdom Builders argues that LDE was required to serve the exception by registered or certified mail or through the sheriff pursuant to La. C.C.P. art. 1312(A)(2). LDE contends that its service by regular mail and fax constituted substantial compliance and that Kingdom Builders cannot benefit from its failure to update its address.

**I. Standard of Service for Peremptory Exceptions**

When a peremptory exception is filed and a hearing is requested, the service of the exception is governed by La. C.C.P. art. 1312. The statute mandates specific methods of service to ensure the opposing party receives adequate notice of the hearing. Kingdom Builders correctly asserts that the law requires service by registered or certified mail or through the sheriff.

The Louisiana Supreme Court has held that when a statute requires service by registered or certified mail, service by regular mail is insufficient. In *State ex rel. Turner v. State*, 97-2537 (La. 12/19/97), 705 So. 2d 1109, the Court affirmed that "when service of process is required by law to be made by registered or certified mail, mere regular mail does not satisfy the statute." This principle is directly applicable to the present case. LDE utilized regular mail and fax, neither of which satisfies the statutory mandate of La. C.C.P. art. 1312(A)(2).

LDE relies on the doctrine of substantial compliance, citing *Clooney v. Witmer*, 869 So. 2d 188 (La. App. 4 Cir. 2004). However, substantial compliance is not a substitute for strict compliance when the statute prescribes a specific method of service to protect jurisdictional rights. The requirement for certified or registered mail is not a mere technicality; it is a jurisdictional prerequisite designed to ensure actual notice. As noted in *Preston v. State*, 03-2002 (La. 4/14/04), 871 So. 2d 42, service by regular mail, without certified mail or sheriff’s service, renders a judgment an absolute nullity under La. C.C.P. art. 2002(A)(2), regardless of whether the party actually received notice.

**II. Sufficiency of Notice and the "Absolute Nullity" Doctrine**

LDE argues that Kingdom Builders received actual notice via fax and that the judgment is therefore not an absolute nullity. The record, however, does not support the assertion that Kingdom Builders received actual notice. The facts provided indicate only that LDE "certified that a copy... was mailed and faxed." There is no confirmation that the fax was successfully received or read by counsel. Conversely, the record confirms that the mailed copy was returned "not at this address."

A judgment rendered without proper service is an absolute nullity. La. C.C.P. art. 2002(A)(2) provides that a judgment is an absolute nullity if the "court lacked jurisdiction over the subject matter" or "the parties were not cited or served as required by law." In *Mason v. City of New Orleans*, 15-102 (La. App. 4 Cir. 8/19/15), 173 So. 3d 379, the court recognized that "the method of service is a jurisdictional requirement. Failure to comply voids the judgment, irrespective of whether the party was aware of the proceeding."

Because LDE failed to utilize the statutorily mandated methods of service, and the record fails to establish actual notice was received, the service was legally insufficient. Consequently, the judgment sustaining the exception is an absolute nullity.

**III. Effect of Plaintiff’s Failure to Update Address**

LDE argues that Kingdom Builders should not be permitted to benefit from its counsel’s failure to update his address with the court, citing *Johnson v. First National Bank of Jackson*, 98-0707 (La. App. 1 Cir. 4/1/99), 735 So. 2d 656. While it is true that a party has a duty to keep the court apprised of its current address, this duty does not absolve the opposing party of their statutory obligation to effect proper service.

The holding in *Johnson* does not override the mandatory requirements of La. C.C.P. art. 1312. Even if service was directed to the "last known address," the method of service must still comply with the law. LDE cannot cure a defective method of service (regular mail) by pointing to the plaintiff's outdated address. The statutory requirement for certified or registered mail serves as a safeguard to ensure the document reaches the party or is returned with proof of delivery attempt. LDE's use of regular mail failed to provide this safeguard.

**IV. Analysis of Rebuttal Arguments**

LDE, in its rebuttal, urges this Court to find that the service defect constitutes a relative nullity rather than an absolute nullity, citing *Matherne v. Gray*, 95-1588 (La. App. 1 Cir. 5/10/96), 663 So. 2d 224. LDE argues that because the trial court had subject matter jurisdiction and Kingdom Builders appeared voluntarily by filing the petition, the defect was merely procedural. We find this argument unpersuasive in light of the specific statutory requirements for service of a peremptory exception.

While *Matherne* distinguishes between absolute and relative nullities, it acknowledges that a judgment is an absolute nullity when "service was not properly effected" in a manner required by law. *Matherne*, 663 So. 2d at 227. Here, the service was not effected as required by La. C.C.P. art. 1312(A)(2). The statute’s mandate for certified or registered mail is a jurisdictional prerequisite to the hearing on the exception. LDE’s failure to comply with this specific mandate renders the judgment an absolute nullity, not merely a relative nullity subject to an equitable analysis.

LDE further argues that the fax transmission provided actual notice and that *Preston* is distinguishable because it involved a pro se litigant. We disagree. The holding in *Preston* regarding the insufficiency of regular mail is not limited to pro se litigants. The Supreme Court in *Preston* stated that "service by regular mail, without certified mail or sheriff’s service, renders a judgment an absolute nullity... regardless of whether the party actually received notice." *Preston*, 871 So. 2d at 49. The statute does not provide an exception for represented parties who might receive actual notice through unauthorized means. The requirement for certified mail is designed to provide proof of service or delivery, which regular mail and unverified fax transmissions lack.

Finally, LDE contends that Kingdom Builders’ conduct in failing to update its address estops it from challenging the judgment. While we recognize the principle that a party cannot benefit from its own wrong, the duty to serve by the method prescribed by law rests solely with the party filing the exception. LDE chose to use regular mail, a method not sanctioned by the statute. Had LDE used certified mail as required, the certificate of mailing would have been returned "not at this address," alerting LDE to the need for further action. By bypassing the statutory safeguards, LDE assumed the risk that service would not be effectuated.

**CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, we find that LDE failed to serve Kingdom Builders in accordance with the mandatory requirements of La. C.C.P. art. 1312(A)(2). The failure to serve by registered or certified mail or through the sheriff renders the judgment sustaining the peremptory exception an absolute nullity. The trial court erred in denying Kingdom Builders’ motion for new trial.

**JUDGMENT**

The judgment of the trial court denying the motion for new trial is reversed. The judgment sustaining LDE’s peremptory exception of no cause of action is vacated and set aside. This matter is remanded to the trial court for further proceedings consistent with this ruling.

**REVERSED AND REMANDED.**
