**PRELIMINARY RULING**

**I. INTRODUCTION**

This matter comes before the court on appeal from the judgment of the trial court following a jury verdict in favor of the plaintiff, Rita A. Wallace. The defendants, Hector L. Haddock and Subaru Leasing Corporation, appeal from the trial court's judgment ordering an additur of $15,000. The defendants contend that the trial court abused its discretion by (1) concluding that the jury's award of $1715 in noneconomic damages was insufficient as a matter of law, and (2) improperly failing to offer the parties the alternative of a new trial if the additur was not accepted, in contravention of General Statutes §§ 52-216a and 52-228b. After reviewing the arguments presented by both parties, the court finds that the trial court committed reversible error by rendering judgment immediately upon granting the additur without providing the defendants the statutory right to reject the additur and proceed to a new trial.

**II. PROCEDURAL HISTORY**

The jury returned a verdict on January 7, 2002, awarding the plaintiff $3285 in economic damages and $1715 in noneconomic damages. On January 17, 2002, the plaintiff filed a motion for an additur or, in the alternative, a motion for a new trial on the issue of damages. By memorandum of decision filed July 12, 2002, the court granted the motion for an additur in the amount of $15,000. The court then immediately rendered judgment for the plaintiff in the amount of $16,715. It is undisputed that the court did not offer the defendants an opportunity to reject the additur and elect a new trial prior to entering the judgment.

**III. DISCUSSION**

The dispositive issue in this appeal is whether the trial court's failure to provide the defendants with an election between accepting the additur or proceeding to a new trial requires reversal of the judgment.

Connecticut General Statutes § 52-216a governs the procedure for additur and remittitur. The statute provides explicitly that "the court shall not render judgment until the party has been given an opportunity to accept or reject the additur or remittitur." This statutory command is mandatory and leaves no room for judicial discretion. The legislature's intent in enacting this provision was to preserve the parties' fundamental right to a jury trial by ensuring that a party is not forced to accept a judgment modified by the court without the option of having the issue relitigated.

The defendants argue, and the court agrees, that the trial court's actions constitute a clear violation of this statute. The court granted the motion for additur on July 12, 2002, and "immediately rendered judgment" for the plaintiff. By doing so, the court bypassed the election process required by § 52-216a and § 52-228b.

This violation is not subject to harmless error analysis. In *Harty v. LeFevre*, 234 Conn. 564, 572 (1995), the Connecticut Supreme Court unequivocally held that "the trial court's failure to provide the parties with the option of accepting the additur or proceeding to a new trial constitutes reversible error." The Court further clarified that the statutory mandate reflects the legislature's determination that parties must retain the fundamental right to choose between accepting a court-modified verdict or proceeding to a new trial. This principle was reaffirmed in *Smith v. City of Hartford*, 262 Conn. 535, 545 (2003), where the Court stated: "The statutory procedure is mandatory, and the trial court has no discretion to bypass it."

The plaintiff has not offered any argument to overcome this mandatory statutory requirement. While the defendants have also raised substantive arguments regarding the adequacy of the evidence supporting the additur, the court need not reach those merits. The procedural error alone is sufficient to warrant reversal. As established in *Harty v. LeFevre*, 234 Conn. 564, 572 (1995), the failure to provide the election is reversible error regardless of the substantive reasonableness of the additur amount. The trial court's error deprived the defendants of a valuable procedural right—the opportunity to assess the risks of a new trial against the certainty of the increased judgment.

**IV. CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the court concludes that the trial court abused its discretion and violated Connecticut General Statutes §§ 52-216a and 52-228b by rendering judgment without providing the defendants an opportunity to accept or reject the additur. The judgment of the trial court is reversed. The matter is remanded to the trial court with direction to provide the parties with the opportunity to accept or reject the additur pursuant to the applicable statutes.

**FINAL RULING**

**I. INTRODUCTION**

This matter is before the court on appeal from the judgment of the trial court following a jury verdict in favor of the plaintiff, Rita A. Wallace. The defendants, Hector L. Haddock and Subaru Leasing Corporation, appeal from the trial court's judgment ordering an additur of $15,000. The defendants claim the court abused its discretion by concluding the jury's award was insufficient and by failing to offer the parties the alternative of a new trial. The court has reviewed the closing arguments of both the prosecution and the defense. For the reasons that follow, the judgment of the trial court is reversed, and the matter is remanded for a new trial on the issue of damages.

**II. PROCEDURAL ERROR**

The court first addresses the procedural error, which the prosecution concedes. The trial court rendered judgment immediately after ordering the additur, without providing the defendants an opportunity to accept or reject the increased award. This action was in direct contravention of General Statutes § 52-216a. As established in *Harty v. LeFevre*, 234 Conn. 564, 572 (1995), the trial court's failure to provide the parties with the option of accepting the additur or proceeding to a new trial constitutes reversible error. The statutory mandate is clear: the court "shall not render judgment" until the party has been given this opportunity. The trial court's failure to do so requires reversal.

**III. SUBSTANTIVE VALIDITY OF THE ADDITUR**

The primary dispute on appeal concerns whether the trial court's substantive determination—the $15,000 additur—should be preserved on remand. The prosecution argues that the trial court's review of the plaintiff's medical records and testimony regarding her 35 percent permanent disability satisfies the legal requirements for an additur. The court disagrees.

Connecticut law requires that a trial court provide a reasoned explanation for an additur to permit meaningful appellate review. In *Gormley v. D'Elia*, 249 Conn. 392, 404 (1999), the Supreme Court held that a trial court must articulate the basis for an additur order. The purpose of this requirement is to ensure that the court's modification of the jury's verdict is not arbitrary and is reasonably related to the evidence presented.

The prosecution relies on *Johnson v. City of Hartford*, 250 Conn. 364, 378 (1999), to argue that a mathematical formula is not required. While *Johnson* states that a mathematical formula is not required, it also implies that the court must provide a sufficient explanation connecting the evidence to the award. The defense correctly distinguishes *Johnson*, noting that in that case, the trial court "set forth its reasons for the additur" and "specifically addressed the evidence." Here, the trial court's memorandum of decision, while identifying the existence of medical evidence and a 35 percent disability, failed to explain how that evidence translated into the specific $15,000 figure.

The defense argues that the trial court failed to "bridge the gap" between the evidence and the specific dollar amount. The court agrees. A mere recitation of the plaintiff's injuries, without an explanation of the valuation of those injuries, does not satisfy the *Gormley* standard. The trial court did not explain why $15,000, rather than $10,000 or $20,000, was the appropriate compensation for the plaintiff's noneconomic damages. Without this explanation, the additur appears arbitrary.

Furthermore, the defense argues that the trial court improperly substituted its judgment for that of the jury regarding the credibility of the plaintiff's injuries. Under *Finneran v. N. Stamford Hosp.*, 261 Conn. 756, 772 (2002), additur is permitted only when the jury's award is "so inadequate as to shock the judicial conscience" and falls "outside the range of reasonable compensation." The trial court failed to provide a reasoned analysis explaining why the jury's credibility assessment was erroneous or how the $15,000 figure corrected the alleged inadequacy. The prosecution's assertion that the award "shocks the conscience" is not a substitute for the trial court's required analysis.

**IV. REMEDY**

The prosecution requests that the court remand solely for the statutory election process while preserving the $15,000 additur. The court declines this request. Because the trial court's additur order is substantively defective due to the lack of a reasoned explanation for the amount, the remedy cannot be a simple "procedural do-over."

The defense argues, and the court agrees, that the appropriate remedy is a new trial on the issue of damages. The procedural error and the substantive deficiency in the additur order cannot be cured by merely offering the defendants an election on an arbitrary figure. As held in *Gormley v. D'Elia*, 249 Conn. 392, 404 (1999), a trial court must articulate the basis for an additur. The failure to do so here precludes the court from enforcing that specific amount. The defendants are entitled to a new trial where the issue of damages can be litigated without the taint of the trial court's improper ruling.

**V. CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the court concludes that the trial court abused its discretion and violated Connecticut General Statutes §§ 52-216a and 52-228b. The judgment of the trial court is reversed. The matter is remanded to the trial court with direction to grant a new trial on the issue of damages.
