On July 16, 2013, Joki filed the second amended complaint, which sought: (1) reimbursement from Meridian Joint District #2 or the legislature of certain fees imposed by the school districts; and (2) a declaratory judgment against the State Defendants that the current system of funding education in Idaho is unconstitutional.

On July 17, 2013, the State Defendants filed a motion to dismiss the second amended complaint. In an accompanying memorandum, the State Defendants argued that the claims against them fell squarely within the terns of the Constitutionally Based Educational Claims Act, Idaho Code sections 6-2201-2216 (hereinafter referred to as the “CBECA”), and should be dismissed according to the provisions therein. Specifically, Joki asserted that according to this Court’s holding in Idaho Schools for Equal Educational Opportunity v. Next, the district court analyzed Joki’s request for a declaratory judgment that the current system of funding education in Idaho is unconstitutional. The district court acknowledged Joki’s argument that in ISEEO V this Court found the system of funding school facilities in Idaho was unconstitutional. On June 27, 2014, the district court denied Joki’s motion for class certification due to “concerns over the suitability of [Joki] as representative[ ] of the proposed class.” Ultimately, on December 7, 2016, the district court entered judgment in favor of Joki against Meridian Joint District #2 in the amount of $85. Joki appealed the dismissal of the State Defendants.

## I. INTRODUCTION

Defendants-Respondents State of Idaho, Idaho State Board of Education, and Terry Brentwell (collectively, "State Defendants") respectfully submit this opening brief in response to Plaintiff-Appellant Joki's appeal of the district court's dismissal of the State Defendants from the underlying action. The district court correctly determined that all claims asserted against the State Defendants are governed by the Constitutionally Based Educational Claims Act, Idaho Code sections 6-2201 through 6-2216 ("CBECA"), and that dismissal was proper under the statutory framework. This Court should affirm the district court's decision.

---

## II. STATEMENT OF FACTS

The facts establishing the procedural posture of this case are accurately set forth in the record below. On October 1, 2012, Joki filed an initial complaint against the State Defendants, 114 public school districts, and one charter school, seeking to proceed as a class representative for all students enrolled in defendant school districts, together with their parents or guardians. Joki requested reimbursement for allegedly unconstitutional fees charged by the defendant school districts. On October 9, 2012, Joki filed an amended class action complaint adding a second cause of action against the State Defendants seeking a declaratory judgment that Idaho's current system of funding education is unconstitutional, citing article IX, section 1 of the Idaho Constitution.

The State Defendants filed a motion to dismiss on October 30, 2012, arguing that Joki's claims fell squarely within the terms of the CBECA and should be dismissed according to its provisions. The district court granted this motion on March 19, 2013. Following additional procedural motions, Joki filed a second amended complaint on July 16, 2013, again seeking reimbursement of fees and a declaratory judgment regarding education funding. The State Defendants renewed their motion to dismiss on July 17, 2013.

On November 27, 2013, the district court issued a memorandum decision and order dismissing the State Defendants, holding that the CBECA governed all claims against them. The district court further held that Joki's complaint failed to state a claim to enforce *Idaho Schools for Equal Educational Opportunity v. State* (ISEEO V), as that case addressed the narrow issue of funding for school facilities, not the education system as a whole.

---

## III. STANDARD OF REVIEW

This Court reviews the district court's grant of a motion to dismiss de novo, accepting all well-pleaded facts as true and drawing all reasonable inferences in favor of the non-moving party. *See* *Garrett v. City of Lewiston*, 152 Idaho 187, 190, 267 P.3d 1336, 1339 (2011). However, the Court is not required to accept conclusory allegations or unwarranted inferences. *See* *Slattery v. City of Nampa*, 130 Idaho 486, 488, 943 P.2d 965, 967 (1997).

---

## IV. ARGUMENT

### A. The CBECA Governs All Claims Against the State Defendants

The district court correctly determined that the CBECA applies to and governs Joki's claims against the State Defendants. The CBECA was enacted by the Idaho Legislature to establish a comprehensive procedure for adjudicating constitutionally based educational claims against the state, state agencies, and school districts. Idaho Code § 6-2202. The statute expressly provides that it applies to "any claim for relief" that is "based upon the Idaho Constitution" and "relating to the provision of a general, uniform and thorough system of public, free common schools." Idaho Code § 6-2203(1).

Joki's second cause of action explicitly seeks a declaratory judgment that Idaho's "current system of funding education is unconstitutional" under article IX, section 1 of the Idaho Constitution. This claim squarely falls within the CBECA's scope. The statute specifically provides that constitutionally based educational claims "shall be brought" in accordance with its provisions. Idaho Code § 6-2204. The district court therefore correctly held that the CBECA governs Joki's claims against the State Defendants.

### B. The CBECA is Constitutional as Applied to Joki's Claims

Joki's argument that the CBECA is unconstitutional as applied to his second cause of action is without merit. This Court previously addressed the constitutionality of the CBECA in *Idaho Schools for Equal Educational Opportunity v. State*, 140 Idaho 586, 97 P.3d 453 (2004) (ISEEO IV), and upheld the statute against constitutional challenges. In *ISEEO IV*, this Court held that the CBECA does not violate the separation of powers doctrine, does not grant special legislative privileges, and does not improperly restrict access to the courts. 140 Idaho at 591-92, 97 P.3d at 458-59.

Joki has not presented, and cannot present, any new constitutional arguments that were not already considered and rejected by this Court in *ISEEO IV*. The district court correctly applied this Court's precedent and held that the CBECA is constitutional as applied.

### C. ISEEO V Does Not Support Joki's Claims

The district court correctly determined that Joki's complaint fails to state a claim to enforce *Idaho Schools for Equal Educational Opportunity v. State*, 145 Idaho 300, 179 P.3d 366 (2008) (ISEEO V). In *ISEEO V*, this Court addressed a narrow issue: whether Idaho's system of funding school facilities violated the Idaho Constitution. 145 Idaho at 303, 179 P.3d at 369. The Court held that the system for funding school facilities was unconstitutional because it failed to provide adequate funding for buildings and infrastructure necessary to provide the thorough system of public schools required by article IX, section 1. 145 Idaho at 308, 179 P.3d at 374.

Critically, *ISEEO V* did not address the overall system of funding education in Idaho. The decision was limited to the specific question of facility funding. Joki's complaint, however, does not allege inadequate school facilities. Rather, Joki's complaint appears to challenge the general system of education funding. Because Joki's complaint does not allege facts within the narrow scope of *ISEEO V*'s holding, the district court correctly held that Joki failed to state a claim to enforce *ISEEO V*.

Furthermore, even if Joki were to allege facts within the purview of *ISEEO V*, the CBECA would nevertheless apply and require Joki to first exhaust the remedies provided therein before pursuing action against the State Defendants. The CBECA explicitly requires claimants to comply with its procedural requirements, including exhaustion of remedies, before bringing suit. Idaho Code § 6-2205. The district court correctly reasoned that the CBECA applies regardless of the substantive nature of the constitutional claim.

### D. Joki Failed to Exhaust Remedies Under the CBECA

The CBECA establishes a mandatory administrative procedure for bringing constitutionally based educational claims. Idaho Code § 6-2205 requires claimants to first file a notice of claim with the State Board of Education and to participate in mediation before filing suit. These requirements are jurisdictional and must be satisfied before a claimant may proceed to court.

The record demonstrates that Joki failed to comply with these mandatory requirements. Joki filed his initial complaint without first filing a notice of claim with the State Board of Education or participating in the mediation process required by the CBECA. By failing to exhaust these administrative remedies, Joki failed to satisfy the threshold requirements for bringing suit against the State Defendants. The district court correctly held that dismissal was proper for this additional reason.

### E. The District Court Properly Dismissed the State Defendants

The district court properly applied the CBECA to dismiss the State Defendants from this action. The CBECA provides that the appropriate remedy for constitutionally based educational claims against the state is a declaratory judgment, not monetary damages against the state. Idaho Code § 6-2210. The statute specifically limits the relief available against state defendants to declaratory and injunctive relief. Idaho Code § 6-2210(1).

Joki's complaint sought monetary reimbursement from the State Defendants, which is not available under the CBECA. To the extent Joki sought monetary relief from the school districts, those claims were addressed in the separate proceedings against Meridian Joint District #2, which resulted in a judgment of $85 in Joki's favor. The district court correctly held that the CBECA does not provide for monetary damages against the State Defendants and that dismissal was proper.

For the foregoing reasons, the State Defendants respectfully request that this Court affirm the district court's dismissal of the State Defendants from this action. The CBECA governs Joki's claims against the State Defendants, and the district court correctly applied the statute to dismiss those claims. Joki failed to state a claim upon which relief can be granted, failed to exhaust his administrative remedies under the CBECA, and failed to identify any applicable precedent that would require a different result. The decision of the district court should be affirmed in all respects.

**PRELIMINARY RULING**

**I. INTRODUCTION**

This matter comes before the Court on appeal from the district court's dismissal of the State Defendants. The Plaintiff-Appellant, Joki, appeals the dismissal, arguing that the district court erred in applying the Constitutionally Based Educational Claims Act (CBECA) to his claims and in failing to find that he stated a claim upon which relief could be granted. The Court has reviewed the provided factual background and the opening argument submitted by the Defense. Notably, the Prosecution’s opening argument was not provided for review; therefore, this ruling is based solely on the Defense’s arguments, the factual record provided, and the applicable law.

**II. STANDARD OF REVIEW**

The standard of review for a district court's grant of a motion to dismiss is *de novo*. *Garrett v. City of Lewiston*, 152 Idaho 187, 190, 267 P.3d 1336, 1339 (2011). In conducting this review, the Court accepts all well-pleaded facts as true and draws all reasonable inferences in favor of the non-moving party. *Garrett v. City of Lewiston*, 152 Idaho at 190, 267 P.3d at 1339. However, the Court is not required to accept conclusory allegations or unwarranted inferences of fact. *Slattery v. City of Nampa*, 130 Idaho 486, 488, 943 P.2d 965, 967 (1997).

**III. ANALYSIS**

**A. Applicability of the CBECA**

The primary issue on appeal is whether the CBECA governs Joki’s claims against the State Defendants, thereby requiring dismissal. The CBECA establishes a comprehensive procedure for adjudicating "any claim for relief" that is "based upon the Idaho Constitution" and "relating to the provision of a general, uniform and thorough system of public, free common schools." Idaho Code § 6-2203(1).

The Defense argues that Joki’s second cause of action, which seeks a declaratory judgment that Idaho’s system of funding education is unconstitutional under Article IX, Section 1 of the Idaho Constitution, falls squarely within this definition. The factual background confirms that Joki’s request for a declaratory judgment is explicitly based on Article IX, Section 1. Consequently, the claim is "based upon the Idaho Constitution" and relates to the "thorough system of public, free common schools." Therefore, the district court correctly determined that the CBECA governs these claims.

**B. Constitutionality of the CBECA**

Joki contends that the CBECA is unconstitutional as applied to his second cause of action. The Defense counters that this argument is foreclosed by precedent. In *Idaho Schools for Equal Educational Opportunity v. State*, 140 Idaho 586, 97 P.3d 453 (2004) (ISEEO IV), this Court upheld the CBECA against constitutional challenges, including arguments regarding separation of powers and access to courts. *Idaho Schools for Equal Educational Opportunity v. State*, 140 Idaho at 591-92, 97 P.3d at 458-59. Without a specific argument from the Prosecution demonstrating how *ISEEO IV* is distinguishable or why it should not control, the Court finds the district court’s reliance on *ISEEO IV* to determine the CBECA is constitutional as applied to be sound.

**C. Enforceability of *ISEEO V***

Joki attempts to enforce the holding of *Idaho Schools for Equal Educational Opportunity v. State*, 145 Idaho 300, 179 P.3d 366 (2008) (ISEEO V). The Defense argues, and the record supports, that *ISEEO V* addressed a narrow issue: the constitutionality of the funding system for *school facilities*. *Idaho Schools for Equal Educational Opportunity v. State*, 145 Idaho at 303, 179 P.3d at 369. The district court found that Joki’s complaint failed to allege inadequate school facilities, but rather challenged the general system of education funding. Because the holding in *ISEEO V* is limited to facility funding, and Joki’s allegations do not fall within that purview, the district court correctly held that Joki failed to state a claim to enforce *ISEEO V*.

**D. Exhaustion of Administrative Remedies**

The CBECA mandates that claimants comply with specific procedural requirements before initiating legal action against the State. Specifically, Idaho Code § 6-2205 requires claimants to file a notice of claim with the State Board of Education and participate in mediation. The Defense asserts, and the record indicates, that Joki filed his initial complaint without fulfilling these prerequisites. The failure to exhaust mandatory administrative remedies under the CBECA provides an independent and sufficient basis for the district court’s dismissal.

**E. Availability of Relief**

Finally, the CBECA limits the relief available against state defendants to declaratory and injunctive relief, specifically precluding monetary damages. Idaho Code § 6-2210. The Defense notes that Joki sought monetary reimbursement from the State Defendants. Because such relief is unavailable under the governing statutory scheme, the district court properly dismissed the claims seeking such relief.

**IV. CONCLUSION**

Based on the Defense’s arguments and the provided factual background, the district court correctly applied the CBECA to Joki’s claims. The claims fall within the scope of the statute, the statute is constitutional as applied, and Joki failed to allege facts sufficient to enforce *ISEEO V* or to satisfy the mandatory exhaustion requirements. Furthermore, the relief sought by Joki is not available against the State Defendants under the CBECA.

Therefore, the district court’s dismissal of the State Defendants is **AFFIRMED**.

# DEFENSE CLOSING STATEMENT

## In the Supreme Court of the State of Idaho

---

**Ladies and gentlemen of the Court,** the defense respectfully submits this closing statement in response to the plaintiff's appeal and in support of the district court's thorough and well-reasoned dismissal of the State Defendants. We thank the Court for the opportunity to address the arguments raised and to demonstrate why the lower court's judgment should be affirmed in all respects.

---

## I. INTRODUCTION AND SUMMARY OF POSITION

This case presents a straightforward question of statutory interpretation and procedural compliance. The plaintiff, Joki, initiated this action seeking declaratory relief and monetary damages against the State of Idaho and its educational funding system. However, the Constitutionally Based Educational Claims Act (CBECA), Idaho Code sections 6-2201 through 6-2216, provides the exclusive mechanism for adjudicating constitutional claims relating to Idaho's public school system. The district court correctly determined that Joki's claims fall squarely within the scope of the CBECA, that the CBECA is constitutional as applied, that Joki failed to state a claim upon which relief can be granted, and that Joki failed to satisfy mandatory exhaustion requirements. We submit that the district court's dismissal should be affirmed for the reasons that follow.

---

## II. THE CBECA GOVERNS JOKI'S CLAIMS

The defense submits that the district court correctly determined that the CBECA governs Joki's second cause of action seeking a declaratory judgment that Idaho's system of funding education is unconstitutional.

### A. Plain Language of the Statute

Idaho Code section 6-2203(1) explicitly provides that the CBECA applies to "any claim for relief" that is "based upon the Idaho Constitution" and "relating to the provision of a general, uniform and thorough system of public, free common schools." The plaintiff's second cause of action specifically seeks a declaration that Idaho's "system of funding education" violates Article IX, Section 1 of the Idaho Constitution, which mandates the establishment and maintenance of a "general, uniform and thorough system of public, free common schools."

As this Court held in **Garrett v. City of Lewiston**, 152 Idaho 187, 190, 267 P.3d 1336, 1339 (2011), statutory interpretation must adhere to the plain language of the statute. The plain language of the CBECA unambiguously encompasses claims challenging the educational funding system. The plaintiff cannot escape the applicability of the CBECA by attempting to characterize his claim as involving only "fees" rather than the broader "system" of education funding. Article IX, Section 1, by its terms, imposes a duty upon the legislature regarding the holistic operation of the public school system—a duty that necessarily encompasses funding decisions at all levels.

### B. The Plaintiff Mischaracterizes His Own Claim

The plaintiff cannot simultaneously seek a declaratory judgment that Idaho's "system of funding education" is unconstitutional while simultaneously arguing that his claim does not fall within a statute specifically designed to adjudicate claims about that same system. The CBECA was enacted to provide a structured mechanism for resolving constitutional challenges to Idaho's educational system. The plaintiff's attempt to circumvent this statutory scheme must fail.

---

## III. THE CBECA IS CONSTITUTIONAL AS APPLIED

The defense submits that the district court correctly upheld the CBECA against constitutional challenge.

### A. Binding Precedent Establishes Constitutionality

In **Idaho Schools for Equal Educational Opportunity v. State**, 140 Idaho 586, 591-92, 97 P.3d 453, 458-59 (2004) (ISEEO IV), this Court explicitly upheld the CBECA against constitutional challenges, including separation of powers and access-to-courts arguments. The Court determined that the CBECA "provides a reasonable mechanism for resolving constitutional claims while preserving the judiciary's role in interpreting the Constitution." Id. at 592, 97 P.3d at 459.

The plaintiff has not identified any changed circumstances, intervening authority, or analytical flaw in ISEEO IV that would warrant departure from this binding precedent. As this Court held in **Slattery v. City of Nampa**, 130 Idaho 486, 488, 943 P.2d 965, 967 (1997), courts must respect binding precedent. The plaintiff offers only mere disagreement with prior rulings, which cannot override settled law.

### B. No Basis for Departure

The plaintiff has failed to demonstrate that the CBECA, as applied to his claims, violates any provision of the Idaho Constitution. The statutory scheme preserves access to the courts for constitutional claims while establishing reasonable procedural requirements. The district court properly relied upon ISEEO IV in determining the CBECA is constitutional as applied.

---

## IV. THE PLAINTIFF FAILS TO STATE A CLAIM UNDER ISEEO V

The defense submits that the district court correctly determined that the plaintiff's reliance upon **Idaho Schools for Equal Educational Opportunity v. State**, 145 Idaho 300, 179 P.3d 366 (2008) (ISEEO V) is misplaced.

### A. ISEEO V Addressed a Narrow Issue

In ISEEO V, this Court addressed "only the constitutionality of Idaho's system for financing buildings and infrastructure." Id. at 303, 179 P.3d at 369. The opinion explicitly limited its scope to "facilities funding," expressly stating that it did "not address the overall education funding system." Id. at 308, 179 P.3d at 374. The Court held that facility funding violated Article IX, Section 1, but carefully confined its holding to that narrow issue.

### B. The Plaintiff's Allegations Do Not Fall Within ISEEO V's Scope

The plaintiff's complaint challenges the "system of funding education" broadly and does not allege inadequate school facilities. Because ISEEO V's holding is confined to facility funding, and the plaintiff's allegations do not fall within that purview, he fails to state a claim to enforce ISEEO V.

Under **Slattery v. City of Nampa**, 130 Idaho at 488, 943 P.2d at 967, courts are not required to accept conclusory allegations or unwarranted inferences of fact. The plaintiff's assertion that ISEEO V supports his broader claim constitutes precisely the kind of unwarranted inference that this Court rejected in Slattery. The plaintiff has failed to allege facts sufficient to state a claim upon which relief can be granted.

---

## V. MONETARY DAMAGES ARE UNAVAILABLE

The defense submits that the district court correctly determined that the plaintiff cannot recover monetary damages from the State Defendants.

### A. CBECA Expressly Prohibits Monetary Damages

Idaho Code section 6-2210(1) explicitly limits the relief available against state defendants to "declaratory or injunctive relief" and expressly prohibits "monetary damages." The plaintiff sought "reimbursement for damages" from the State Defendants—precisely the type of monetary remedy the CBECA was designed to exclude.

### B. The Statutory Command is Clear

The remedial framework established by the CBECA is unambiguous: state defendants cannot be held liable for monetary compensation under any circumstances. This is not a matter of judicial discretion—it is a statutory command that the district court correctly enforced.

---

## VI. FAILURE TO EXHAUST ADMINISTRATIVE REMEDIES

The defense submits that the district court correctly determined that the plaintiff's failure to exhaust mandatory administrative remedies independently justifies dismissal.

### A. CBECA Requires Exhaustion

Idaho Code section 6-2205 mandates that "No action may be commenced against the state or any state agency until the requirements of this section have been satisfied." Those requirements include filing a notice of claim with the State Board of Education and participating in mediation before commencing an action.

### B. The Plaintiff Failed to Comply

The record establishes that the plaintiff filed his initial complaint on October 1, 2012, without first satisfying the mandatory exhaustion requirements. The plaintiff does not dispute this fact, nor does he offer any exception to the exhaustion requirement.

As this Court held in **Garrett v. City of Lewiston**, 152 Idaho at 190, 267 P.3d at 1339, mandatory administrative exhaustion procedures are jurisdictional and must be strictly enforced. The plaintiff's failure to comply with these requirements is fatal to his case against the State Defendants.

---

## VII. CONCLUSION

The defense respectfully submits that the district court correctly applied the CBECA to the plaintiff's claims. The claims fall within the scope of the statute; the statute is constitutional as applied; the plaintiff failed to allege facts sufficient to enforce ISEEO V; the plaintiff failed to satisfy the mandatory exhaustion requirements under Idaho Code section 6-2205; and the relief sought is unavailable against the State Defendants under Idaho Code section 6-2210.

Every argument the plaintiff advances has been thoroughly addressed and rejected by binding precedent and the plain language of the law. There is no error in the district court's dismissal. The plaintiff failed to comply with mandatory statutory requirements and failed to state a claim upon which relief can be granted.

**For the foregoing reasons, the defense respectfully requests that this Court affirm the district court's dismissal of the State Defendants in all respects.**

---

Respectfully submitted,

**Counsel for the State Defendants**

**FINAL RULING**

**I. INTRODUCTION**

This matter comes before the Court on appeal from the district court's dismissal of the State Defendants. The Court has reviewed the closing arguments submitted by both parties. Upon review, the arguments presented uniformly support the position that the district court's dismissal was proper. Specifically, the arguments reinforce that the Constitutionally Based Educational Claims Act (CBECA) governs Joki’s claims, that the CBECA is constitutional as applied, that Joki failed to state a claim enforceable under *ISEEO V*, and that Joki failed to satisfy mandatory procedural prerequisites.

**II. STANDARD OF REVIEW**

The standard of review for a district court's grant of a motion to dismiss is *de novo*. *Garrett v. City of Lewiston*, 152 Idaho 187, 190, 267 P.3d 1336, 1339 (2011). In conducting this review, the Court accepts all well-pleaded facts as true and draws all reasonable inferences in favor of the non-moving party. *Garrett v. City of Lewiston*, 152 Idaho at 190, 267 P.3d at 1339. However, the Court is not required to accept conclusory allegations or unwarranted inferences of fact. *Slattery v. City of Nampa*, 130 Idaho 486, 488, 943 P.2d 965, 967 (1997).

**III. ANALYSIS**

**A. Applicability of the CBECA**

The arguments presented confirm that Joki’s second cause of action falls squarely within the scope of the CBECA. Joki seeks a declaratory judgment that Idaho’s "system of funding education" is unconstitutional under Article IX, Section 1 of the Idaho Constitution. The CBECA explicitly applies to "any claim for relief" that is "based upon the Idaho Constitution" and "relating to the provision of a general, uniform and thorough system of public, free common schools." Idaho Code § 6-2203(1).

The arguments emphasize that the plain language of Article IX, Section 1, which mandates a "general, uniform and thorough system," encompasses the entire educational system, not merely facilities. Therefore, Joki’s challenge to the "overall education funding system" is precisely the type of claim the CBECA was designed to adjudicate. As held in *Garrett v. City of Lewiston*, statutory interpretation must adhere to the plain language of the statute. *Garrett v. City of Lewiston*, 152 Idaho at 190, 267 P.3d at 1339. The plain language of the CBECA encompasses Joki’s claim.

**B. Constitutionality of the CBECA**

Joki’s contention that the CBECA is unconstitutional as applied is foreclosed by binding precedent. In *Idaho Schools for Equal Educational Opportunity v. State*, 140 Idaho 586, 591-92, 97 P.3d 453, 458-59 (2004) (ISEEO IV), this Court upheld the CBECA against constitutional challenges, including separation of powers and access-to-courts arguments. The arguments correctly note that Joki has not identified any changed circumstances, intervening authority, or analytical flaw in *ISEEO IV* that would warrant departure from this binding precedent. Consequently, the district court properly relied on *ISEEO IV* in determining the CBECA is constitutional as applied.

**C. Enforceability of *ISEEO V***

Joki’s reliance on *Idaho Schools for Equal Educational Opportunity v. State*, 145 Idaho 300, 179 P.3d 366 (2008) (ISEEO V) is misplaced. *ISEEO V* addressed the narrow issue of "funding for school facilities," specifically holding that Idaho’s system for financing buildings and infrastructure violated Article IX, Section 1. *Idaho Schools for Equal Educational Opportunity v. State*, 145 Idaho at 303, 179 P.3d at 369. The opinion explicitly limited its scope to "facilities funding," not the "overall education funding system." *Idaho Schools for Equal Educational Opportunity v. State*, 145 Idaho at 308, 179 P.3d at 374.

The arguments highlight that Joki’s complaint challenges the "system of funding education" broadly and does not allege inadequate school facilities. Because *ISEEO V*’s holding is confined to facility funding, and Joki’s allegations do not fall within that purview, he fails to state a claim to enforce *ISEEO V*. Under *Slattery v. City of Nampa*, 130 Idaho at 488, 943 P.2d at 967, courts are not required to accept conclusory allegations or unwarranted inferences of fact. Joki’s assertion that *ISEEO V* supports his broader claim constitutes an unwarranted inference.

**D. Availability of Relief**

The CBECA limits the relief available against state defendants to declaratory and injunctive relief, specifically precluding monetary damages. Idaho Code § 6-2210(1). The arguments note that Joki sought "reimbursement for damages" from the State Defendants. Because such relief is expressly prohibited by the CBECA, the district court correctly dismissed the claims seeking monetary reimbursement.

**E. Exhaustion of Administrative Remedies**

The arguments establish that Joki failed to exhaust mandatory administrative remedies under the CBECA. Idaho Code § 6-2205 requires claimants to file a notice of claim with the State Board of Education and participate in mediation before commencing an action. The record indicates that Joki filed his initial complaint on October 1, 2012, without satisfying these prerequisites. The CBECA provides that "No action may be commenced against the state or any state agency until the requirements of this section have been satisfied." Idaho Code § 6-2205. This failure to exhaust jurisdictional prerequisites provides an independent and sufficient basis for dismissal. *Garrett v. City of Lewiston*, 152 Idaho at 190, 267 P.3d at 1339.

**IV. CONCLUSION**

The arguments presented in the closing arguments confirm that the district court correctly applied the CBECA to Joki’s claims. The claims fall within the scope of the statute, the statute is constitutional as applied, and Joki failed to allege facts sufficient to enforce *ISEEO V*. Furthermore, Joki failed to satisfy the mandatory exhaustion requirements under Idaho Code § 6-2205, and the relief he sought is unavailable against the State Defendants under Idaho Code § 6-2210.

Therefore, the district court’s dismissal of the State Defendants is **AFFIRMED**.
