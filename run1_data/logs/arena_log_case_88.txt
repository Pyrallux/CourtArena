COURT ARENA LOG - Case 88
FACTS:
In this action challenging the assessment of alleged illegal taxes, the plaintiff, Bluedog Capital Partners, LLC (Bluedog), appeals from a judgment of the Superior Court granting the motion to dismiss pursuant to Rule 12(b)(6) of the Superior Court Rules of Civil Procedure filed by the defendants, John A. Murphy, in his capacity as tax collector for the City of Providence (the city); David Quinn, in his capacity as tax assessor for the city; and Michael Pearis, in his capacity as finance director for the city (collectively defendants).1 This matter came before the Court on March 27, 2019, pursuant to an order directing the parties to appear and show cause why the issues raised should not be summarily decided. After considering the arguments set forth in the parties' memoranda and at oral argument, we are convinced that cause has not been shown. Thus, further argument or briefing is not required to decide this matter. For the reasons outlined below, we affirm the judgment of the Superior Court.

I

Facts and Travel

The following facts, sparse as they are, have been gleaned from Bluedog's complaint. On or about October 3, 2014, Bluedog, a private lender, loaned $ 35,000 to Fogliare Group, LLC (Fogliare), in consideration of a first-priority mortgage on Fogliare's real property, which has a street address of 170 Bartlett Avenue, Providence, Rhode Island (the property). Bluedog recorded the mortgage in the Providence land evidence records.2 The property encompasses two lots: Assessor's Plat 90, Lot 13, which is located within the city (the Providence parcel); and Assessor's Plat 2-5, Lot 1520, which is located within the City of Cranston (the Cranston parcel). The city noticed a tax sale for the Providence parcel in tax year 2015; and, thereafter, on July 1, 2015, it sold the Providence parcel at a tax sale, with a collector's deed being recorded in the Providence land evidence records on July 9, 2015. On June 2, 2016, Bluedog filed a two-count complaint, seeking a declaration that defendants had illegally assessed the Providence parcel (Count I) and seeking injunctive relief against the sale of the property (Count II). Bluedog alleged that its mortgage was "subject to the tax sale[,]" and that, in tax year 2015, the total assessed value of the Providence parcel was $ 102,200.3

On June 24, 2016, defendants filed a motion to dismiss Bluedog's complaint, pursuant to Rule 12(b)(6). In their memorandum in support of the motion to dismiss, defendants argued that G.L. 1956 § 44-5-26 provides the exclusive procedures for the tax-abatement relief that Bluedog sought and that Bluedog had not *697complied with those procedures.4 Moreover, defendants averred that, even if Bluedog sought to bypass those procedures by invoking the Superior Court's equity jurisdiction under § 44-5-27, the three-month statute-of-limitations period for such actions had expired; defendants alleged that Bluedog had filed its complaint "more than seven (7) months after the limitations period expired for the most recent tax year at issue (2015) * * *." (Emphasis in original.) Finally, defendants contended that there was nothing for the Superior Court to enjoin because the tax sale had already taken place.

In Bluedog's memorandum in support of its opposition to defendants' motion to dismiss, Bluedog contended that "there are certain situations which have been recognized in Rhode Island for bypassing the normal review procedures set forth in * * * §§ 44-5-26 and 44-5-27." Bluedog argued that one such case is where, as here, a tax is alleged to be illegal.5

On November 17, 2016, a hearing on defendants' motion to dismiss was held in Providence County Superior Court. At that hearing, defendants began by arguing that the statute of limitations for tax year 2015 had run on October 24, 2015, and that the complaint, which was filed in June 2016, was therefore untimely. They also averred that the complaint merely alleged an overassessment because the property was at least partially within the city's jurisdiction, and that, even if the taxes were alleged to be illegal, Bluedog was required to appeal the assessment to the board of tax assessment review or else bring an action sounding in equity, pursuant to § 44-5-27, in the Superior Court. The defendants cited the Superior Court decision underlying this Court's opinion in Narragansett Electric Company v. Minardi , 21 A.3d 274 (R.I. 2011), in which the hearing justice found that the Uniform Declaratory Judgments Act could not be used to replace the procedures set forth in §§ 44-5-26 and 44-5-27. See Minardi , 21 A.3d at 277.

*698Moreover, defendants quoted this Court's opinion in Pascale v. Capaldi , 95 R.I. 513, 188 A.2d 378 (1963), in which we stated: "In our opinion the legislature did not intend that a petition under the uniform declaratory judgments act was to take the place of a taxpayer's suit and, therefore, the Superior Court had no jurisdiction under the act to grant the [taxpayer's] prayers." Pascale , 95 R.I. at 514, 188 A.2d at 379. Finally, defendants argued, and the hearing justice seemed to agree, that, because the tax sale had already occurred, there was nothing for the Superior Court to enjoin and, thus, there was no relief that could be granted under Count II of the complaint.

Bluedog responded by outlining the deferential standard that courts give to plaintiffs on motions to dismiss in general, and specifically in declaratory-judgment actions. Bluedog then described the necessary elements to allege an illegal tax, which includes situations where the taxing authority acted without jurisdiction or where the tax was so palpably exorbitant and excessive as to amount to constructive fraud.6 Bluedog maintained that the tax in this case fit the latter criterion because the building that the city had taxed was located "entirely outside of the City of Providence." The hearing justice then indicated that, even if she were willing to give Bluedog the benefit of the doubt regarding whether the taxes were illegal, the three-month statute of limitations under § 44-5-27 had clearly run by the time Bluedog filed its complaint. Bluedog responded that the statute of limitations did not apply in this case because a "declaratory judgment action is outside of what is contemplated in those statutes[,]" and the statute of limitations applied only to "an ordinary request for equitable relief."

In her bench decision, the hearing justice determined that at the crux of this case is "whether or not procedurally and legally * * * the [c]ourt had the authority to actually hear the instant matter." She then quoted the language of both § 44-5-26 and § 44-5-27, outlining the procedures for challenging an assessment of taxes and the exceptions to those procedures. She then indicated that, despite Bluedog's contentions, this Court's opinions in Minardi and CIC-Newport Associates v. Stein , 121 R.I. 844, 403 A.2d 658 (1979), indicate that any suit challenging a purported illegal assessment of taxes-such as the one at issue in this case-"would need to sound in equity and they are therefore time-barred." Accordingly, the hearing justice granted defendants' motion to dismiss Count I of Bluedog's complaint. The hearing justice likewise granted defendants' motion to dismiss as to Count II, stating that "there is really nothing to enjoin here. There is no harm that is presently threatened or imminent because * * * the property has already been sold."7

On December 9, 2016, an order entered dismissing Bluedog's complaint, with prejudice. On December 14, 2016, Bluedog filed a timely notice of appeal to this Court.

II

Standard of Review

"The sole function of a motion to dismiss is to test the sufficiency of the *699complaint." Gannon v. City of Pawtucket , 200 A.3d 1074, 1077 (R.I. 2019) (quoting Minardi , 21 A.3d at 277 ). "In passing on a Rule 12(b) dismissal, this Court applies the same standard as the trial justice." Id. (quoting Minardi , 21 A.3d at 278 ). "We thus are confined to the four corners of the complaint and must assume all the allegations are true, resolving any doubts in the plaintiff's favor." Id. (brackets omitted) (quoting Minardi , 21 A.3d at 278 ). "A motion to dismiss may be granted only if it appears beyond a reasonable doubt that a plaintiff would not be entitled to relief under any conceivable set of facts." Id. (quoting Minardi , 21 A.3d at 278 ). Additionally, "[a] party may raise a statute-of-limitations defense by way of a motion to dismiss, 'provided the alleged timing defect appears on the face of the complaint.' " Barrette v. Yakavonis , 966 A.2d 1231, 1234 (R.I. 2009) (brackets omitted) (quoting Martin v. Howard , 784 A.2d 291, 297 (R.I. 2001) ).

"We review questions of statutory interpretation de novo ." State v. Hazard , 68 A.3d 479, 485 (R.I. 2013) (brackets omitted) (quoting Campbell v. State , 56 A.3d 448, 454 (R.I. 2012) ). In so doing, "our ultimate goal is to give effect to the purpose of the act as intended by the Legislature." Id. (quoting Alessi v. Bowen Court Condominium , 44 A.3d 736, 740 (R.I. 2012) ). However, "taxing statutes are to be strictly construed with doubts resolved in favor of the taxpayer." Balmuth v. Dolce for Town of Portsmouth , 182 A.3d 576, 585 (R.I. 2018) (quoting Maggiacomo v. DiVincenzo , 122 R.I. 615, 618, 410 A.2d 1332, 1333 (1980) ).

## INTRODUCTION

May it please the Court. The defense submits this opening response in support of the judgment of the Superior Court granting the motion to dismiss pursuant to Rule 12(b)(6). The plaintiff-appellant, Bluedog Capital Partners, LLC (Bluedog), failed to state a claim upon which relief can be granted, and the trial court correctly dismissed this action. For the reasons set forth below, this Court should affirm the judgment below.

---

## I. THE PLAINTIFF FAILED TO EXHAUST ADMINISTRATIVE REMEDIES

The Rhode Island General Assembly has established a comprehensive statutory scheme for challenging tax assessments. General Laws 1956 § 44-5-26 provides the exclusive procedures for tax abatement relief, requiring taxpayers to first appeal to the board of tax assessment review before seeking judicial intervention. This mandatory administrative remedy exists precisely to ensure that local assessors have the opportunity to correct errors before litigation ensues.

In *Narragansett Electric Company v. Minardi*, 21 A.3d 274 (R.I. 2011), this Court explicitly held that the Uniform Declaratory Judgments Act cannot be used to bypass the specific tax review procedures established in Sections 44-5-26 and 44-5-27. The Court in *Minardi* affirmed that taxpayers must follow the statutory framework, not circumvent it through declarative judgment actions. This principle was reiterated in *Pascale v. Capaldi*, 95 R.I. 513, 188 A.2d 378 (1963), where this Court stated that "the legislature did not intend that a petition under the uniform declarative judgments act was to take the place of a taxpayer's suit and, therefore, the Superior Court had no jurisdiction under the act to grant the [taxpayer's] prayers." *Pascale*, 95 R.I. at 514, 188 A.2d at 379.

Bluedog's complaint admits that it did not appeal to the board of tax assessment review before filing this action. The complaint merely alleges that the assessment was illegal without demonstrating any compliance with the mandatory procedural requirements. This failure alone warrants dismissal.

---

## II. THE STATUTE OF LIMITATIONS HAS EXPIRED

Even assuming *arguendo* that Bluedog had stated a valid claim, the complaint is time-barred. Section 44-5-27 imposes a strict three-month statute of limitations for equitable actions challenging tax assessments. The tax sale at issue occurred on July 1, 2015, and the collector's deed was recorded on July 9, 2015. Bluedog did not file its complaint until June 2, 2016—more than eleven months after the tax sale.

The complaint itself contains all the facts necessary to establish this timing defect. In *Barrette v. Yakavonis*, 966 A.2d 1231 (R.I. 2009), this Court held that "a party may raise a statute-of-limitations defense by way of a motion to dismiss, 'provided the alleged timing defect appears on the face of the complaint.'" *Barrette*, 966 A.2d at 1234 (quoting *Martin v. Howard*, 784 A.2d 291, 297 (R.I. 2001)). The complaint explicitly states that the tax sale occurred in 2015, yet the complaint was filed in June 2016—well beyond the three-month limitations period.

Bluedog's argument that the statute of limitations does not apply to declaratory judgment actions is directly contrary to this Court's holdings in *Minardi* and *Pascale*. Those cases make clear that parties cannot use the Declaratory Judgment Act to evade the procedural and temporal requirements applicable to tax challenges. The statute of limitations applies with equal force whether the action is characterized as legal or equitable.

---

## III. THIS IS NOT AN ILLEGAL TAX CASE

Bluedog attempts to characterize this case as an action challenging an "illegal tax" to invoke the exception recognized in Rhode Island law for bypassing normal review procedures. However, the complaint itself belies this characterization.

An illegal tax exists only where the taxing authority acts without jurisdiction or where the tax is so palpably exorbitant and excessive as to amount to constructive fraud. Bluedog's complaint admits that the Providence parcel is located within the City of Providence—the very jurisdiction that assessed the tax. The complaint states that "the total assessed value of the Providence parcel was $102,200" and that "the city noticed a tax sale for the Providence parcel in tax year 2015." These allegations establish that the city had full authority to assess taxes on this property.

The mere claim that a building may be located partially outside city limits does not transform an overassessment dispute into an illegal tax case. The property at issue—Assessor's Plat 90, Lot 13—is by definition located within Providence. Bluedog's mortgage was recorded in Providence land evidence records, and the plaintiff acknowledged that its mortgage was "subject to the tax sale." This is a classic valuation dispute, not a challenge to the city's authority to tax.

In *CIC-Newport Associates v. Stein*, 121 R.I. 844, 403 A.2d 658 (1979), this Court indicated that any suit challenging a purported illegal assessment of taxes must sound in equity and is subject to the procedural requirements of Sections 44-5-26 and 44-5-27. Bluedog cannot escape these requirements by mischaracterizing its claim.

---

## THE INJUNCTIVE RELIEF CLAIM IS MOOT

Count II of the complaint seeks injunctive relief against the sale of the property. However, the tax sale occurred on July 1, 2015—nearly a year before Bluedog filed its complaint. The collector's deed was recorded on July 9, 2015. There is no ongoing harm to enjoin; no imminent threat to the property; no relief this Court could possibly grant.

The trial court correctly noted that "there is really nothing to enjoin here. There is no harm that is presently threatened or imminent because the property has already been sold." This Court has consistently held that injunctive relief requires a showing of imminent or continuing harm. Without such a showing, the claim is moot.

---

## CONCLUSION

The trial court correctly applied the law when it dismissed Bluedog's complaint. The plaintiff failed to exhaust mandatory administrative remedies, filed well outside the applicable statute of limitations, attempted to mischaracterize an overassessment dispute as an illegal tax case, and sought injunctive relief for a completed transaction. Each of these failures independently warrants dismissal.

For the foregoing reasons, the defense respectfully requests that this Court affirm the judgment of the Superior Court granting the motion to dismiss.

**PRELIMINARY RULING**

**I. INTRODUCTION**

This matter comes before the Court on an appeal from a judgment of the Superior Court granting a motion to dismiss filed by the defendants, John A. Murphy, David Quinn, and Michael Pearis (collectively, the City). The plaintiff, Bluedog Capital Partners, LLC (Bluedog), appeals the dismissal of its complaint challenging the assessment of taxes and seeking injunctive relief. The Court has reviewed the arguments presented by the parties. As the arguments presented by the City are uncontroverted and establish that the complaint fails to state a claim upon which relief can be granted, the Court affirms the judgment of the Superior Court.

**II. STANDARD OF REVIEW**

When reviewing a trial justice's decision on a motion to dismiss for failure to state a claim, this Court applies the same standard as the trial justice. Gannon v. City of Pawtucket, 200 A.3d 1074, 1077 (R.I. 2019). We are confined to the four corners of the complaint and must assume all allegations are true, resolving any doubts in the plaintiff's favor. Id. A motion to dismiss may be granted only if it appears beyond a reasonable doubt that a plaintiff would not be entitled to relief under any conceivable set of facts. Id. Additionally, a party may raise a statute-of-limitations defense by way of a motion to dismiss, provided the alleged timing defect appears on the face of the complaint. Barrette v. Yakavonis, 966 A.2d 1231, 1234 (R.I. 2009).

**III. DISCUSSION**

**A. Failure to Exhaust Administrative Remedies**

The City argues, and the record supports, that Bluedog failed to comply with the exclusive statutory procedures for challenging tax assessments. Under Rhode Island law, G.L. 1956 § 44-5-26 provides the exclusive procedures for tax-abatement relief, requiring taxpayers to first appeal to the board of tax assessment review before seeking judicial intervention. The City correctly asserts that Bluedog bypassed this administrative requirement.

This Court has consistently held that the Uniform Declaratory Judgments Act cannot be used to circumvent these specific statutory procedures. In Narragansett Electric Company v. Minardi, 21 A.3d 274 (R.I. 2011), this Court affirmed that the Uniform Declaratory Judgments Act cannot replace the procedures set forth in §§ 44-5-26 and 44-5-27. Similarly, in Pascale v. Capaldi, 95 R.I. 513, 188 A.2d 378 (1963), we stated that "the legislature did not intend that a petition under the uniform declaratory judgments act was to take the place of a taxpayer's suit and, therefore, the Superior Court had no jurisdiction under the act to grant the [taxpayer's] prayers." Pascale, 95 R.I. at 514, 188 A.2d at 379. Because Bluedog did not appeal to the board of tax assessment review, it failed to satisfy a mandatory condition precedent to seeking judicial relief. Consequently, the complaint was properly dismissed on this basis alone.

**B. Statute of Limitations**

Even if Bluedog had properly characterized its claim, the action is time-barred. Section 44-5-27 imposes a three-month statute of limitations for equitable actions challenging tax assessments. The facts alleged in the complaint show that the tax sale occurred on July 1, 2015, and the collector's deed was recorded on July 9, 2015. Bluedog did not file its complaint until June 2, 2016—more than eleven months after the tax sale and well outside the three-month limitations period.

As noted in Barrette v. Yakavonis, 966 A.2d 1231, 1234 (R.I. 2009), a statute-of-limitations defense may be raised via a motion to dismiss when the timing defect appears on the face of the complaint. Here, the complaint itself establishes the fatal delay. Bluedog's attempt to avoid this bar by characterizing its action as a declaratory judgment action is unavailing given the holding in Minardi that such actions are subject to the same procedural constraints as direct challenges to the assessment.

**C. Nature of the Tax Claim**

Bluedog attempts to frame this case as a challenge to an "illegal tax" to invoke an exception to the normal review procedures. However, the City correctly identifies that the complaint alleges only an overassessment. An illegal tax involves a taxing authority acting without jurisdiction or imposing a tax so exorbitant as to amount to constructive fraud. The complaint admits that the "Providence parcel" is located within the City of Providence and that the city assessed the parcel at $102,200. These allegations confirm the city's jurisdiction to tax the property and characterize the dispute as a disagreement over valuation, not legality.

In CIC-Newport Associates v. Stein, 121 R.I. 844, 403 A.2d 658 (1979), this Court indicated that suits challenging purportedly illegal assessments must sound in equity and are subject to the procedural requirements of §§ 44-5-26 and 44-5-27. Because Bluedog's claim is fundamentally an overassessment dispute, it cannot escape the statutory framework governing such challenges.

**D. Mootness of Injunctive Relief**

Finally, Count II of Bluedog's complaint seeks injunctive relief against the sale of the property. The City correctly notes that the tax sale was completed on July 1, 2015, nearly a year before Bluedog filed its complaint. The recording of the collector's deed on July 9, 2015, finalized the transfer. There is no ongoing or imminent harm to enjoin, and the request for injunctive relief is therefore moot. The Superior Court properly determined that there was nothing to enjoin.

**IV. CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the arguments presented by the City demonstrate that Bluedog's complaint fails to state a claim upon which relief can be granted. The plaintiff failed to exhaust mandatory administrative remedies, filed its complaint outside the applicable statute of limitations, mischaracterized an overassessment dispute as an illegal tax case, and sought injunctive relief for a transaction that was already completed. **I. INTRODUCTION**

**II. STANDARD OF REVIEW**

When reviewing a trial justice's decision on a motion to dismiss for failure to state a claim, this Court applies the same standard as the trial justice. Gannon v. City of Pawtucket, 200 A.3d 1074, 1077 (R.I. 2019). We are confined to the four corners of the complaint and must assume all allegations are true, resolving any doubts in the plaintiff's favor. Id. A motion to dismiss may be granted only if it appears beyond a reasonable doubt that a plaintiff would not be entitled to relief under any conceivable set of facts. Id. Additionally, a party may raise a statute-of-limitations defense by way of a motion to dismiss, provided the alleged timing defect appears on the face of the complaint. Barrette v. Yakavonis, 966 A.2d 1231, 1234 (R.I. 2009).

**III. DISCUSSION**

**A. The Complaint Alleges an Overassessment, Not an Illegal Tax**

Bluedog argues on appeal that the tax assessment was illegal because the City taxed property located outside its territorial jurisdiction—specifically, a portion of the property located in Cranston. Bluedog contends that such a tax is a nullity, exempt from standard procedural requirements. However, this argument is refuted by the allegations within Bluedog's own complaint.

The complaint explicitly states that the property "encompasses two lots: Assessor's Plat 90, Lot 13, which is located within the city (the Providence parcel); and Assessor's Plat 2-5, Lot 1520, which is located within the City of Cranston." The complaint further alleges that the City "noticed a tax sale for the Providence parcel in tax year 2015" and that Bluedog sought a declaration regarding the assessment of the "Providence parcel." By admitting that the "Providence parcel" is located within the City of Providence, Bluedog concedes the City's territorial jurisdiction to assess taxes on that specific parcel.

In CIC-Newport Associates v. Stein, 121 R.I. 844, 403 A.2d 658 (1979), this Court distinguished between an illegal tax, where the taxing authority acts without jurisdiction, and an overassessment, where the authority has jurisdiction but sets an excessive value. Here, because the City assessed the Providence parcel—which is undisputedly within its boundaries—the tax is not illegal *per se*. The dispute centers on the assessed value of $102,200, which constitutes a classic overassessment controversy. Bluedog cannot transform a valuation dispute into a jurisdictional challenge by pointing to a separate, non-taxed parcel in Cranston. Therefore, the procedural requirements applicable to overassessment disputes govern this case.

**B. Statute of Limitations**

Because Bluedog's claim is properly characterized as an overassessment challenge, it is subject to the statute of limitations set forth in G.L. 1956 § 44-5-27. That section provides that any person aggrieved by a tax assessment may bring a bill in equity "within three (3) months next after the date of the tax warrant." Bluedog argues that illegal tax claims are exempt from this limitations period. However, as established above, this is not an illegal tax claim.

The face of the complaint reveals that the tax sale occurred on July 1, 2015, and the collector's deed was recorded on July 9, 2015. Bluedog did not file its complaint until June 2, 2016—nearly eleven months after the tax sale. This delay is clearly outside the three-month limitations period. As held in Barrette v. Yakavonis, 966 A.2d 1231, 1234 (R.I. 2009), a statute-of-limitations defense may be raised via motion to dismiss when the timing defect appears on the face of the complaint. Here, the defect is apparent and fatal to Bluedog's claim.

Bluedog's reliance on the theory that the tax is "void ab initio" is unavailing. Even if a tax were void, a taxpayer must still adhere to established legal channels to challenge it. Narragansett Electric Company v. Minardi, 21 A.3d 274 (R.I. 2011), confirms that the Uniform Declaratory Judgments Act cannot be used to circumvent the specific procedures and time limits outlined in the tax statutes. Bluedog's failure to file within the statutory period requires dismissal.

**C. Failure to Exhaust Administrative Remedies**

Bluedog also argues that it was not required to exhaust administrative remedies because the tax was illegal. Again, this premise fails because the tax was not illegal. Since the City had jurisdiction over the Providence parcel, Bluedog was required to follow the exclusive procedures for tax-abatement relief set forth in G.L. 1956 § 44-5-26. This statute requires a taxpayer to first appeal to the board of tax assessment review before seeking judicial intervention.

This Court has consistently held that the legislature did not intend the Uniform Declaratory Judgments Act to take the place of a taxpayer's suit or to bypass the statutory administrative process. Pascale v. Capaldi, 95 R.I. 513, 514, 188 A.2d 378, 379 (1963). Because Bluedog failed to appeal the assessment to the board of tax assessment review, it failed to satisfy a mandatory condition precedent to maintaining this action. The Superior Court correctly determined that Bluedog could not use the declaratory judgment action to sidestep these requirements.

**D. Mootness of Injunctive Relief**

Finally, Count II of Bluedog's complaint seeks injunctive relief against the sale of the property. The defense correctly notes that the tax sale was completed on July 1, 2015, with the collector's deed recorded on July 9, 2015. Bluedog filed its complaint nearly a year later. Injunctive relief is designed to prevent imminent or ongoing harm, not to undo a completed transaction. As stated in Narragansett Electric Company v. Minardi, 21 A.3d 274 (R.I. 2011), there must be a present threat of harm to warrant such relief. Because the sale had already occurred, there was nothing for the Superior Court to enjoin, and this claim was properly dismissed as moot.

**IV. CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the arguments presented by the City demonstrate that Bluedog's complaint fails to state a claim upon which relief can be granted. The complaint admits the City's jurisdiction over the taxed parcel, characterizing the dispute as an overassessment rather than an illegal tax. Consequently, Bluedog was required to exhaust administrative remedies and file within the three-month statute of limitations—requirements it failed to meet. Furthermore, the request for injunctive relief was moot due to the completion of the tax sale. The judgment of the Superior Court granting the motion to dismiss is affirmed.

**PER CURIAM.**

This matter came before the Court for consideration of the closing arguments submitted by the parties. The plaintiff, Bluedog Capital Partners, LLC (Bluedog), appeals from a judgment of the Superior Court granting the motion to dismiss filed by the defendants, John A. Murphy, David Quinn, and Michael Pearis (collectively, the City). For the reasons set forth below, we affirm the judgment of the Superior Court.

**I. Standard of Review**

When reviewing a trial justice's decision on a motion to dismiss for failure to state a claim, this Court applies the same standard as the trial justice. Gannon v. City of Pawtucket, 200 A.3d 1074, 1077 (R.I. 2019). We are confined to the four corners of the complaint and must assume all allegations are true, resolving any doubts in the plaintiff's favor. Id. A motion to dismiss may be granted only if it appears beyond a reasonable doubt that a plaintiff would not be entitled to relief under any conceivable set of facts. Id. Additionally, a party may raise a statute-of-limitations defense by way of a motion to dismiss, provided the alleged timing defect appears on the face of the complaint. Barrette v. Yakavonis, 966 A.2d 1231, 1234 (R.I. 2009).

**II. Analysis**

On appeal, Bluedog argues that the tax assessment at issue was illegal, thereby exempting it from the standard procedural requirements for challenging tax assessments. Specifically, Bluedog contends that because the property at issue encompasses a lot located in Cranston, the City's assessment of the "Providence parcel" was an act outside its jurisdiction. We are not persuaded.

**A. The Complaint Alleges an Overassessment, Not an Illegal Tax**

The threshold issue is whether Bluedog has stated a claim for an "illegal tax" sufficient to bypass the statutory procedural framework. In CIC-Newport Associates v. Stein, 121 R.I. 844, 403 A.2d 658 (1979), this Court distinguished between an illegal tax, where the taxing authority acts without jurisdiction, and an overassessment, where the authority has jurisdiction but sets an excessive value. Id. at 849-50, 403 A.2d at 661-62.

Here, Bluedog's complaint explicitly admits that the "Providence parcel" is "located within the city" of Providence. The complaint further alleges that the City "noticed a tax sale for the Providence parcel" and that Bluedog sought a declaration regarding the assessment of that specific parcel. By its own pleading, Bluedog concedes that the City of Providence had territorial jurisdiction over the parcel it assessed and taxed. Consequently, this is not a case where the City attempted to tax property outside its borders. Rather, the dispute centers on the assessed value of $102,200, which constitutes a classic overassessment controversy. Bluedog cannot transform a valuation dispute into a jurisdictional challenge by pointing to a separate, non-taxed parcel in Cranston. Therefore, the procedural requirements applicable to overassessment disputes govern this case.

**B. Statute of Limitations**

Because Bluedog's claim is properly characterized as an overassessment challenge, it is subject to the statute of limitations set forth in G.L. 1956 § 44-5-27. That section provides that any person aggrieved by a tax assessment may bring a bill in equity "within three (3) months next after the date of the tax warrant."

The face of the complaint reveals that the tax sale occurred on July 1, 2015, and the collector's deed was recorded on July 9, 2015. Bluedog did not file its complaint until June 2, 2016—nearly eleven months after the tax sale. This delay is clearly outside the three-month limitations period. As held in Barrette v. Yakavonis, 966 A.2d 1231, 1234 (R.I. 2009), a statute-of-limitations defense may be raised via motion to dismiss when the timing defect appears on the face of the complaint. Here, the defect is apparent and fatal to Bluedog's claim.

Bluedog's reliance on the theory that the tax is "void ab initio" is unavailing. Even if a tax were void, a taxpayer must still adhere to established legal channels to challenge it. Narragansett Electric Company v. Minardi, 21 A.3d 274 (R.I. 2011), confirms that the Uniform Declaratory Judgments Act cannot be used to circumvent the specific procedures and time limits outlined in the tax statutes. Bluedog's failure to file within the statutory period requires dismissal.

**C. Failure to Exhaust Administrative Remedies**

Bluedog also argues that it was not required to exhaust administrative remedies because the tax was illegal. Again, this premise fails because the tax was not illegal. Since the City had jurisdiction over the Providence parcel, Bluedog was required to follow the exclusive procedures for tax-abatement relief set forth in G.L. 1956 § 44-5-26. This statute requires a taxpayer to first appeal to the board of tax assessment review before seeking judicial intervention.

This Court has consistently held that the legislature did not intend the Uniform Declaratory Judgments Act to take the place of a taxpayer's suit or to bypass the statutory administrative process. Pascale v. Capaldi, 95 R.I. 513, 514, 188 A.2d 378, 379 (1963). Because Bluedog failed to appeal the assessment to the board of tax assessment review, it failed to satisfy a mandatory condition precedent to maintaining this action. The Superior Court correctly determined that Bluedog could not use the declaratory judgment action to sidestep these requirements.

**D. Mootness of Injunctive Relief**

Finally, Count II of Bluedog's complaint seeks injunctive relief against the sale of the property. The defense correctly notes that the tax sale was completed on July 1, 2015, with the collector's deed recorded on July 9, 2015. Bluedog filed its complaint nearly a year later. Injunctive relief is designed to prevent imminent or ongoing harm, not to undo a completed transaction. As stated in Narragansett Electric Company v. Minardi, 21 A.3d 274 (R.I. 2011), there must be a present threat of harm to warrant such relief. Because the sale had already occurred, there was nothing for the Superior Court to enjoin, and this claim was properly dismissed as moot.

**III. Conclusion**

For the foregoing reasons, the arguments presented by the City demonstrate that Bluedog's complaint fails to state a claim upon which relief can be granted. The complaint admits the City's jurisdiction over the taxed parcel, characterizing the dispute as an overassessment rather than an illegal tax. Consequently, Bluedog was required to exhaust administrative remedies and file within the three-month statute of limitations—requirements it failed to meet. Furthermore, the request for injunctive relief was moot due to the completion of the tax sale. The judgment of the Superior Court granting the motion to dismiss is affirmed.

**SO ORDERED.**
