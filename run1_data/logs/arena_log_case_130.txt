**FINAL RULING**

This matter comes before the court on appeal from the judgment of the trial court following a jury verdict in favor of the plaintiff, Rita A. Wallace. The defendants, Hector L. Haddock and Subaru Leasing Corporation, appeal the trial court's order granting an additur and immediately entering judgment without offering the defendants the statutory alternative of a new trial. The court has carefully considered the closing arguments of both the prosecution and the defense.

The court reaffirms its conclusion that the trial court committed reversible error. The undisputed facts establish that on July 12, 2002, the trial court granted the plaintiff's motion for an additur in the amount of $15,000 and "immediately rendered judgment for the plaintiff, including the additur." There is no indication that the court offered the defendants the choice between accepting this increased award or electing a new trial.

The legal obligations of the trial court are governed by General Statutes §§ 52-216a and 52-228b. As held in *D'Amico v. Hayward*, 171 Conn. 1, 368 A.2d 18 (1976), "the statute requires that when the court orders an additur, it must offer the defendant the alternative of a new trial if the additur is not accepted. Failure to do so is reversible error." This principle was reaffirmed in *Steele v. Searle*, 216 Conn. 13, 579 A.2d 145 (1990), where the court emphasized that the trial court's duty is mandatory and non-discretionary. The Appellate Court, in *Petrone v. Fernandez*, 12 Conn. App. 524, 532 A.2d 503 (1987), reversed a judgment under similar circumstances, noting that the statutory requirement is a prerequisite to the validity of any additur order. The trial court's action in this case directly contravened these mandates.

The defense acknowledges this procedural error but requests that this court address the substantive merits of the additur to prevent a "futile remand." Specifically, the defense asks the court to hold that the trial court abused its discretion in granting the additur because the record does not support a finding that the jury's award "shocks the conscience."

The court declines the invitation to rule on the substantive adequacy of the damages at this time. In *March v. Perel*, 156 Conn. 373, 242 A.2d 333 (1968), the Supreme Court held that an additur is warranted only where the verdict is "so inadequate as to shock the conscience of the court" or "so low that the court can infer from it the influence of improper motives, partiality, or prejudice on the part of the jury." 156 Conn. 373, 380, 242 A.2d 333, 337 (1968).

The record provided on appeal is limited to the fact that the plaintiff was struck from behind at a busy intersection and that the jury awarded $1,715 in noneconomic damages. The record does not contain a detailed description of the plaintiff's injuries, the medical evidence presented, or other factors necessary for this court to independently determine whether the award "shocks the conscience." Without a sufficient factual basis to evaluate the trial court's discretionary decision, the court cannot conclude that the trial court abused its discretion in granting the additur, nor can it conclude that the additur was improper. As the prosecution correctly argues, appellate review is confined to the record before it, and the current record is insufficient to support a substantive ruling on the adequacy of the damages.

While the defense expresses concern that a remand without substantive guidance will result in a "circular process" of appeals, this concern does not justify the appellate court speculating on the merits of the additur absent a factual record. On remand, if the trial court again determines that an additur is appropriate, it must comply with the statutory mandates of General Statutes §§ 52-216a and 52-228b by offering the defendants the alternative of a new trial. Furthermore, if the trial court grants an additur, it must base its decision on specific findings of fact supported by the evidence to withstand future appellate scrutiny under the *March v. Perel* standard.

Therefore, the judgment of the trial court is reversed solely on the ground that the court failed to offer the defendants the alternative of a new trial as required by General Statutes §§ 52-216a and 52-228b. The case is remanded for further proceedings consistent with this opinion.

IT IS SO ORDERED.
