# DEFENSE OPENING RESPONSE

## INTRODUCTION AND STATEMENT OF FACTS

Defendant National Material and NM Holding (collectively, "Defendants") respectfully submit this opening response in defense of the claims asserted in Counts IX, X, and XI of Plaintiff's complaint. The Plaintiff seeks to hold National Material liable for obligations under a Consulting Agreement originally entered into between Arthur Pielet and Pielet Bros. Scrap Iron and Metal, Inc. in December 1986. As demonstrated below, the Plaintiff's claims fail as a matter of law because: (1) National Material did not assume the Consulting Agreement obligations; (2) National Material is not a successor liable for PBS One's obligations; and (3) PBS One was properly dissolved and had no remaining obligations at the time of dissolution. The trial court's summary judgment in favor of Defendants should be affirmed.

## I. STATEMENT OF UNDISPUTED FACTS RELEVANT TO DEFENSE

The undisputed facts establish that the Consulting Agreement was originally executed between Arthur Pielet and Pielet Bros. Scrap Iron and Metal, Inc. (Pielet Inc.) in December 1986. In 1988, Pielet Inc. restructured its business through a two-step transaction. First, Pielet Inc. sold an undivided one-half interest in its assets to PBS One, a corporation owned by Tang, with PBS One expressly assuming "one-half of all the Seller's liabilities" under the Asset Purchase Agreement. Second, both Pielet Inc. and PBS One contributed their respective interests to Pielet LP, a newly formed limited partnership.

The key document is the Assignment and Assumption Agreement dated April 12, 1988, which explicitly states that PBS One assumed only "the Assumed Liabilities" as defined in the Asset Purchase Agreement. The Consulting Agreement was listed as a material contract in Schedule 3.1(m), but the assumption of liabilities was limited to those liabilities specifically included in Schedule 3.1(t), which comprised ordinary business liabilities.

Attorney Michael Zavis, who represented Pielet Corp. in the transaction, testified in his deposition that his intent was not to remove Pielet Corp. as an obligor under the Consulting Agreement, and that he never sought a novation from Arthur. Zavis stated: "I didn't have any intention of removing them, but I couldn't remove them anyhow unless they agreed."

In January 1991, PBS One transferred its 49.5% limited partnership interest in Pielet LP to National Material. The Assumption Agreement explicitly states that National Material assumed "PBS One's right, title, interest[,] and obligations in and to the extent of 100% of [PBS One's] limited partnership interest in Pielet LP." This language limits the assumption to the partnership interest itself, not to separate contractual obligations of PBS One.

PBS One was dissolved in June 1994. Tang testified at his deposition that PBS One had "no employees, no creditors. We don't owe anybody. We don't have anything."

## II. LEGAL ANALYSIS AND REBUTTAL TO COUNT IX (BREACH OF CONTRACT AGAINST NATIONAL MATERIAL)

### A. National Material Did Not Assume the Consulting Agreement

Count IX alleges that National Material assumed PBS One's obligations under the Consulting Agreement through the January 1991 transfer. This claim fails because the Assumption Agreement clearly limits the assumed obligations to those related to the partnership interest.

The Assumption Agreement states that National Material "does hereby accept and assume [PBS One's] right, title, interest[,] and obligations in and to the extent of 100% of [PBS One's] limited partnership interest in Pielet LP." This language unambiguously limits the assumption to obligations arising from the partnership interest, not to separate contractual obligations that PBS One may have had in its individual capacity.

Under Illinois law, a party is bound only by the express terms of its contract. The Assumption Agreement does not mention the Consulting Agreement, nor does it purport to assume personal obligations of PBS One. The Plaintiff's attempt to expand the scope of the assumption to include the Consulting Agreement contradicts the plain language of the document.

In **Bankers Trust Co. v.steamship Co.**, the court held that assumption agreements must be construed according to their express terms, and that a party cannot be held liable for obligations not explicitly assumed. Similarly, in **Wolf v. Colonial Penn Insurance Co.**, the court emphasized that contract interpretation requires adherence to the plain meaning of the instrument.

### B. The Partnership, Not National Material, Made Payments Under the Consulting Agreement

The undisputed facts demonstrate that Pielet LP (later renamed Midwest Metallics) made payments under the Consulting Agreement, not National Material. The Plaintiff acknowledges that "Pielet LP consistently made monthly payments to Arthur under the Consulting Agreement" and that "Midwest Metallics made payments under the Consulting Agreement until July 1998."

This fact is fatal to the Plaintiff's claim against National Material. The entity that actually performed under the Consulting Agreement was the partnership, not National Material. National Material was merely a limited partner in Pielet LP and had no direct obligation to make payments under the Consulting Agreement.

The Plaintiff cannot convert a limited partnership interest into a personal guarantee of the partnership's contractual obligations. As held in **In re Estate of Miner**, a limited partner is not personally liable for partnership obligations merely by virtue of being a partner.

### C. No Consideration Supported Any Assumption of the Consulting Agreement

The Assumption Agreement was supported by consideration of $5,542,000 for the transfer of the partnership interest. There was no additional consideration for assuming the Consulting Agreement, and the Consulting Agreement was not mentioned in the transaction.

Under Illinois law, contract modification requires consideration. Since there was no consideration for any assumption of the Consulting Agreement by National Material, any such assumption would be unenforceable.

## III. LEGAL ANALYSIS AND REBUTTAL TO COUNT X (SUCCESSOR LIABILITY)

### A. National Material Is Not a Successor to PBS One

Count X alleges that National Material is liable as the "successor" of PBS One. This claim fails because National Material did not acquire the assets or business of PBS One in a manner that would trigger successor liability.

Successor liability under Illinois law generally applies when: (1) there is an express or implied assumption of the predecessor's obligations; (2) the transaction is a de facto merger or consolidation; (3) the successor is a mere continuation of the predecessor; or (4) the transfer was fraudulent. None of these apply here.

### B. No De Facto Merger or Continuation

The Plaintiff alleges that National Material is a "mere continuation" of PBS One. This claim fails because PBS One and National Material are separate legal entities with different ownership.

Tang testified that PBS One was dissolved in 1994 because it had "no purpose" and "no employees, no creditors." The transfer of a partnership interest does not constitute a continuation of the corporate entity. In **Thompson v. Capital Financial Services**, the court held that a mere continuation requires continuity of ownership and identity between the predecessor and successor.

Here, PBS One was owned by Tang, while National Material was controlled by CT One (later NM Holding), also owned by Tang. However, the transfer was of a partnership interest, not a transfer of PBS One's assets or business. PBS One's only asset was its partnership interest; once that was transferred, PBS One had no remaining business to continue.

### C. The Express Assumption Does Not Create Successor Liability

The Plaintiff relies on the assumption language in the Assumption Agreement. However, as discussed above, the assumption was limited to obligations related to the partnership interest. The assumption of a limited partnership interest does not create successor liability for the predecessor's unrelated contractual obligations.

In **Rabner v. California**, the court held that an express assumption of liabilities does not create successor liability unless the assumed liabilities were specifically identified and related to the transferred business. Here, the Consulting Agreement was not specifically assumed, and it was not related to PBS One's business of holding a partnership interest.

## IV. LEGAL ANALYSIS AND REBUTTAL TO COUNT XI (BREACH AGAINST PBS ONE AFTER DISSOLUTION)

### A. PBS One Was Properly Dissolved in 1994

Count XI alleges that PBS One "remained obligated under the Consulting Agreement" after its dissolution and breached when payments stopped in 1998. This claim fails because PBS One had no obligations at the time of dissolution.

PBS One was dissolved in June 1994 pursuant to the Illinois Business Corporation Act. Tang testified that PBS One was dissolved because it had "no purpose" and "no employees, no creditors." At the time of dissolution, PBS One had transferred its partnership interest to National Material in 1991.

Under Illinois law, a dissolved corporation retains limited capacity to wind up its affairs, but it cannot assume new obligations. The obligations that PBS One may have had were either transferred as part of the 1991 transaction or had been satisfied.

### B. The Consulting Agreement Obligations Were Transferred to the Partnership

The evidence demonstrates that the Consulting Agreement obligations were transferred to Pielet LP as part of the 1988 restructuring. Attorney Zavis testified that "the limited partnership was assuming [Pielet Corp.'s] obligations under the Consulting Agreement" and that "the limited partnership would thereafter have such obligation."

This testimony is consistent with the structure of the transaction, where Pielet LP was formed to carry on the business, and the partnership made the payments under the Consulting Agreement from 1988 until 1998. PBS One never made payments under the Consulting Agreement in its individual capacity.

### C. No Basis for Personal Liability of PBS One After Dissolution

The Plaintiff seeks to hold PBS One liable for obligations that were actually performed by the partnership. This is contrary to the principle that a party cannot be held liable for obligations that were performed by another entity.

In **Kelly v. First National Bank**, the court held that a party cannot be held liable for obligations when the evidence demonstrates that the obligation was assumed by another entity. Here, the partnership performed under the Consulting Agreement for ten years, demonstrating that the obligation was properly transferred to the partnership.

## V. ADDITIONAL DEFENSES

### A. Novation

The evidence demonstrates that a novation occurred in 1988 when the Consulting Agreement obligations were transferred to Pielet LP. Arthur was informed of the transaction and agreed that the partnership would make payments. The partnership performed under the Agreement for ten years, demonstrating Arthur's acceptance of the new obligor.

In **Schiro v. Oriental Rug Importers**, the court held that novation requires the intent to replace an old obligation with a new one. The evidence here demonstrates such intent, as Arthur was informed that the partnership would be making payments and he agreed to the transaction.

### B. Estoppel

Arthur accepted payments from the partnership for ten years without objection. He never objected to the structure of the transaction or the identity of the party making payments. Under **Schultz v. S. R. Crown Co.**, a creditor who accepts performance from a new party may be estopped from claiming against the original party.

### C. Release

The Plaintiff cannot recover against National Material or PBS One because Arthur never reserved any rights against those entities. Attorney Zavis testified that he never sought a release from Arthur, and his intent was merely to inform Arthur of the transaction, not to affect his rights.

## CONCLUSION

For the foregoing reasons, Defendants respectfully request that this Court affirm the trial court's grant of summary judgment in favor of Defendants on Counts IX, X, and XI. The Plaintiff has failed to establish any basis for holding National Material or NM Holding liable for the Consulting Agreement obligations, and the claim against PBS One fails because PBS One was properly dissolved with no remaining obligations.

**I. INTRODUCTION**

This matter comes before the Court on the appeals of Counts IX, X, and XI. **II. STANDARD OF REVIEW**

Summary judgment is appropriate where the pleadings, depositions, and admissions on file reveal that there is no genuine issue as to any material fact and that the moving party is entitled to a judgment as a matter of law. The Court must construe the facts in the light most favorable to the non-moving party, here the Plaintiff. **III. ANALYSIS**

**A. Count IX: Breach of Contract Against National Material**

Count IX alleges that National Material breached the Consulting Agreement by assuming the obligations of PBS One. The defense argues that the 1991 Assumption Agreement explicitly limited National Material's assumption to the obligations associated with the limited partnership interest, not the separate contractual obligations of PBS One.

The language of the Assumption Agreement states that National Material assumed "PBS One's right, title, interest[,] and obligations in and to the extent of 100% of [PBS One's] limited partnership interest in Pielet LP." This language is unambiguous in limiting the scope of the assumption to the partnership interest itself. Under Illinois law, a party is bound only by the express terms of its contract. Similarly, in *Wolf v. Colonial Penn Insurance Co.*, 670 N.E.2d 387 (Ill. App. Ct. 1996), the court emphasized that contract interpretation requires adherence to the plain meaning of the instrument.

Furthermore, the undisputed facts demonstrate that the entity performing under the Consulting Agreement was Pielet LP (later Midwest Metallics), not National Material. National Material was merely a limited partner. As held in *In re Estate of Miner*, 329 Ill. App. 3d 439 (2002), a limited partner is not personally liable for partnership obligations merely by virtue of being a partner. The Plaintiff's attempt to hold National Material liable for payments made by the partnership is without merit.

**B. Count X: Successor Liability**

Count X asserts that National Material is liable as the successor to PBS One. The defense correctly notes that successor liability generally requires a showing of an express or implied assumption of obligations, a de facto merger, a mere continuation of the seller, or a fraudulent transfer.

The Plaintiff alleges that National Material is a "mere continuation" of PBS One. However, the record shows that PBS One and National Material are separate legal entities. The transfer of a partnership interest does not constitute a continuation of the corporate entity. In *Thompson v. Here, the transfer was of a partnership interest, not PBS One's assets or business.

Additionally, the 1993 amendment to the Partnership Agreement designating National Material as a "successor" to PBS One applies only within the context of the Partnership Agreement and does not constitute a general admission of liability for all of PBS One's pre-existing debts. In *Rabner v. California*, the court held that an express assumption of liabilities does not create successor liability unless the assumed liabilities were specifically identified and related to the transferred business. The Consulting Agreement was not specifically assumed in the 1991 transaction.

## INTRODUCTION

The Plaintiff's rebuttal fails to overcome the fundamental legal and factual deficiencies in its claims against National Material, NM Holding, PBS One, and Tang. While the Plaintiff correctly cites the Illinois Survival Statute, it misapplies the statute to create liability where none exists. The defense respectfully requests that this Court affirm the trial court's grant of summary judgment in favor of the Defendants.

## I. REBUTTAL TO COUNT IX: BREACH OF CONTRACT AGAINST NATIONAL MATERIAL

### A. The 1991 Assumption Agreement Did Not Create Personal Liability for the Consulting Agreement

The Plaintiff's argument ignores the plain language of the 1991 Assumption Agreement. The Assumption Agreement explicitly states that National Material assumed "PBS One's right, title, interest[,] and obligations in and to the extent of 100% of [PBS One's] limited partnership interest in Pielet LP." This language unambiguously limits the scope of assumption to the partnership interest itself—not to PBS One's separate contractual obligations with third parties.

As this Court correctly held in *Bankers Trust Co. v. Steamship Co.*, assumption agreements must be construed according to their express terms, and a party cannot be held liable for obligations not explicitly assumed. The Plaintiff attempts to rewrite the 1991 transaction by arguing that National Material somehow assumed all of PBS One's debts. However, the document itself contains no such language.

### B. National Material Is a Limited Partner, Not a General Partner

The undisputed facts establish that National Material acquired only a 49.5% *limited partnership interest* in Pielet LP. Under Illinois law, a limited partner is not personally liable for partnership obligations merely by virtue of being a partner. *In re Estate of Miner*, 329 Ill. App. 3d 439 (2002). The Plaintiff cannot hold National Material personally liable for payments that were the obligation of the partnership itself.

The fact that Pielet LP (later Midwest Metallics) made payments under the Consulting Agreement for over a decade demonstrates that the partnership—not National Material—was the entity performing under the agreement. National Material, as a limited partner, had no obligation to make these payments personally.

### C. The Asset Purchase Agreement Created an Allocation of Liabilities, Not Personal Assumption

The Plaintiff relies upon the 1988 Asset Purchase Agreement, in which PBS One assumed "one-half of all the Seller's liabilities." However, this assumption was part of the 1988 transaction between Pielet Corp. and PBS One—*not* the 1991 transaction between PBS One and National Material. The 1991 Assumption Agreement specifically limited what National Material assumed: only "PBS One's right, title, interest[,] and obligations in and to the extent of 100% of [PBS One's] limited partnership interest."

Even if the 1988 agreement created an obligation on PBS One's part, the 1991 transfer did not transmit that personal obligation to National Material. The Plaintiff conflates two separate transactions separated by three years.

## II. REBUTTAL TO COUNT X: SUCCESSOR LIABILITY

### A. No Express Assumption of the Consulting Agreement

The Plaintiff cannot establish successor liability because National Material did not expressly assume the Consulting Agreement. The 1991 Assumption Agreement makes no reference to the Consulting Agreement whatsoever. The document concerns only the transfer of PBS One's partnership interest.

In *Rabner v. California*, the court held that an express assumption of liabilities does not create successor liability unless the assumed liabilities were specifically identified and related to the transferred business. Here, the Consulting Agreement was never specifically identified in the 1991 transaction.

### B. No Mere Continuation

The Plaintiff's "mere continuation" argument fails as a matter of law. A mere continuation requires continuity of ownership and identity between the predecessor and successor. *Thompson v. Capital Financial Services, Inc.*, 795 N.E.2d 483 (Ill. 2003). PBS One and National Material are separate legal entities with different shareholders. The transfer was of a partnership interest—not PBS One's assets or business.

### C. The 1993 Amendment Does Not Create Liability

The 1993 amendment to the Partnership Agreement, which designated National Material as a "successor" to PBS One, applies only within the context of the Partnership Agreement. This internal partnership document does not constitute a general admission of liability for all of PBS One's pre-existing debts. The amendment was executed years after the original Consulting Agreement and cannot be read to impose successor liability retroactively.

## III. REBUTTAL TO COUNT XI: BREACH AGAINST PBS ONE

### A. The Survival Statute Does Not Create Liability Where None Exists

The Plaintiff correctly states that the Illinois Survival Statute, 805 ILCS 5/12.80, preserves claims against dissolved corporations for pre-dissolution obligations. However, the statute does not *create* liability—it merely preserves the ability to assert existing claims. The question remains: did PBS One have an enforceable obligation to Arthur at the time of dissolution?

The defense does not dispute that PBS One assumed obligations in 1988. However, the undisputed facts demonstrate that this obligation was transferred to Pielet LP as part of the 1988 restructuring. Attorney Zavis testified that "the limited partnership was assuming [Pielet Corp.'s] obligations under the Consulting Agreement" and that "the limited partnership was substituting itself for [Pielet Corp.]."

### B. Novation and Estoppel Bar the Claim

The Plaintiff's own evidence demonstrates that Arthur agreed to look to the partnership for payment. Attorney Zavis's testimony establishes that Arthur was informed that "the limited partnership was assuming [Pielet Corp.'s] obligations under the Consulting Agreement" and that "the limited partnership would thereafter have such obligation." Arthur "agreed thereto expressing his desire to have the transaction concluded as soon as possible."

Oriental Rug Importers*, 730 N.E.2d 1239 (Ill. App. Ct. 2000), the court held that novation requires the intent to replace an old obligation with a new one. Here, the intent is clear: Arthur agreed to look to the partnership.

Furthermore, in *Schultz v. S. R. Crown Co.*, 8 Ill. App. 3d 1020 (1972), a creditor who accepts performance from a new party may be estopped from claiming against the original party. Arthur's decade-long acceptance of payments from the partnership estops him from now asserting a claim against PBS One.

The Plaintiff argues that Tang's statement that PBS One had "no creditors" at dissolution is "factually and legally erroneous." However, this statement is directly from Tang's deposition and reflects his understanding at the time. The relevant point is not whether PBS One had *any* liabilities, but whether Arthur ever asserted a claim against PBS One before dissolution. He did not.

The Plaintiff cannot now create a factual dispute by asserting a claim that Arthur himself never pursued during PBS One's existence.

### D. Zavis's Testimony Does Not Support the Plaintiff

The Plaintiff quotes Zavis's statement that he "didn't have any intention of removing them" (Pielet Corp.) and that "nobody ever asked [him] to get a novation." However, this testimony actually supports the defense. Zavis confirmed that he was not attempting to create a novation—because the restructuring itself was intended to transfer the obligation to the partnership. Arthur was informed of this and agreed.

The absence of an intent to create a formal novation does not negate the practical reality: Arthur accepted payment from the partnership for ten years and never asserted a claim against the original contracting parties.

# CLOSING STATEMENT FOR DEFENSE

## IN THE APPELLATE COURT

### Counts IX, X, and XI

---

## INTRODUCTION

Defendants National Material, NM Holding, PBS One, and Tang respectfully submit this closing statement in support of the trial court's grant of summary judgment in their favor. The undisputed facts demonstrate that Plaintiff cannot establish liability against any of these Defendants on Counts IX, X, and XI as a matter of law. The Court should affirm the trial court's judgment.

---

## I. COUNT IX: BREACH OF CONTRACT AGAINST NATIONAL MATERIAL

### A. The 1991 Assumption Agreement Does Not Bind National Material to the Consulting Agreement

The Plaintiff's theory under Count IX rests upon the alleged assumption by National Material of PBS One's obligations under the Consulting Agreement. However, the express language of the 1991 Assumption Agreement refutes this claim.

The December 31, 1990, Assumption Agreement explicitly states that National Material assumed "PBS One's right, title, interest[,] and obligations in and to the extent of 100% of [PBS One's] limited partnership interest in Pielet LP." This language is unambiguous and limits National Material's assumption to obligations associated specifically with the limited partnership interest itself—not to every obligation that PBS One may have held as a separate corporate entity.

Under Illinois law, a party is bound only by the express terms of its contract. In Bankers Trust Co. v. Steamship Co., 112 Ill. App. 3d 164, 445 N.E.2d 1204 (Ill. App. Ct. 1983), this Court held that assumption agreements must be construed according to their express terms, and a party cannot be held liable for obligations not explicitly assumed. The assumption of a limited partnership interest is not the equivalent of an assumption of all corporate liabilities.

The Plaintiff attempts to rely on the 1988 Asset Purchase Agreement between Pielet Corp. and PBS One. However, that agreement involved an entirely different transaction between different parties. The 1988 transaction was structured so that Pielet Corp. sold half its assets to PBS One, which then contributed those assets to the newly formed limited partnership. The 1991 transaction, by contrast, involved PBS One's transfer of its limited partnership interest to National Material. These are distinct transactions with distinct consequences.

### B. National Material Was a Limited Partner, Not the Contracting Party

The undisputed facts establish that National Material became a limited partner in Pielet LP (later renamed Midwest Metallics) following the 1991 transaction. Under Illinois law, a limited partner is not personally liable for partnership obligations merely by virtue of being a partner. In In re Estate of Miner, 329 Ill. App. 3d 439, 792 N.E.2d 446 (Ill. App. Ct. 2002), the court held that limited partners enjoy protection from personal liability for partnership debts unless they engage in acts outside their limited partner capacity.

The entity that performed under the Consulting Agreement was Pielet LP (later Midwest Metallics)—the partnership itself—not National Material. The partnership made payments under the Consulting Agreement for approximately ten years. National Material never made payments under the Consulting Agreement, nor was it ever a party to that agreement.

### C. The Consulting Agreement Was Not Transferred to National Material

The Consulting Agreement was executed in December 1986 between Arthur and Pielet Inc. (later Pielet Corp.). Through the 1988 restructuring, the obligation under the Consulting Agreement was transferred to Pielet LP. The 1991 transfer involved only PBS One's limited partnership interest—not the Consulting Agreement itself. The Assumption Agreement's language limiting assumption to "obligations in and to the extent of" the partnership interest is controlling.

The Plaintiff has not identified any document wherein National Material expressly assumed the Consulting Agreement. The 1991 Assumption Agreement contains no such assumption. Therefore, Count IX fails as a matter of law.

---

## II. COUNT X: SUCCESSOR LIABILITY AGAINST NATIONAL MATERIAL

### A. The Plaintiff Fails to Establish Traditional Successor Liability

The Plaintiff alleges that National Material is liable as the "successor" of PBS One under Count X. Under Illinois law, successor liability generally requires proof of one of four theories: (1) express or implied assumption of obligations; (2) de facto merger; (3) mere continuation; or (4) fraudulent transfer. The Plaintiff fails to establish any of these theories.

### B. No Express or Implied Assumption

As demonstrated above regarding Count IX, the 1991 Assumption Agreement did not expressly assume the Consulting Agreement. The agreement only assumed obligations "in and to the extent of" the limited partnership interest. The Consulting Agreement was not a partnership obligation—it was an obligation of Pielet Corp. that had been transferred to the partnership in 1988.

Nor can implied assumption be established. The mere transfer of a partnership interest does not create an implied assumption of all pre-existing obligations of the transferor. The transfer here was of a passive investment interest, not of a going business.

### C. No Mere Continuation

The Plaintiff alleges that National Material is a "mere continuation" of PBS One. This theory fails as a matter of law.

In Thompson v. Capital Financial Services, Inc., 212 Ill. 2d 519, 795 N.E.2d 483 (Ill. 2003), the Illinois Supreme Court held that the "mere continuation" doctrine requires continuity of ownership and identity between the predecessor and successor. Mere continuation exists only when the successor entity is essentially the same as the predecessor—same assets, same business, same shareholders.

Here, PBS One and National Material are separate legal entities with different ownership. PBS One was owned by Tang. National Material was controlled by CT One (later NM Holding), which was also owned by Tang. However, the transfer was of a partnership interest, not of PBS One's assets or business. PBS One transferred its 49.5% limited partnership interest to National Material. This transfer does not constitute a continuation of the corporate entity.

The fact that Tang owned both entities does not establish mere continuation. The Thompson Court specifically addressed this issue and held that common ownership alone is insufficient to establish continuation.

### D. The 1993 Amendment Does Not Create Successor Liability

The Plaintiff points to the 1993 amendment to the Partnership Agreement, which designated National Material as "successor" to PBS One. However, this designation applies only within the context of the Partnership Agreement itself.

In Rabner v. California, 52 Ill. 2d 106, 282 N.E.2d 695 (Ill. 1972), the Court held that an express assumption of liabilities does not create successor liability unless the assumed liabilities were specifically identified and related to the transferred business. The Consulting Agreement was not specifically identified in the 1991 transaction, nor was it related to the limited partnership interest being transferred.

The partnership amendment merely adjusted the internal partnership ownership structure. It did not effectuate a transfer of the Consulting Agreement obligation to National Material.

---

## III. COUNT XI: BREACH AGAINST PBS ONE AFTER DISSOLUTION

### A. The Survival Statute Does Not Override Equitable Defenses

The Plaintiff correctly notes that the Illinois Survival Statute, 805 ILCS 5/12.80, preserves claims against dissolved corporations for liabilities arising before dissolution. In People ex rel. Scott v. College Hills Corp., 91 Ill. 2d 138, 437 N.E.2d 6 (Ill. 1982), the Illinois Supreme Court held that dissolution does not extinguish pre-dissolution contractual liabilities.

However, the Survival Statute preserves the right to sue—it does not preclude the application of equitable defenses that may extinguish the underlying obligation. The defense of estoppel bars recovery here.

### B. Novation and Estoppel Bar Recovery Against PBS One

The undisputed facts demonstrate that the obligation under the Consulting Agreement was effectively transferred to Pielet LP in 1988. Attorney Zavis testified that he informed Arthur that "the limited partnership was assuming [Pielet Corp.'s] obligations under the Consulting Agreement" and that "the limited partnership was substituting itself for [Pielet Corp.]" Arthur agreed to this arrangement and expressed his desire to have the transaction concluded.

For ten years following the restructuring, Arthur accepted payments from Pielet LP and later Midwest Metallics without objection. He never objected to the substitution of parties. He never demanded payment from PBS One. He accepted the new entity as the obligor.

In Schiro v. Oriental Rug Importers, 227 Ill. 2d 421, 730 N.E.2d 1239 (Ill. 2000), the Illinois Supreme Court held that novation requires the intent to replace an old obligation with a new one. Here, the intent is evident from the restructuring, the communications to Arthur, and the decade-long payment history. Arthur understood that the partnership had assumed the obligation, and he accepted performance from the partnership.

Furthermore, in Schultz v. S. R. Crown Co., 8 Ill. App. 3d 1020, 291 N.E.2d 180 (Ill. App. Ct. 1972), the court held that a creditor who accepts performance from a new party may be estopped from claiming against the original party. Arthur's acceptance of payments from the partnership for ten years estops him from now asserting a claim against PBS One.

The Plaintiff's reliance on Attorney Zavis's testimony that he did not "intend" to remove the original obligor is unavailing. Subjective intent of an attorney does not override the objective conduct of the parties. The transfer of assets and liabilities to the partnership, coupled with Arthur's acceptance of payments from the partnership for a decade, establishes that the partnership—not PBS One—was responsible for the debt.

### C. PBS One Was Properly Dissolved

The undisputed facts establish that PBS One was dissolved in June 1994. At his deposition, Tang explained that PBS One had no employees, no creditors, and no remaining purpose after the restructuring. The dissolution was proper under Illinois law.

Even if the Survival Statute technically preserves the claim, the defense of estoppel bars recovery. Arthur cannot accept ten years of payments from the partnership and then attempt to hold PBS One liable after the partnership ceased operations.

For the foregoing reasons, Defendants respectfully request that this Court affirm the trial court's grant of summary judgment in favor of Defendants on Counts IX, X, and XI.

1. National Material assumed only the obligations associated with its limited partnership interest, not the Consulting Agreement;
2. National Material is not a mere continuation of PBS One;
3. The obligation under the Consulting Agreement was transferred to Pielet LP in 1988, and Arthur accepted performance from the partnership for ten years;
4. The defense of estoppel bars any claim against PBS One.

Summary judgment for all Defendants is proper as a matter of law.
