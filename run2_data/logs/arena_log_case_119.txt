The district court's concern regarding Plaintiff's suitability as a class representative is a legitimate basis for denying class certification. **PRELIMINARY RULING**

**I. INTRODUCTION**

This matter comes before the Court on appeal from the district court’s dismissal of the State Defendants. The Appellant, Joki, filed a complaint seeking a declaratory judgment that Idaho’s system of funding public education is unconstitutional, alongside claims for reimbursement of fees. The district court dismissed the claims against the State Defendants, concluding that the Constitutionally Based Educational Claims Act (CBECA), Idaho Code sections 6-2201-2216, governed the action and required dismissal for failure to exhaust administrative remedies. The Appellant did not provide an argument challenging the application of the CBECA to these specific facts. Conversely, the State Defendants correctly note that this Court previously upheld the constitutionality of the CBECA in *Idaho Schools for Equal Educational Opportunity v. State*, 140 Idaho 586, 97 P.3d 453 (2004) (ISEEO IV). In *ISEEO IV*, the Court held that the statutory procedures established by the legislature must be followed. Specifically, the CBECA mandates that a claimant must first exhaust administrative remedies before pursuing judicial action. Idaho Code § 6-2205 outlines this process, requiring a claim to be filed with the State Board of Education or the appropriate political subdivision and awaiting a final decision.

The facts indicate that the Appellant filed this lawsuit directly in district court without first filing a claim with the State Board of Education or pursuing any administrative remedies. **REBUTTAL TO DEFENSE OPENING STATEMENT**

As this Court held in *Idaho Schools for Equal Educational Opportunity v. Joki’s argument that the CBECA is an “unconstitutional amendment to the Idaho Rules of Civil Procedure” is legally baseless. See *State v. Doe*, 142 Idaho 325, 330, 128 P.3d 499, 504 (2006) (clarifying that statutes and procedural rules operate in distinct legal spheres). This Court in *ISEEO IV* explicitly rejected the notion that the CBECA is unconstitutional, holding that it “does not infringe on judicial authority but rather provides a procedural framework for resolving constitutional claims consistent with separation of powers.” 140 Idaho at 594, 97 P.3d at 461. Joki’s attempt to conflate statutory law with procedural rules is without foundation.  

### II. Joki’s Claim Does Not Satisfy the Requirements of *ISEEO V*

The Appellant’s request for a declaratory judgment falls squarely within this statutory definition. The Appellant has not demonstrated that the CBECA’s administrative remedies are inadequate to address his specific allegations regarding the funding system. **B. The Appellant Failed to Exhaust Mandatory Administrative Remedies**

The Appellant filed this lawsuit directly in district court without first filing a claim with the State Board of Education. The Appellant’s complaint fails to provide the factual allegations necessary to support a finding that the funding system is "thorough" or "uniform" as those terms were interpreted in *ISEEO V*. The district court properly determined that the Appellant failed to state a claim upon which relief could be granted.

**III. CONCLUSION**

The Appellant’s arguments do not overcome the procedural and substantive barriers to his claims. The CBECA applies to his claims against the State Defendants, and he failed to exhaust the mandatory administrative remedies. Furthermore, the complaint failed to state a claim under the narrow holding of *ISEEO V*. The district court’s dismissal of the State Defendants is affirmed.

## I. THE CBECA APPLIES TO JOKI'S CLAIMS AND IS CONSTITUTIONAL

The Appellant's primary argument—that the Constitutionally Based Educational Claims Act ("CBECA") is unconstitutional as applied to his declaratory judgment claim—fails as a matter of law. The Appellant's request for a declaratory judgment that Idaho's entire education funding system is unconstitutional explicitly alleges a violation of Article IX, Section 1. It therefore falls squarely within the statutory definition.

In *Idaho Schools for Equal Educational Opportunity v. ## II. THE EXHAUSTION REQUIREMENT IS MANDATORY AND JURISDICTIONAL

The Appellant's failure to exhaust administrative remedies provides an independent and sufficient basis for dismissal. This Court held in *ISEEO IV* that the CBECA's exhaustion requirement is "mandatory and jurisdictional." 140 Idaho at 594, 97 P.3d at 461.

The Appellant confuses the purpose of administrative exhaustion. The process is designed to develop a factual record, investigate the state's funding mechanisms, and allow the political branches an opportunity to address constitutional concerns before judicial intervention. **FINAL RULING**

**I. INTRODUCTION**

This matter is before the Court upon review of the closing arguments submitted by the parties regarding the district court’s dismissal of the State Defendants. The Appellant, Joki, challenges the dismissal of his second amended complaint, which sought a declaratory judgment that Idaho’s system of funding public education is unconstitutional. The State Defendants argue that the district court correctly dismissed the action based on the Constitutionally Based Educational Claims Act (CBECA), Idaho Code sections 6-2201-2216, and the Appellant’s failure to state a claim.

Having reviewed the record, the applicable law, and the arguments presented by both parties, the Court affirms the district court’s dismissal.

**II. ANALYSIS**

**A. The CBECA Applies to Joki’s Claims and Is Constitutional as Applied**

The State Defendants correctly assert that the CBECA governs the Appellant’s claims. Idaho Code § 6-2202 explicitly defines the scope of the CBECA to include "any claim brought against the state, any state agency, or any political subdivision of the state alleging a violation of Article IX, Section 1 or Section 2 of the Idaho Constitution." The Appellant’s request for a declaratory judgment falls squarely within this statutory definition.

The Appellant argues that the CBECA is unconstitutional as applied to his claim for a declaratory judgment regarding the entire education funding system. However, as established in *Idaho Schools for Equal Educational Opportunity v. State*, 140 Idaho 586, 97 P.3d 453 (2004) (ISEEO IV), the CBECA "was enacted to provide a comprehensive statutory scheme for addressing constitutionally based educational claims" and its procedures "must be followed." 140 Idaho at 592, 97 P.3d at 459. The Appellant has not demonstrated that the CBECA’s administrative remedies are inadequate to address his specific allegations regarding the funding system. The statute’s plain language and the holding in *ISEEO IV* confirm that the CBECA governs this action, and no "as applied" exception exists for systemic funding claims.

**B. The Appellant Failed to Exhaust Mandatory Administrative Remedies**

The dismissal is further warranted by the Appellant’s failure to exhaust administrative remedies. The CBECA mandates that a claimant "shall file a claim with the state board of education or the appropriate political subdivision" before commencing litigation. Idaho Code § 6-2205. This Court has previously characterized this requirement as "mandatory and jurisdictional." *ISEEO IV*, 140 Idaho at 594, 97 P.3d at 461.

The Appellant filed this lawsuit directly in district court without first filing a claim with the State Board of Education. The Appellant argues that exhaustion should be waived due to futility, claiming the State Board of Education lacks the authority to declare the funding system unconstitutional. This argument is misplaced. The doctrine of futility excuses exhaustion only when an administrative body lacks the capacity to provide any meaningful relief. As recognized in *ISEEO IV*, the administrative process "allows for the investigation and resolution of factual disputes regarding the adequacy of the funding system prior to judicial intervention." The Board’s role in developing a factual record and investigating funding mechanisms is a critical function of the CBECA process, regardless of whether the Board can issue the ultimate declaratory relief sought. Because the Appellant failed to comply with this mandatory prerequisite, the district court was correct in dismissing the action.

**C. The Appellant Failed to State a Claim Under *ISEEO V***

The district court properly determined that the Appellant failed to state a claim upon which relief could be granted. In *Idaho Schools for Equal Educational Opportunity v. State*, 148 Idaho 630, 227 P.3d 378 (2010) (ISEEO V), this Court addressed the narrow issue of whether the state’s funding system for *school facilities* was unconstitutional. The Court found a violation based on specific evidence regarding the safety and adequacy of school facilities. 148 Idaho at 637, 227 P.3d at 385.

The Appellant’s complaint in the present case contains no allegations regarding inadequate school facilities. Instead, it presents a broad, systemic challenge to the education funding structure without the specific factual context required to state a claim. To state a claim for a violation of Article IX, Section 1, a plaintiff must allege more than a bare legal conclusion that the system is unconstitutional. The Appellant’s complaint fails to provide the factual allegations necessary to support a finding that the funding system is "thorough" or "uniform" as those terms were interpreted in *ISEEO V*. The district court correctly distinguished *ISEEO V* and concluded that the Appellant failed to state a claim.

**D. The Separation of Powers Argument Is Without Merit**

The Appellant suggests that the CBECA infringes on judicial authority. This argument misunderstands the nature of the CBECA. The Legislature has the constitutional authority to establish procedural requirements for bringing claims against the state. The CBECA represents a valid exercise of that authority and does not preclude judicial review; it merely requires that claimants first pursue administrative remedies. This Court has repeatedly upheld the CBECA as a valid exercise of legislative power that does not violate separation of powers. See *ISEEO IV*, 140 Idaho at 592, 97 P.3d at 459.

**E. Class Certification Is Irrelevant to the Substantive Analysis**

The Appellant attempts to link the denial of class certification to the merits of his claims. However, the district court denied class certification on grounds unrelated to the substantive constitutional questions—specifically, "concerns over the suitability of [Joki] as representative of the proposed class." This ruling does not affect the analysis of whether the claims against the State Defendants were properly dismissed. The dismissal of the State Defendants rested on independent substantive grounds: the applicability of the CBECA, the failure to exhaust remedies, and the failure to state a claim.

**III. CONCLUSION**

The Appellant’s arguments do not overcome the procedural and substantive barriers to his claims. The CBECA applies to his claims against the State Defendants, and he failed to exhaust the mandatory administrative remedies. Furthermore, the complaint failed to state a claim under the narrow holding of *ISEEO V*. The district court’s dismissal of the State Defendants is affirmed.
