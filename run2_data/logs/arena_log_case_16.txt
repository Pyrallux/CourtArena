## III. ARGUMENT

### A. The District Court Correctly Dismissed Joki's Fee Reimbursement Claims Against the State Defendants

The district court properly dismissed Joki's claims for reimbursement of school fees imposed by local school districts. This Court has squarely addressed the precise issue presented and held that "state officials are not proper defendants for claims concerning the imposition of school fees by local districts." *Idaho School District No. 1 v. State*, 120 Idaho 227, 232, 815 P.2d 461, 466 (1991).

In *Idaho School District No. 1*, this Court examined whether state officials could be held liable for fees imposed by local school districts. This Court concluded that such claims are properly directed at the local school districts themselves because "the decision to impose fees...rests with the local school boards." *Id.* at 233, 815 P.2d at 467. The Court emphasized that "the state board and superintendent have no authority to regulate the fees charged by local school districts." *Id.*

Joki's opening argument attempts to distinguish *Idaho School District No. 1* by suggesting that the State Defendants possess "general responsibilities" over education that might somehow render them proper defendants. This argument fails for several reasons.

First, general supervisory authority over the public education system does not create liability for decisions made by local entities. The Idaho Constitution grants local school boards the authority to "manage and control" public schools within their districts. Idaho Const. art. VIII, § 6. The State Defendants simply do not possess the statutory or constitutional authority to set, collect, or regulate the specific fees challenged by Joki.

Second, this Court in *Idaho School District No. 1* explicitly considered and rejected the argument that general state oversight authority makes state officials proper defendants for local fee decisions. The Court held that "the state board and superintendent have no authority to regulate the fees charged by local school districts." *Idaho School District No. 1*, 120 Idaho at 233, 815 P.2d at 467. This holding controls the instant case.

Third, Joki's characterization of his claims as challenging a "system" of fees rather than individual fee impositions does not alter the analysis. Regardless of how Joki frames his claims, the underlying factual allegation remains that local school districts imposed specific fees. The entity that actually imposed those fees—the local school district—is the proper defendant. *See id.*

The district court correctly applied *Idaho School District No. 1* and dismissed the fee reimbursement claims against the State Defendants.

### B. The District Court Correctly Dismissed Joki's Declaratory Judgment Claim

The district court also correctly dismissed Joki's claim for a declaratory judgment that Idaho's education funding system violates Article IX, Section 1 of the Idaho Constitution. This claim fails for two independent reasons.

#### 1. Joki Failed to State a Cognizable Constitutional Claim

This Court has explicitly addressed the requirements for stating a valid constitutional claim under Article IX, Section 1. In *ISEEO IV*, this Court held that "constitutional challenges to public education funding must be narrowly tailored to the specific system at issue." *Idaho Schools for Equal Educational Opportunity v. State*, 140 Idaho 586, 597, 97 P.3d 453, 464 (2004). The Court emphasized that its prior decisions addressed "specific constitutional violations" rather than general challenges to the education funding system. *Id.*

Joki's complaint failed to meet this demanding standard. The complaint did not allege any deficiency in school facilities—the specific subject of this Court's holdings in *ISEEO IV* and related cases. Rather, Joki's complaint broadly challenged the "entire education system" without identifying any specific, cognizable constitutional violation.

This Court has consistently held that Article IX, Section 1 creates a "general, uniform and thorough system of public, free common schools." Idaho Const. art. IX, § 1. However, the Court has also recognized that this provision does not create a judicially enforceable right to any particular level of funding or educational program. *See ISEEO IV*, 140 Idaho at 595, 97 P.3d at 462. Rather, the Court's prior decisions have addressed only "specific constitutional violations" related to particular aspects of the education system. *Id.* at 597, 97 P.3d at 464.

Joki's complaint failed to identify any such specific violation. The complaint did not allege inadequate school facilities, insufficient instructional materials, or any other particularized deficiency that this Court has recognized as cognizable under Article IX, Section 1. Instead, the complaint presented a generalized attack on the education funding system that fails to state a valid cause of action.

#### 2. The CBECA Bars Joki's Claims Against the State Defendants

Independent of the failure to state a cognizable claim, the CBECA independently requires dismissal of Joki's claims against the State Defendants. The CBECA establishes a comprehensive scheme for resolving constitutionally based educational claims against the state, its agencies, and officials. Idaho Code §§ 6-2201-2216.

The district court correctly held that the CBECA applies to all of Joki's claims against the State Defendants. The CBECA defines "educational claim" to include "any claim arising directly or indirectly from a duty or obligation imposed on the state, the state board, the department, or any political subdivision of the state by the Constitution of the United States or the Constitution of the state of Idaho, with respect to the establishment, maintenance, or funding of the public school system." Idaho Code § 6-2202(3).

Joki's claims—whether characterized as challenging school fees or the overall funding system—unequivocally arise from duties imposed by the Idaho Constitution, Article IX, Section 1. Thus, they fall squarely within the CBECA's definition of "educational claims."

Under the CBECA, a plaintiff must exhaust the administrative remedies provided therein before commencing litigation against the state or its officials. Idaho Code § 6-2203. The statute specifically provides that "no educational claim may be brought against the state, the state board, the department, or any political subdivision of the state without first exhausting the remedies provided in this chapter." Idaho Code § 6-2204.

Joki failed to allege or demonstrate that he exhausted these remedies. The district court therefore correctly held that the CBECA required dismissal of Joki's claims against the State Defendants.

Joki's argument that the CBECA is unconstitutional as applied to his claims was properly rejected by the district court. This Court has upheld the CBECA's constitutionality when applied to claims similar to those asserted by Joki. *See ISEEO IV*, 140 Idaho at 591-92, 97 P.3d at 458-59. The statute represents a legitimate exercise of the Legislature's authority to establish procedures for resolving constitutional claims against the state.

**FINAL RULING**

**I. INTRODUCTION**

This matter comes before the Court on appeal from the district court’s order dismissing the State Defendants. The Appellant, Joki, initiated this action seeking reimbursement for fees imposed by local school districts and a declaratory judgment that Idaho’s system of funding public education is unconstitutional. The district court dismissed the State Defendants, determining that the Constitutionally Based Educational Claims Act (CBECA) barred the claims and that Joki failed to state a cognizable cause of action. Joki appeals the dismissal. Having reviewed the closing arguments submitted by the parties, the Court issues its final ruling as follows.

**II. STANDARD OF REVIEW**

The Court reviews a district court’s grant of a motion to dismiss de novo. *Roper v. Lambda Chi Alpha Fraternity*, 156 Idaho 847, 852, 332 P.3d 715, 720 (2014). In doing so, the Court accepts all well-pleaded facts as true and draws all reasonable inferences in favor of the non-moving party. *Id.* However, the Court is not required to accept conclusory allegations or unwarranted inferences of fact. *Id.*

**III. ANALYSIS**

The State Defendants argue that the district court correctly dismissed Joki’s claims for three independent reasons: (1) Joki’s fee reimbursement claims are misdirected because state officials lack authority over local school fees; (2) the declaratory judgment claim fails to state a cognizable constitutional claim because it is not narrowly tailored and lacks a concrete injury; and (3) the CBECA bars the claim. The Court finds these arguments persuasive and dispositive.

**A. The Fee Reimbursement Claims Were Properly Dismissed**

The State Defendants correctly assert that they are not proper defendants for claims concerning the imposition of school fees by local districts. This Court has previously held that "state officials are not proper defendants for claims concerning the imposition of school fees by local districts," because the decision to impose fees rests exclusively with local school boards. *Idaho School District No. 1 v. State*, 120 Idaho 227, 232, 815 P.2d 461, 466 (1991). The Court further emphasized that "the state board and superintendent have no authority to regulate the fees charged by local school districts." *Id.* at 233, 815 P.2d at 467.

Joki’s complaint targets fees established by local school board decisions and does not allege that the State Defendants imposed or collected the fees. The Appellant has not provided any argument overcoming the clear precedent that fee-setting power resides solely with local entities. Consequently, the district court properly dismissed the fee reimbursement claims against the State Defendants.

**B. The Declaratory Judgment Claim Was Properly Dismissed**

Joki seeks a declaratory judgment that Idaho’s entire system of funding education is unconstitutional. The district court dismissed this claim, and the State Defendants argue this ruling was correct for two independent reasons.

**1. Failure to State a Cognizable Claim and Lack of Narrow Tailoring**

Joki’s declaratory judgment claim fails to state a valid cause of action under Article IX, Section 1 of the Idaho Constitution. This Court has explicitly held that constitutional challenges to public education funding must be "narrowly tailored to the specific system at issue." *Idaho Schools for Equal Educational Opportunity v. State*, 140 Idaho 586, 597, 97 P.3d 453, 464 (2004). In *ISEEO IV*, the Court addressed a specific constitutional violation regarding the funding of school facilities. *Id.*

Joki’s complaint broadly challenges the "entire education system" without alleging any specific deficiency in school facilities or other particularized deficiencies recognized by this Court. The arguments correctly note that Joki has failed to allege a concrete constitutional injury, such as a specific student being denied educational opportunities due to unconstitutional funding deficiencies. Because the complaint is not narrowly tailored to the specific issue addressed in *ISEEO IV* and fails to allege a concrete injury, it fails to state a valid cause of action.

**2. The CBECA Bars the Claim**

Alternatively, the CBECA independently requires dismissal of Joki’s claims against the State Defendants. The CBECA establishes a comprehensive scheme for resolving constitutionally based educational claims against the state, its agencies, and its officials. Idaho Code §§ 6-2201-2216. The statute defines an "educational claim" to include any claim arising from duties imposed by the state constitution regarding the establishment, maintenance, or funding of the public school system. Idaho Code § 6-2202(3).

Joki’s claims fall squarely within this definition. Under the CBECA, a plaintiff must exhaust the administrative remedies provided in the chapter before commencing litigation against the state or its officials. Idaho Code § 6-2204. Joki failed to allege or demonstrate that he exhausted these remedies. While Joki argues that the CBECA is unconstitutional as applied to his claims, this argument is unpersuasive. The holding in *ISEEO IV* addressed the constitutionality of specific provisions as applied to particular claims, not the statute in its entirety or as applied here. Therefore, the district court correctly held that the CBECA requires dismissal of Joki’s claims against the State Defendants.

**IV. CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the district court did not err in dismissing the State Defendants. The State Defendants lack authority over the local fees at issue, and Joki’s declaratory judgment claim is barred by precedent and the CBECA. The arguments presented by the State Defendants are legally sound, supported by the record, and remain unrefuted by the Appellant.

**IT IS ORDERED** that the district court’s order dismissing the State Defendants is affirmed.
