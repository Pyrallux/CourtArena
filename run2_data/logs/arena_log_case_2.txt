**PRELIMINARY RULING**

**I. INTRODUCTION**

This matter comes before the court on appeal from the judgment of the trial court following a jury verdict in favor of the plaintiff, Rita A. Wallace. The defendants, Hector L. Haddock and Subaru Leasing Corporation, appeal from the trial court's decision to grant an additur of $15,000 and immediately render judgment, contending that the court failed to offer them the statutory option to reject the additur in favor of a new trial. For the reasons set forth below, the court finds that the trial court committed reversible error by entering judgment without affording the defendants this choice. Consequently, the judgment is reversed, and the matter is remanded with direction to offer the defendants the option of a new trial.

**II. DISCUSSION**

**A. Standard of Review and Applicable Law**

The issue presented is whether the trial court's failure to offer the defendants the alternative of a new trial before entering judgment on an additur constitutes reversible error. The proper procedure for additur is governed by General Statutes §§ 52-216a and 52-228b, as well as controlling precedent.

In *Kozouff v. Kozouff*, 189 Conn. 281, 456 A.2d 312 (Conn. 1983), the Supreme Court explicitly established the procedural safeguards required when a trial court alters a jury's damage award. The Court held that "the trial court must offer the defendant the option of a new trial in lieu of the additur before entering judgment." This rule is not merely a suggestion but a fundamental requirement of due process and statutory compliance.

This principle was reaffirmed by the Appellate Court in *Ostrer v. Schwartz*, 126 Conn. App. 654, 12 A.3d 1100 (Conn. App. 2011). In *Ostrer*, the court reversed a judgment where the trial court granted an additur and entered judgment without providing the defendant the opportunity to elect a new trial. The court stated unequivocally that "the failure to offer the defendant the alternative of a new trial before rendering judgment on the additur constitutes reversible error as a matter of law."

**B. Application to the Present Case**

The record in the present case mirrors the procedural defect identified in *Ostrer*. On July 12, 2002, the trial court issued a memorandum of decision granting the plaintiff's motion for an additur in the amount of $15,000. The court immediately rendered judgment for the plaintiff in the total amount of $16,715. There is no indication in the record that the court offered the defendants the choice between accepting this increased award or proceeding to a new trial on the issue of damages.

Both the appellants and the appellee agree that this omission constitutes error. The appellee concedes that the trial court "failed to offer the defendants the option of rejecting the additur and proceeding to a new trial before rendering judgment." This admission aligns with the clear mandates of *Kozouff* and *Ostrer*. Because the trial court deprived the defendants of their statutory right to make an informed election regarding the additur, the entry of judgment was premature and invalid.

**C. Determination of the Appropriate Remedy**

While the parties agree that reversible error occurred, they dispute the proper remedy on remand. The appellants request that the matter be remanded for the trial court to properly offer the defendants the option to accept the additur or proceed to a new trial. The appellee, however, argues that the court should vacate the additur entirely and order a new trial on damages, asserting that the additur is void because it was imposed without consent.

The court finds the appellants' proposed remedy to be the correct application of the law. General Statutes § 52-228b provides that an additur may be ordered only if the party to be charged consents. The statutory scheme is designed to protect the defendant's right to a jury trial. If the defendant chooses to accept the additur, they waive that right. If they reject it, they are entitled to a new trial.

The appellee's argument that the additur must be vacated and a new trial ordered immediately misconstrues the purpose of the statute. The error here was the lack of *consent*, not necessarily the validity of the additur amount itself. By ordering a new trial outright, the court would deprive the defendants of the opportunity to consent to the additur—a right they possess under the statute. If the defendants are willing to accept the $15,000 additur to avoid the expense and uncertainty of a new trial, the law permits them to do so. The appellee cannot force a new trial if the defendants are willing to accept the increased judgment.

Therefore, the appropriate remedy is to reverse the judgment and remand the matter to the trial court with directions to offer the defendants the statutory choice: accept the additur of $15,000 or undergo a new trial on the issue of damages.

**III. CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the court concludes that the trial court abused its discretion and violated statutory law by failing to offer the defendants the alternative of a new trial before entering judgment on the additur. The judgment of the trial court is reversed, and the case is remanded for further proceedings consistent with this ruling.

**FINAL RULING**

**I. INTRODUCTION**

This matter is before the court upon the review of the closing arguments submitted by the plaintiff, Rita A. Wallace, and the defendants, Hector L. Haddock and Subaru Leasing Corporation. The court has previously determined that the trial court committed reversible error by entering judgment on an additur without first offering the defendants the statutory option to reject the additur in favor of a new trial. Having considered the parties' final submissions, the court finds that they are in agreement regarding the existence of this procedural error and the necessity of a remand to correct it. Accordingly, the court adopts its preliminary ruling as the final judgment of the court, with further clarification regarding the scope of the remand.

**II. DISCUSSION**

**A. The Procedural Violation**

Both the prosecution and the defense correctly identify the dispositive issue in this appeal: the trial court's failure to comply with General Statutes §§ 52-216a and 52-228b. The record reflects that on July 12, 2002, the trial court granted the plaintiff's motion for an additur of $15,000 and immediately rendered judgment for the plaintiff. At no point did the court offer the defendants the opportunity to elect a new trial instead of accepting the increased award.

The prosecution argues, and the defense concedes, that this omission constitutes reversible error per se. This position is well-founded in Connecticut law. In *Kozouff v. Kozouff*, 189 Conn. 281, 456 A.2d 312 (Conn. 1983), the Supreme Court held that "the trial court must offer the defendant the option of a new trial in lieu of the additur before entering judgment." This rule is designed to protect the defendant's constitutional right to a jury trial by ensuring that any increase in damages is consented to by the defendant rather than imposed by the court.

The Appellate Court reaffirmed this principle in *Ostrer v. Schwartz*, 126 Conn. App. 654, 12 A.3d 1100 (Conn. App. 2011), stating that "the failure to offer the defendant the alternative of a new trial before rendering judgment on the additur constitutes reversible error as a matter of law." The prosecution correctly characterizes this as a "bright-line rule" that is not subject to harmless error analysis or dependent on the substantive adequacy of the verdict. Because the trial court failed to adhere to this mandatory procedural step, the judgment entered on July 12, 2002, cannot stand.

**B. The Scope of Remand and Reservation of Rights**

The defense argues persuasively that the proper remedy is a limited remand for the sole purpose of conducting the statutory election. The court agrees. The error here was purely procedural; there is no indication in the record that the trial judge was biased or that the court's substantive determination regarding the inadequacy of the verdict was made in bad faith. Therefore, the matter is remanded to the same trial court to offer the defendants the choice mandated by statute.

The defense further clarifies that by seeking the statutory election, they do not concede the substantive reasonableness of the $15,000 additur. The court finds this reservation of rights to be entirely consistent with the statutory framework. The purpose of the election is to allow the defendant to choose between accepting the court's determination of damages or challenging it before a new jury. As the defense notes, if the defendants reject the additur on remand, they retain the right to fully contest the adequacy of the award in a new trial. The prosecution's attempt to argue the substantive merits of the additur at this juncture is premature; the "shock the conscience" standard articulated in *Kozouff v. Kozouff*, 189 Conn. 281, 456 A.2d 312 (Conn. 1983), is relevant to the trial court's initial determination and to any future new trial, but it does not cure the procedural defect that occurred here.

**III. CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the court concludes that the trial court abused its discretion by failing to offer the defendants the alternative of a new trial before entering judgment on the additur. This violation of General Statutes §§ 52-216a and 52-228b requires reversal of the judgment. The matter is remanded to the trial court with directions to offer the defendants the statutory election between accepting the additur of $15,000 or proceeding to a new trial on the issue of damages.

**SO ORDERED.**
