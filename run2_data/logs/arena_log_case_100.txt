FACTS:
Robert Fischer sustained serious injuries while riding a bicycle through a public park in Sioux Falls. Fischer sued the City of Sioux Falls for negligence, but the circuit court granted the City's request for summary judgment, concluding that the City is immune from liability for such negligence claims. Fischer appeals, arguing there is a genuine issue of material fact as to whether the City's conduct amounts to gross negligence or willful or wanton misconduct. We affirm.

Facts and Procedural History

[¶ 2.] On June 29, 2014, Fischer and his grandson were riding their bicycles along a paved path in Kuehn Park, which is owned by the City of Sioux Falls. Kuehn Park offers a golf course, playground, softball diamonds, swimming pool, and tennis courts. For parkgoers entering via the paved path, the most direct route to the tennis courts and swimming pool is through the northern gate of the tennis courts. Noticing that the tennis courts' northern gate was open, Fischer diverted from the path and rode through the grass, intending to access the tennis courts and swimming pool. While riding into a depressed area, the front tire of Fischer's bicycle became lodged in a natural drainage ditch that had been concealed by grass. Fischer was thrown from his bicycle and sustained serious injuries, including fractures in his back, neck, and sternum.

[¶ 3.] Fischer filed an action against the City on May 3, 2016, alleging a single claim of "negligence." In the complaint, Fischer alleged that the City owed him a duty to make the park reasonably safe or to warn him of concealed dangers like the drainage ditch. He also alleged that the City "failed to use reasonable care or diligence to design, construct, maintain in good repair, inspect and upgrade the area where [Fischer] was injured or to warn [him] of the concealed danger."

[¶ 4.] On November 14, 2016, the City filed a motion requesting summary judgment. The City argued that it was immune from liability for negligence under SDCL 20-9-20, which generally states that a political subdivision of South Dakota owes no duty of care to keep land used for outdoor *214recreational purposes safe or to warn of dangerous conditions. Although Fischer had not alleged gross negligence or willful or wanton misconduct in his complaint, he responded that SDCL 20-9-20 did not immunize the City from liability for such claims. The parties deposed several of the City's employees, who generally testified that they were aware of the natural drainage ditch, that the ditch was often concealed by grass, and that they believed a bicyclist attempting to ride over the ditch could be injured. After holding a hearing on September 11, 2017, the circuit court granted the City's motion.

[¶ 5.] Fischer appeals, raising the following issue: Whether the circuit court erred by granting the City's motion for summary judgment.

Standard of Review

[¶ 6.] Summary judgment is appropriate "if the pleadings, depositions, answers to interrogatories, and admissions on file, together with the affidavits, if any, show that there is no genuine issue as to any material fact and that the moving party is entitled to a judgment as a matter of law." SDCL 15-6-56(c). This Court "view[s] the evidence 'most favorably to the nonmoving party and resolve[s] reasonable doubts against the moving party[,]' " Gades v. Meyer Modernizing Co. , 2015 S.D. 42, ¶ 7, 865 N.W.2d 155, 158 (quoting Peters v. Great W. Bank, Inc. , 2015 S.D. 4, ¶ 5, 859 N.W.2d 618, 621 ), but a plaintiff must "substantiate [his] allegations with sufficient probative evidence that would permit a finding in [his] favor on more than mere speculation, conjecture, or fantasy[,]" Schaefer v. Sioux Spine & Sport, PLLC , 2018 S.D. 5, ¶ 9, 906 N.W.2d 427, 431 (quoting Peters , 2015 S.D. 4, ¶ 13, 859 N.W.2d at 624 ). Questions of law are reviewed de novo. Mont.-Dakota Utils. Co. v. Parkshill Farms, LLC , 2017 S.D. 88, ¶ 9, 905 N.W.2d 334, 338.

## IV. LEGAL ANALYSIS

### A. SDCL 20-9-20 Provides Immunity for Negligence Claims Arising from Outdoor Recreational Land Use

South Dakota law explicitly provides that political subdivisions of this state owe no duty of care to keep land used for outdoor recreational purposes safe or to warn of dangerous conditions. SDCL 20-9-20. This statute reflects a deliberate policy choice by the Legislature to encourage public access to parks and open spaces without exposing municipalities to constant litigation for ordinary accidents arising from the use of recreational lands.

This Court has consistently interpreted SDCL 20-9-20 as providing broad immunity from negligence claims. In Johnson v. City of Rapid City, 2004 SD 122, 691 N.W.2d 235, this Court explicitly held that the statute "imposes no duty to keep recreational land safe or to warn of dangerous conditions." The statute's purpose is to protect municipalities from liability for the ordinary risks inherent in outdoor recreational activities.

In In re Estate of Giese, 2002 SD 32, 641 N.W.2d 699, this Court affirmed that when a claim arises from recreational land use, immunity attaches unless the political subdivision's conduct crosses the threshold into "willful or malicious" territory. The Giese Court made clear that the exception to immunity is narrow and requires conduct that goes far beyond ordinary negligence.

### B. Fischer's Complaint Alleges Only Negligence, Not Willful or Wanton Misconduct

A fundamental flaw in Fischer's appeal is that his complaint alleged only simple negligence. He never alleged gross negligence, willful misconduct, or wanton conduct. The complaint specifically stated a single claim of "negligence" and alleged that the City "failed to use reasonable care or diligence." This is the textbook definition of a negligence claim—nothing more.

As this Court has held, a plaintiff cannot amend his complaint through briefing or oral argument. The complaint defines the scope of the claims being litigated. Fischer's attempt to inject "gross negligence" or "wanton misconduct" into this case at the summary judgment stage is improper and should be rejected. His complaint contains no such allegations, and he cannot now manufacture a claim that was never pleaded.

### C. The Record Contains No Evidence of Willful or Wanton Misconduct

Even if Fischer had properly alleged willful or wanton misconduct, the record contains no evidence to support such a claim. The standard for overcoming statutory immunity under SDCL 20-9-20 requires evidence of intentional, malicious conduct—not merely awareness of a hazard.

In City of Sioux Falls v. Schuster, 2000 SD 121, 617 N.W.2d 333, this Court stated that "knowledge of a hazard alone, without evidence of intentional disregard for safety, cannot transform ordinary negligence into the extreme misconduct required to pierce immunity." The Schuster Court made clear that mere knowledge of a dangerous condition is insufficient to impose liability; there must be evidence of intentional misconduct or reckless disregard for safety.

The facts here are indistinguishable from those in Schuster. City employees testified that they were aware of the natural drainage ditch and that it was occasionally concealed by grass. However, awareness of a natural feature of the landscape does not equate to willful or malicious conduct. The City did not create the ditch, did not intentionally conceal it, and did not receive any reports of prior incidents involving the ditch. The City's conduct consisted of routine park maintenance—allowing the natural terrain to exist as it had for decades.

This Court has also held in Johnson v. City of Rapid City that "gross negligence does not satisfy the exception" to recreational use immunity. The Johnson Court was unequivocal: even conduct that might be characterized as "gross" negligence is insufficient to overcome the shield provided by SDCL 20-9-20. Only intentional, malicious conduct can pierce this immunity.

### D. Fischer's Own Conduct, Not the City's, Caused His Injuries

The record establishes that Fischer voluntarily chose to leave the designated paved path and ride across grass-covered terrain. The paved path was the designated route for park visitors. Fischer decided to divert from this path to access the tennis courts through the northern gate, choosing to ride through an area with natural terrain features.

The drainage ditch that caused Fischer's accident was a natural feature of the park—a pre-existing drainage ditch that had been in place for decades. The City did not create this ditch, did not modify it, and did not conceal it with any intent to harm park visitors. The ditch was a natural part of the park's topography, and its occasional concealment by grass is a natural occurrence in any outdoor landscape.

Fischer's decision to ride off the paved path and into an area with natural terrain was his own choice. The City had no duty to protect him from the natural risks associated with leaving the designated path. Under SDCL 20-9-20, the City owed no duty to warn of or make safe the natural drainage ditch that existed in the grass area where Fischer chose to ride.

## II. ARGUMENT

### A. The Appellant's Complaint Alleges Only Ordinary Negligence, Which Is Barred by SDCL 20-9-20

The Appellant's complaint, filed on May 3, 2016, alleged a single claim of "negligence." The complaint specifically alleged that the City "failed to use reasonable care or diligence to design, construct, maintain in good repair, inspect and upgrade the area where [Fischer] was injured or to warn [him] of the concealed danger."

South Dakota Code Law Section 20-9-20 explicitly provides that "A political subdivision of this state owes no duty of care to keep land used for outdoor recreational purposes safe or to warn of dangerous conditions." The statute was clearly designed to bar exactly the type of claim the Appellant has brought—ordinary negligence arising from the condition of recreational land.

The Appellant now attempts to argue that his claim should be treated as one for gross negligence or willful misconduct. However, he never pleaded such claims in his complaint. ### B. The Record Contains No Evidence of Willful or Malicious Conduct

1. The drainage ditch was a natural, pre-existing feature of the park;
2. City employees had no knowledge of any prior incidents or complaints regarding the ditch;

4. The City performed only routine maintenance in the area.

In *City of Sioux Falls v. Schuster*, 2000 SD 121, ¶ 12, 617 N.W.2d 333, 337, this Court held that "knowledge of a hazard alone, without evidence of intentional disregard for safety, cannot transform ordinary negligence into the extreme misconduct required to pierce immunity." The Appellant admits that City employees were aware of the ditch's existence, but he has presented no evidence that the City intentionally disregarded safety concerns.

Furthermore, in *Johnson v. City of Rapid City*, 2004 SD 122, ¶ 15, 691 N.W.2d 235, 240, this Court squarely held that "gross negligence does not satisfy the exception" to recreational use immunity. ### D. The Appellant Cannot Survive Summary Judgment
