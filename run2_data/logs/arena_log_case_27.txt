Connecticut General Statutes § 52-228b provides that when a trial court orders an additur, "the plaintiff may either accept the additur or proceed to a new trial." This statutory language contemplates that a defendant who rejects the additur is entitled to a new trial on the issue of damages. The defense is correct that the plaintiff cannot characterize the procedural error as the sole issue while simultaneously arguing that the substantive amount of the additur is preclusively determined. The purpose of the additur/new trial option is to allow defendants to choose between accepting the court's assessment of damages or testing the plaintiff's claims before a new jury.

The defense further argues that the record lacks sufficient evidence of a permanent disability to justify the additur, citing *Ferraro v. Bucci*, 214 Conn. 221, 571 A.2d 1207 (1990), and *Chen v. New Haven*, 248 Conn. 552, 730 A.2d 1040 (1999), for the proposition that substantial additurs require objective proof of severe injury. While the trial court found the evidence sufficient to grant the additur, the defense retains the right to contest the existence and extent of any permanent disability, the necessity of medical treatment, and the reasonableness of the claimed noneconomic damages at a new trial.

The standard for additur in Connecticut is whether the jury's award is "so inadequate as to shock the conscience of the court" or "against the weight of the evidence." *Curtis v. Hartford Hospital*, 252 Conn. 1, 743 A.2d 1 (2000). While we decline to find that the trial court abused its discretion in determining the inadequacy of the verdict, we agree with the defense that this finding does not bind the parties at a new trial. If the defendants reject the additur on remand, the plaintiff bears the burden of proving the extent of her claimed damages *de novo*.

Therefore, while we reverse the judgment due to the procedural error, we clarify that the substantive validity of the $15,000 additur is not preclusively determined by this proceeding. The defense's remedy for their dissatisfaction with the amount lies in their statutory right to reject the additur on remand and proceed to a new trial, where they may fully challenge the plaintiff's evidence regarding the nature, extent, and causation of any alleged injuries.

**CONCLUSION**

The judgment of the trial court is reversed. The case is remanded with directions to offer the parties the opportunity to accept the additur of $15,000 or, in the alternative, to proceed with a new trial on the issue of damages, consistent with the requirements of Connecticut General Statutes §§ 52-216a and 52-228b. Should the defendants reject the additur, they are entitled to a new trial wherein the plaintiff must prove her damages, and the defendants may challenge the adequacy of the evidence supporting the plaintiff's claims.
