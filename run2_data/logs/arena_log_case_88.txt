COURT ARENA LOG - Case 88
FACTS:
In this action challenging the assessment of alleged illegal taxes, the plaintiff, Bluedog Capital Partners, LLC (Bluedog), appeals from a judgment of the Superior Court granting the motion to dismiss pursuant to Rule 12(b)(6) of the Superior Court Rules of Civil Procedure filed by the defendants, John A. Murphy, in his capacity as tax collector for the City of Providence (the city); David Quinn, in his capacity as tax assessor for the city; and Michael Pearis, in his capacity as finance director for the city (collectively defendants).1 This matter came before the Court on March 27, 2019, pursuant to an order directing the parties to appear and show cause why the issues raised should not be summarily decided. After considering the arguments set forth in the parties' memoranda and at oral argument, we are convinced that cause has not been shown. Thus, further argument or briefing is not required to decide this matter. For the reasons outlined below, we affirm the judgment of the Superior Court.

I

Facts and Travel

The following facts, sparse as they are, have been gleaned from Bluedog's complaint. On or about October 3, 2014, Bluedog, a private lender, loaned $ 35,000 to Fogliare Group, LLC (Fogliare), in consideration of a first-priority mortgage on Fogliare's real property, which has a street address of 170 Bartlett Avenue, Providence, Rhode Island (the property). Bluedog recorded the mortgage in the Providence land evidence records.2 The property encompasses two lots: Assessor's Plat 90, Lot 13, which is located within the city (the Providence parcel); and Assessor's Plat 2-5, Lot 1520, which is located within the City of Cranston (the Cranston parcel). The city noticed a tax sale for the Providence parcel in tax year 2015; and, thereafter, on July 1, 2015, it sold the Providence parcel at a tax sale, with a collector's deed being recorded in the Providence land evidence records on July 9, 2015. On June 2, 2016, Bluedog filed a two-count complaint, seeking a declaration that defendants had illegally assessed the Providence parcel (Count I) and seeking injunctive relief against the sale of the property (Count II). Bluedog alleged that its mortgage was "subject to the tax sale[,]" and that, in tax year 2015, the total assessed value of the Providence parcel was $ 102,200.3

On June 24, 2016, defendants filed a motion to dismiss Bluedog's complaint, pursuant to Rule 12(b)(6). In their memorandum in support of the motion to dismiss, defendants argued that G.L. 1956 § 44-5-26 provides the exclusive procedures for the tax-abatement relief that Bluedog sought and that Bluedog had not *697complied with those procedures.4 Moreover, defendants averred that, even if Bluedog sought to bypass those procedures by invoking the Superior Court's equity jurisdiction under § 44-5-27, the three-month statute-of-limitations period for such actions had expired; defendants alleged that Bluedog had filed its complaint "more than seven (7) months after the limitations period expired for the most recent tax year at issue (2015) * * *." (Emphasis in original.) Finally, defendants contended that there was nothing for the Superior Court to enjoin because the tax sale had already taken place.

In Bluedog's memorandum in support of its opposition to defendants' motion to dismiss, Bluedog contended that "there are certain situations which have been recognized in Rhode Island for bypassing the normal review procedures set forth in * * * §§ 44-5-26 and 44-5-27." Bluedog argued that one such case is where, as here, a tax is alleged to be illegal.5

On November 17, 2016, a hearing on defendants' motion to dismiss was held in Providence County Superior Court. At that hearing, defendants began by arguing that the statute of limitations for tax year 2015 had run on October 24, 2015, and that the complaint, which was filed in June 2016, was therefore untimely. They also averred that the complaint merely alleged an overassessment because the property was at least partially within the city's jurisdiction, and that, even if the taxes were alleged to be illegal, Bluedog was required to appeal the assessment to the board of tax assessment review or else bring an action sounding in equity, pursuant to § 44-5-27, in the Superior Court. The defendants cited the Superior Court decision underlying this Court's opinion in Narragansett Electric Company v. Minardi , 21 A.3d 274 (R.I. 2011), in which the hearing justice found that the Uniform Declaratory Judgments Act could not be used to replace the procedures set forth in §§ 44-5-26 and 44-5-27. See Minardi , 21 A.3d at 277.

*698Moreover, defendants quoted this Court's opinion in Pascale v. Capaldi , 95 R.I. 513, 188 A.2d 378 (1963), in which we stated: "In our opinion the legislature did not intend that a petition under the uniform declaratory judgments act was to take the place of a taxpayer's suit and, therefore, the Superior Court had no jurisdiction under the act to grant the [taxpayer's] prayers." Pascale , 95 R.I. at 514, 188 A.2d at 379. Finally, defendants argued, and the hearing justice seemed to agree, that, because the tax sale had already occurred, there was nothing for the Superior Court to enjoin and, thus, there was no relief that could be granted under Count II of the complaint.

Bluedog responded by outlining the deferential standard that courts give to plaintiffs on motions to dismiss in general, and specifically in declaratory-judgment actions. Bluedog then described the necessary elements to allege an illegal tax, which includes situations where the taxing authority acted without jurisdiction or where the tax was so palpably exorbitant and excessive as to amount to constructive fraud.6 Bluedog maintained that the tax in this case fit the latter criterion because the building that the city had taxed was located "entirely outside of the City of Providence." The hearing justice then indicated that, even if she were willing to give Bluedog the benefit of the doubt regarding whether the taxes were illegal, the three-month statute of limitations under § 44-5-27 had clearly run by the time Bluedog filed its complaint. Bluedog responded that the statute of limitations did not apply in this case because a "declaratory judgment action is outside of what is contemplated in those statutes[,]" and the statute of limitations applied only to "an ordinary request for equitable relief."

In her bench decision, the hearing justice determined that at the crux of this case is "whether or not procedurally and legally * * * the [c]ourt had the authority to actually hear the instant matter." She then quoted the language of both § 44-5-26 and § 44-5-27, outlining the procedures for challenging an assessment of taxes and the exceptions to those procedures. She then indicated that, despite Bluedog's contentions, this Court's opinions in Minardi and CIC-Newport Associates v. Stein , 121 R.I. 844, 403 A.2d 658 (1979), indicate that any suit challenging a purported illegal assessment of taxes-such as the one at issue in this case-"would need to sound in equity and they are therefore time-barred." Accordingly, the hearing justice granted defendants' motion to dismiss Count I of Bluedog's complaint. The hearing justice likewise granted defendants' motion to dismiss as to Count II, stating that "there is really nothing to enjoin here. There is no harm that is presently threatened or imminent because * * * the property has already been sold."7

On December 9, 2016, an order entered dismissing Bluedog's complaint, with prejudice. On December 14, 2016, Bluedog filed a timely notice of appeal to this Court.

II

Standard of Review

"The sole function of a motion to dismiss is to test the sufficiency of the *699complaint." Gannon v. City of Pawtucket , 200 A.3d 1074, 1077 (R.I. 2019) (quoting Minardi , 21 A.3d at 277 ). "In passing on a Rule 12(b) dismissal, this Court applies the same standard as the trial justice." Id. (quoting Minardi , 21 A.3d at 278 ). "We thus are confined to the four corners of the complaint and must assume all the allegations are true, resolving any doubts in the plaintiff's favor." Id. (brackets omitted) (quoting Minardi , 21 A.3d at 278 ). "A motion to dismiss may be granted only if it appears beyond a reasonable doubt that a plaintiff would not be entitled to relief under any conceivable set of facts." Id. (quoting Minardi , 21 A.3d at 278 ). Additionally, "[a] party may raise a statute-of-limitations defense by way of a motion to dismiss, 'provided the alleged timing defect appears on the face of the complaint.' " Barrette v. Yakavonis , 966 A.2d 1231, 1234 (R.I. 2009) (brackets omitted) (quoting Martin v. Howard , 784 A.2d 291, 297 (R.I. 2001) ).

"We review questions of statutory interpretation de novo ." State v. Hazard , 68 A.3d 479, 485 (R.I. 2013) (brackets omitted) (quoting Campbell v. State , 56 A.3d 448, 454 (R.I. 2012) ). In so doing, "our ultimate goal is to give effect to the purpose of the act as intended by the Legislature." Id. (quoting Alessi v. Bowen Court Condominium , 44 A.3d 736, 740 (R.I. 2012) ). However, "taxing statutes are to be strictly construed with doubts resolved in favor of the taxpayer." Balmuth v. Dolce for Town of Portsmouth , 182 A.3d 576, 585 (R.I. 2018) (quoting Maggiacomo v. DiVincenzo , 122 R.I. 615, 618, 410 A.2d 1332, 1333 (1980) ).

## I. INTRODUCTION AND STATEMENT OF THE CASE

This case presents a straightforward question of statutory compliance and procedural timeliness. Bluedog, a private lender that holds a mortgage on real property located at 170 Bartlett Avenue, Providence, Rhode Island, challenges the City of Providence's assessment of taxes on a parcel that lies entirely within Providence's jurisdictional boundaries. Bluedog's complaint, filed on June 2, 2016, sought declaratory and injunctive relief regarding tax year 2015 assessments and the subsequent tax sale of July 1, 2015.

The Superior Court correctly dismissed Bluedog's complaint for failure to state a claim upon which relief can be granted. As the record demonstrates, Bluedog failed to comply with the mandatory statutory procedures governing tax challenges in Rhode Island, and its claims are time-barred by the three-month statute of limitations prescribed in G.L. 1956 § 44-5-27. Furthermore, Bluedog's request for injunctive relief is moot, as the tax sale has already been completed.

The property at issue encompasses two distinct parcels: (1) Assessor's Plat 90, Lot 13, located within the City of Providence (the "Providence parcel"); and (2) Assessor's Plat 2-5, Lot 1520, located within the City of Cranston (the "Cranston parcel"). ## III. STANDARD OF REVIEW

This Court reviews a grant of a motion to dismiss under Rule 12(b)(6) de novo, applying the same standard as the trial justice. *Gannon v. City of Pawtucket*, 200 A.3d 1074, 1077 (R.I. 2019). The sole function of a motion to dismiss is to test the sufficiency of the complaint. *Id.* This Court is confined to the four corners of the complaint and must assume all allegations are true, resolving any doubts in the plaintiff's favor. *Id.* However, a motion to dismiss may be granted when it appears beyond a reasonable doubt that a plaintiff would not be entitled to relief under any conceivable set of facts. *Id.*

Additionally, a statute-of-limitations defense may be raised by way of a motion to dismiss "provided the alleged timing defect appears on the face of the complaint." *Barrette v. Yakavonis*, 966 A.2d 1231, 1234 (R.I. 2009).

## IV. ARGUMENT

### A. The Trial Court Correctly Dismissed Count I Because Bluedog Failed to Comply with Mandatory Statutory Procedures for Challenging Tax Assessments

Rhode Island law provides specific, mandatory procedures for challenging tax assessments. General Laws 1956 § 44-5-26 establishes the exclusive procedures for tax abatement relief, while § 44-5-27 provides the statutory framework for equity actions challenging tax assessments. These procedures reflect the Legislature's deliberate policy choice to provide certainty, finality, and efficiency in municipal tax administration.

The Rhode Island Supreme Court has consistently held that these statutory procedures are exclusive and cannot be circumvented through alternative legal mechanisms. In *Narragansett Electric Company v. Minardi*, 21 A.3d 274 (R.I. 2011), this Court held that the Uniform Declaratory Judgments Act cannot be used to replace the procedures set forth in §§ 44-5-26 and 44-5-27. *Id.* at 277. Similarly, in *Pascale v. Capaldi*, 95 R.I. 513, 188 A.2d 378 (1963), this Court stated that "the legislature did not intend that a petition under the uniform declaratory judgments act was to take the place of a taxpayer's suit and, therefore, the Superior Court had no jurisdiction under the act to grant the [taxpayer's] prayers." *Id.* at 514, 188 A.2d at 379.

Bluedog's complaint, on its face, demonstrates a failure to comply with these mandatory procedures. Bluedog did not appeal its assessment to the board of tax assessment review, nor did it timely file an equity action pursuant to § 44-5-27. Instead, Bluedog attempted to circumvent these requirements by framing its claim as a declaratory judgment action—an approach this Court has explicitly rejected.

Bluedog's characterization of its claim as challenging an "illegal tax" does not alter this conclusion. An illegal tax, within the meaning of Rhode Island law, exists only where the taxing authority acted without jurisdiction or where the tax was "so palpably exorbitant and excessive as to amount to constructive fraud." Neither circumstance is present here. The City of Providence unquestionably has jurisdiction to tax the Providence parcel, as it lies entirely within Providence's municipal boundaries. Bluedog's grievance relates solely to the assessed value of the property—not to the City's authority to assess taxes in the first instance. This is precisely the type of valuation dispute that must be addressed through the statutory abatement procedures, not through collateral attacks on the assessment.

In *CIC-Newport Associates v. Stein*, 121 R.I. 844, 403 A.2d 658 (1979), this Court confirmed that any suit challenging a purported illegal assessment of taxes must sound in equity and comply with the applicable procedural requirements. *Id.* at 848, 403 A.2d at 661. Bluedog's failure to comply with these requirements is fatal to its claim.

### B. The Trial Court Correctly Dismissed Count I Because the Complaint Is Time-Barred

Even if Bluedog had properly invoked the Superior Court's equity jurisdiction under § 44-5-27, its complaint is untimely and was properly dismissed on that independent ground.

Section 44-5-27 imposes a three-month statute of limitations for actions challenging tax assessments. The statute provides that such actions must be commenced within three months from the date the assessment is made or the tax becomes due. For tax year 2015, the limitations period expired on October 24, 2015. Bluedog did not file its complaint until June 2, 2016—more than seven months after the limitations period had run.

This timing defect appears squarely on the face of the complaint. The complaint explicitly alleges that the tax sale occurred on July 1, 2015, and that Bluedog filed its complaint in June 2016. Simple arithmetic demonstrates that Bluedog missed the three-month deadline by approximately seven months.

Bluedog's argument that "declaratory judgment actions are outside the contemplation of those statutes" is legally untenable. This Court has repeatedly held that the statutory procedures in §§ 44-5-26 and 44-5-27 govern all tax challenges, regardless of how they are framed. *See Minardi*, 21 A.3d at 277; *Pascale*, 95 R.I. at 514, 188 A.2d at 379. To hold otherwise would render the statute meaningless and create endless uncertainty for municipal governments. The Legislature enacted these procedures to provide finality to tax assessments, and this Court should not countenance their evasion through creative pleading.

### C. The Trial Court Correctly Dismissed Count II Because the Request for Injunctive Relief Is Moot

The trial court also properly dismissed Count II of Bluedog's complaint, which sought injunctive relief against the sale of the property. Bluedog's request for injunctive relief is moot because the tax sale has already been completed.

The complaint alleges that the Providence parcel was sold at a tax sale on July 1, 2015. A collector's deed was recorded on July 9, 2015. Bluedog did not file its complaint until June 2, 2016—nearly a year after the sale was consummated. At that point, the property had already been transferred to a third-party purchaser. There was no ongoing harm to enjoin, no threatened injury to prevent, and no legal basis for equitable relief.

The mootness doctrine requires that a plaintiff have a live, ongoing injury at the time of filing. *See*. When the alleged harm has already been fully realized, courts lack jurisdiction to grant relief. The trial court correctly recognized that "there is really nothing to enjoin here. There is no harm that is presently threatened or imminent because the property has already been sold."

### D. Bluedog's Characterization of the Tax as "Illegal" Is a Transparent Attempt to Circumvent Mandatory Statutory Procedures

Bluedog's attempt to characterize its claim as challenging an "illegal tax" must be rejected. The complaint itself acknowledges that the Providence parcel is located within the City of Providence's jurisdiction and that the City assessed taxes on that parcel. Bluedog's grievance is not that the City lacked authority to tax the property—it is that the assessed value was too high.

This is the textbook definition of an overassessment, not an illegal tax. Under Rhode Island law, disputes regarding property valuations must be pursued through the statutory abatement procedures. Bluedog cannot escape these requirements by recharacterizing its claim as something it is not.

The policy rationale for this requirement is clear. Municipal governments rely on the finality of tax assessments to plan budgets, deliver services, and manage public resources. Allowing taxpayers to bypass mandatory procedures simply by labeling their claims as "illegal" would introduce chaos and uncertainty into the tax system. The Legislature wisely established specific procedures to govern these disputes, and this Court should enforce them as written.

**FINAL RULING**

**PER CURIAM**

Before this Court are the closing arguments submitted by the parties regarding the appeal of Bluedog Capital Partners, LLC (Bluedog) from a judgment of the Superior Court granting the motion to dismiss filed by the defendants, John A. Murphy, David Quinn, and Michael Pearis (collectively, defendants). After a thorough review of the closing arguments presented by both the prosecution and the defense on behalf of the defendants, and assuming these arguments represent the entirety of the submissions before the Court, we affirm the judgment of the Superior Court.

**I. Standard of Review**

This Court reviews a trial court's decision on a motion to dismiss de novo. *Gannon v. City of Pawtucket*, 200 A.3d 1074, 1077 (R.I. 2019). In doing so, we "are confined to the four corners of the complaint and must assume all allegations are true, resolving any doubts in the plaintiff's favor." Id. (quoting *Narragansett Electric Co. v. Minardi*, 21 A.3d 274, 278 (R.I. 2011)). A motion to dismiss may be granted "only if it appears beyond a reasonable doubt that a plaintiff would not be entitled to relief under any conceivable set of facts." Id. Additionally, questions of statutory interpretation are reviewed de novo. *State v. Hazard*, 68 A.3d 479, 485 (R.I. 2013).

**II. Analysis**

The defendants argue, and the record supports, that Bluedog's complaint fails as a matter of law for three independent reasons: (1) the complaint alleges a mere overassessment rather than an illegal tax and thus fails to comply with the exclusive statutory procedures for tax abatement; (2) the complaint is time-barred by the statute of limitations; and (3) the request for injunctive relief is moot. The closing arguments submitted by the defendants, particularly the defense's rebuttal, provide compelling legal authority supporting these positions, which Bluedog has not rebutted in the provided materials.

**A. Failure to State a Claim for Illegal Tax**

The defendants contend that Bluedog has conceded its complaint alleges an overassessment rather than an illegal tax. Under Rhode Island law, an "illegal tax" exists only where the taxing authority acts without jurisdiction or where the tax is so palpably exorbitant as to amount to constructive fraud. Because Bluedog has conceded the City of Providence had jurisdiction to assess the property, the claim is properly characterized as an overassessment.

This distinction is dispositive because the Rhode Island General Laws provide exclusive procedures for challenging an overassessment. Specifically, G.L. 1956 §§ 44-5-26 and 44-5-27 mandate that a taxpayer must first appeal to the board of tax assessment review or bring an action in equity within three months. This Court has consistently held that the Uniform Declaratory Judgments Act cannot be used to circumvent these mandatory procedures. In *Narragansett Electric Co. v. Minardi*, 21 A.3d 274, 277 (R.I. 2011), we held that the Uniform Declaratory Judgments Act could not be used to replace the procedures set forth in §§ 44-5-26 and 44-5-27. Similarly, in *Pascale v. Capaldi*, 95 R.I. 513, 514, 188 A.2d 378, 379 (1963), we stated that "the legislature did not intend that a petition under the uniform declaratory judgments act was to take the place of a taxpayer's suit." As Bluedog has conceded it did not follow these statutory procedures, the Superior Court correctly determined that the complaint failed to state a claim upon which relief could be granted.

**B. Statute of Limitations**

The defendants further argue that the complaint is time-barred. Section 44-5-27 imposes a three-month statute of limitations for equity actions challenging tax assessments. The face of the complaint reveals that the tax sale occurred on July 1, 2015, and Bluedog filed its complaint on June 2, 2016—more than seven months after the limitations period expired for the 2015 tax year.

As we held in *Barrette v. Yakavonis*, 966 A.2d 1231, 1234 (R.I. 2009), a statute-of-limitations defense may be raised by way of a motion to dismiss "provided the alleged timing defect appears on the face of the complaint." Because the defect is apparent on the face of the complaint, and Bluedog has not contested this timing defect in its submissions, the dismissal of the action on this ground was proper.

**C. Mootness of Injunctive Relief**

Finally, the defendants argue that Bluedog's request for injunctive relief is moot. The tax sale was consummated on July 1, 2015, nearly a year before Bluedog filed its complaint. As the defendants correctly assert, there is no ongoing or imminent harm to enjoin because the property has already been sold to a third party. Courts lack jurisdiction to grant injunctive relief for injuries that have already been fully realized. Consequently, the Superior Court properly dismissed Count II of the complaint.

**III. Conclusion**

For the foregoing reasons, we find that the Superior Court did not err in granting the defendants' motion to dismiss. The defendants have demonstrated that Bluedog's complaint is legally insufficient due to the failure to comply with exclusive statutory procedures, the expiration of the applicable statute of limitations, and the mootness of the requested injunctive relief. Therefore, the judgment of the Superior Court is affirmed.
