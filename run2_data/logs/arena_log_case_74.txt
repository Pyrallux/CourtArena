Your Honor, the trial court correctly ruled that the Survival Statute preserved Plaintiff’s claim against PBS One. The law is unequivocal: when a corporation assumes a contractual obligation, that obligation constitutes a liability that exists from the time of assumption until satisfied. The Survival Statute, 755 ILCS 5/12-15, preserves claims against dissolved corporations for liabilities that existed at the time of dissolution, even if the breach occurs afterward. As the Illinois Supreme Court held in *In re Estate of Jones*, 171 Ill. 2d 423, 431 (1996), "the Survival Statute preserves claims that existed at the time of dissolution, even if the claim does not mature until after dissolution." Here, PBS One expressly assumed the Consulting Agreement obligations through the Asset Purchase Agreement and Assignment Agreement, creating a liability that persisted until dissolution. Schedule 3.1(t) of the Purchase Agreement specifically listed the Consulting Agreement as a liability, and the Assignment Agreement explicitly transferred the obligation to PBS One. Thus, from the time of assumption until dissolution, PBS One had an ongoing liability under the agreement. The breach occurred after dissolution, but the underlying obligation existed at dissolution, triggering the Survival Statute. This Court further confirmed in *Cimino v. City of Chicago*, 355 Ill. App. 3d 881, 885 (2005), that the Survival Statute applies to claims where the obligation existed prior to dissolution, regardless of when the breach occurred. Therefore, the trial court’s conclusion was legally sound, and we respectfully request that this Court affirm its ruling.

# DEFENSE OPENING RESPONSE

## Introduction and Statement of Issues

The defense respectfully submits this opening response on behalf of National Material, NM Holding (formerly CT One), PBS One, and Tang (collectively, "Defendants"). The plaintiff seeks to hold these Defendants liable for obligations arising under a Consulting Agreement originally executed in 1986 between Arthur Pielet and Pielet Bros. Scrap Iron and Metal, Inc. For the reasons set forth below, the trial court's ruling should be reversed as to Counts IX, X, and XI, and summary judgment should be entered in favor of Defendants.

---

## I. Response to Count XI: The Survival Statute Does Not Preserve Claims Against PBS One

### A. The Plaintiff's Survival Statute Argument Fails as a Matter of Law

The plaintiff contends that the Survival Statute, 755 ILCS 5/12-15, preserves Plaintiff's claim against PBS One because the Consulting Agreement obligation "existed" at the time of dissolution. This argument fundamentally misapprehends the nature of the obligation and the purpose of the Survival Statute.

The Survival Statute provides that "[n]o action pending in any court shall abate by reason of the death of any party * * *." However, the statute does not create new liabilities; it merely preserves claims that have already accrued. As the Illinois Supreme Court held in *In re Estate of Jones*, 171 Ill. 2d 423, 431 (1996), the statute preserves "claims that existed at the time of dissolution." The critical question is whether a claim—meaning a right to enforce an obligation—actually existed against PBS One at the time of its dissolution in 1994.

### B. The Obligation Was Transferred Before Dissolution

The undisputed facts demonstrate that any obligation PBS One may have had under the Consulting Agreement was transferred to Pielet LP (and subsequently to National Material) well before PBS One's dissolution in 1994. The April 12, 1988, Asset Purchase Agreement and Assignment Agreement effectuated a transfer of all assets and liabilities—including the Consulting Agreement obligation—from Pielet Inc. to PBS One, and then from PBS One to Pielet LP. Attorney Zavis's affidavit confirms that "the limited partnership was assuming [Pielet Corp.'s] obligations under the Consulting Agreement, that the limited partnership was substituting itself for [Pielet Corp.] under the Consulting Agreement, and would thereafter be making the payments to him in lieu of [Pielet Corp.]."

Critically, the evidence demonstrates that from 1988 onward, **Pielet LP—not PBS One—made the payments** under the Consulting Agreement. The plaintiff acknowledged in Count IX that "Pielet LP and Midwest Metallics" made payments under the Consulting Agreement, though the plaintiff asserted these entities "had never contracted to assume the Consulting Agreement." This fact alone demonstrates that PBS One was not the party performing under the agreement at the time of its dissolution.

### C. PBS One Had No Creditors at Dissolution

The defense further notes Tang's unrebutted testimony that at the time of PBS One's dissolution in 1994, "PBS One has no employees, no creditors. We don't owe anybody. We don't have anything. Just an investment." This is consistent with the corporate restructuring that had occurred years earlier, whereby PBS One's interest in Pielet LP was transferred to National Material in 1991.

Under Illinois law, a dissolved corporation's remaining liabilities are limited to those owed to existing creditors. See *In re Estate of Jones*, 171 Ill. 2d at 431 (holding that the Survival Statute preserves claims "that existed at the time of dissolution" but does not create new obligations). If PBS One had no creditors at the time of dissolution—because the obligation had been transferred and no party was asserting a claim—then no claim "existed" against PBS One that could be preserved.

### D. The Plaintiff's Reliance on *Cimino* Is Misplaced

The plaintiff cites *Cimino v. City of Chicago*, 355 Ill. App. 3d 881, 885 (2005), for the proposition that the Survival Statute applies "regardless of when the breach occurred." However, *Cimino* is distinguishable. In *Cimino*, the plaintiff alleged that the City had violated her civil rights during her employment, and the court held that the Survival Statute applied because the underlying obligation (to refrain from discrimination) existed throughout the employment relationship. Here, by contrast, the plaintiff does not allege a continuing tort; she alleges breach of a contract that had been assigned, assumed, and performed by other parties years before PBS One's dissolution.

The plaintiff cannot circumvent the requirement that a claim must have "existed" at dissolution by arguing that the mere possibility of future breach is sufficient. The Survival Statute does not preserve speculative or contingent claims; it preserves accrued claims. See *In re Estate of Jones*, 171 Ill. 2d at 431.

---

## II. Response to Count IX: National Material Did Not Assume the Consulting Agreement

### A. The Assumption Agreement Limited National Material's Assumption to Partnership Interests

Count IX alleges that National Material "assumed the right, title, interest[,] and obligations" of PBS One, including the Consulting Agreement. This claim fails as a matter of law because the Assumption Agreement did not encompass the Consulting Agreement obligation.

The December 31, 1990, Assumption Agreement explicitly states that National Material "does hereby accept and assume [PBS One's] right, title, interest[,] and obligations in and to the extent of 100% of [PBS One's] limited partnership interest in [Pielet LP] (representing 49.5% of the total outstanding partnership interest)." The agreement thus limited National Material's assumption to obligations **in its capacity as a limited partner**—that is, obligations arising from PBS One's partnership interest, not from the Consulting Agreement.

The distinction is critical. The Consulting Agreement was a personal service contract between Arthur and Pielet Inc. (and its successors). It was not an obligation "in" PBS One's limited partnership interest. The Assumption Agreement's reference to "obligations" must be read in context—it assumed obligations related to the partnership interest, not unrelated contractual obligations that happened to be listed on PBS One's books.

### B. The Consulting Agreement Was Not a Liability of the Partnership

Moreover, the partnership records demonstrate that the Consulting Agreement was treated as an obligation of Pielet LP, not of PBS One individually. After the 1988 restructuring, Pielet LP made payments to Arthur under the Consulting Agreement. After Pielet LP was renamed Midwest Metallics, the entity continued making payments. This demonstrates that the contracting party from 1988 forward was Pielet LP (and later Midwest Metallics), not PBS One.

The plaintiff cannot rewrite the Assumption Agreement to impose obligations that were never transferred. Under basic principles of contract interpretation, a successor only assumes those obligations expressly or implicitly included in the transfer. See *Chicago Investment Corp. v. Dolins*, 107 Ill. App. 3d 708, 713 (1982) (holding that a successor assumes only those obligations expressly assumed or necessarily implied from the transfer instrument).

---

## III. Response to Count X: National Material Is Not a Successor to PBS One

### A. National Material Is Not a Mere Continuation of PBS One

Count X alleges that National Material is liable as the "successor" of PBS One because it is a "mere continuation" of that entity. This claim fails because the evidence demonstrates that National Material is a distinct corporate entity that purchased PBS One's partnership interest for value, not a mere continuation.

Under Illinois law, the "mere continuation" doctrine applies only when there is no change in the ownership of the business and the new entity is merely a reincarnation of the old. See *Pabst Brewing Co. v. L. & A. Importing Co.*, 63 Ill. App. 3d 44, 50 (1978). Here, PBS One was owned by Tang, and National Material was controlled by Tang through CT One (later NM Holding). However, the transfer was a sale for value—National Material paid $5,542,000 for PBS One's partnership interest. This is not a "mere continuation"; it is a legitimate business transaction.

### B. Even If National Material Assumed Some Obligations, It Did Not Assume the Consulting Agreement

The plaintiff argues that National Material "expressly assumed" PBS One's obligations. However, as discussed in Section II above, the Assumption Agreement limited the assumption to obligations related to the partnership interest. The Consulting Agreement was not among those obligations.

Moreover, the plaintiff cannot rely on the doctrine of successor liability to impose obligations that were never transferred. The "successor" theory presupposes that the successor acquired the liabilities of the predecessor. Here, there is no evidence that National Material ever agreed to assume the Consulting Agreement obligation.

---

## Conclusion

For the foregoing reasons, the defense respectfully requests that this Court reverse the trial court's ruling on Counts IX, X, and XI, and enter summary judgment in favor of Defendants. The Survival Statute does not preserve claims against PBS One because no claim existed at the time of dissolution. National Material did not assume the Consulting Agreement, and National Material is not a successor to PBS One. The plaintiff has failed to state a claim upon which relief can be granted, and the defense is entitled to judgment as a matter of law.

**I. INTRODUCTION**

**II. ANALYSIS OF COUNT XI (SURVIVAL STATUTE)**

The Court affirms the trial court's ruling regarding Count XI. The plaintiff argues that the Survival Statute, 755 ILCS 5/12-15, preserves the claim against PBS One because the contractual obligation existed at the time of dissolution, even though the breach occurred afterward. The defense contends that PBS One had no creditors at the time of dissolution because the obligation had been transferred to Pielet LP.

As the Illinois Supreme Court held in *In re Estate of Jones*, 171 Ill. 2d 423, 431 (1996), "the Survival Statute preserves claims that existed at the time of dissolution, even if the claim does not mature until after dissolution." Here, PBS One's liability under the Consulting Agreement was a fixed obligation that existed in June 1994 when the corporation was dissolved. The defense's reliance on Tang's testimony that PBS One had "no creditors" is insufficient to overcome the express assumption of liability documented in the 1988 agreements and the lack of a novation. The Court reverses the trial court's ruling regarding Count IX. This count alleges that National Material assumed the obligations under the Consulting Agreement. The Consulting Agreement was an obligation of the business entity (Pielet LP), evidenced by the fact that Pielet LP, not PBS One, made the monthly payments to Arthur. App. 3d 708, 713 (1982) (holding that a successor assumes only those obligations expressly assumed or necessarily implied from the transfer instrument). The Assumption Agreement does not mention the Consulting Agreement, nor does it contain language broad enough to encompass the general liabilities of the partnership entity. Instead, it is narrowly tailored to the "limited partnership interest." Consequently, National Material did not expressly assume the Consulting Agreement, and Count IX cannot stand.

The Court reverses the trial court's ruling regarding Count X. This count alleges that National Material is liable as a "successor" to PBS One under the theory of "mere continuation." Therefore, National Material is not a mere continuation of PBS One, and successor liability does not attach.

For the foregoing reasons, the Court affirms the trial court's ruling on Count XI but reverses the rulings on Counts IX and X. The plaintiff’s claim against PBS One is preserved by the Survival Statute; however, National Material is neither liable for having expressly assumed the Consulting Agreement nor liable as a successor to PBS One.

**FINAL RULING**

**I. INTRODUCTION**

This matter is before the Court on appeals regarding Counts IX, X, and XI of the plaintiff’s complaint. The Court has reviewed the closing arguments submitted by both the prosecution and the defense. Based on the undisputed facts provided and the applicable law, the Court updates its preliminary ruling as follows.

**II. ANALYSIS OF COUNT IX (EXPRESS ASSUMPTION)**

The Court affirms its reversal of the trial court’s ruling on Count IX. The plaintiff argues that National Material expressly assumed the obligations under the Consulting Agreement. However, the defense correctly identifies that the 1990 Assumption Agreement strictly limited the scope of the assumption.

The December 31, 1990, Assumption Agreement states that National Material "does hereby accept and assume [PBS One's] right, title, interest[,] and obligations in and to the extent of 100% of [PBS One's] limited partnership interest in [Pielet LP]." The Consulting Agreement was an obligation of the business entity (Pielet LP), not an obligation inherent to PBS One's status as a limited partner. As established in *Chicago Investment Corp. v. Dolins*, 107 Ill. App. 3d 708, 713 (1982), a successor assumes only those obligations that are expressly assumed or necessarily implied from the transfer instrument. The Assumption Agreement does not mention the Consulting Agreement, nor does it contain language broad enough to encompass the general liabilities of the partnership entity. Therefore, National Material did not expressly assume the Consulting Agreement, and Count IX fails.

**III. ANALYSIS OF COUNT X (SUCCESSOR LIABILITY)**

The Court affirms its reversal of the trial court’s ruling on Count X. The plaintiff alleges that National Material is liable as a "successor" to PBS One under the theory of "mere continuation." The defense argues, and the record supports, that this theory is inapplicable because National Material purchased PBS One's interest for valuable consideration.

The "mere continuation" exception typically applies when there is a continuity of ownership, management, and personnel, and the successor is merely a reincarnation of the predecessor without a bona fide sale. See *Pabst Brewing Co. v. L. & A. Importing Co.*, 63 Ill. App. 3d 44, 50 (1978). Here, the undisputed facts show that National Material paid $5,542,000.00 to acquire PBS One's limited partnership interest. This was a bona fide purchase for value. Consequently, National Material is not a mere continuation of PBS One, and successor liability does not attach.

**IV. ANALYSIS OF COUNT XI (SURVIVAL STATUTE)**

The Court affirms the trial court’s ruling on Count XI. The defense argues that the Survival Statute, 755 ILCS 5/12-15, does not preserve the plaintiff's claim against PBS One because PBS One had no creditors at the time of dissolution and the 1988 restructuring effected a novation.

**A. Existence of Creditor at Dissolution**

The defense contends that PBS One had no creditors at dissolution, relying on Tang's testimony that the corporation had "no employees, no creditors." However, this assertion is contradicted by the documentary evidence. The 1988 Assignment Agreement expressly lists the Consulting Agreement as an "Assumed Liability" under Schedule 3.1(t). As held by the Illinois Supreme Court in *In re Estate of Jones*, 171 Ill. 2d 423, 431 (1996), the Survival Statute "preserves claims that existed at the time of dissolution, even if the claim does not mature until after dissolution." The obligation to pay Arthur under the Consulting Agreement was a fixed liability that PBS One assumed in 1988 and that remained in effect in June 1994 when PBS One was dissolved. The fact that the breach (cessation of payments) occurred in 1998 does not negate the existence of the liability at the time of dissolution.

**B. Absence of Novation**

The defense argues that a novation occurred in 1988, implied by the conduct of the parties—specifically, that Arthur accepted payments from Pielet LP for ten years. While *Bank of Ravenswood v. Zawistoski*, 218 Ill. App. 3d 860, 869 (1991), holds that novation may be implied from circumstances and conduct, it requires the consent of the creditor to discharge the original obligor.

The prosecution correctly points to the testimony of Attorney Zavis, who represented the parties during the restructuring and communicated directly with Arthur. Zavis explicitly stated that he did not intend to remove the original obligors and that Arthur was not asked to release his rights against them. Zavis testified: "Nobody ever asked you to get a novation from him? A. No." and "I didn't have any intention of removing them [Pielet Corp.], but I couldn't remove them anyhow unless they agreed." This testimony establishes that Arthur, the creditor, did not agree to release PBS One or Pielet Corp. from liability. Therefore, despite the change in which entity made the payments, no novation occurred.

**C. Distinguishing *Cimino***

The defense relies on *Cimino v. City of Chicago*, 355 Ill. App. 3d 881, 885 (2005), to argue that the Survival Statute does not apply. However, *Cimino* involved a continuing tort where liability arose after dissolution. Here, the duty was a fixed contractual obligation that had been expressly assumed in 1988. The cessation of payments in 1998 was a breach of that pre-existing obligation. Thus, *Cimino* is inapplicable, and the Survival Statute preserves the plaintiff's claim.

**V. CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, the Court affirms the trial court's ruling on Count XI but reverses the rulings on Counts IX and X. The plaintiff’s claim against PBS One is preserved by the Survival Statute; however, National Material is neither liable for having expressly assumed the Consulting Agreement nor liable as a successor to PBS One.
