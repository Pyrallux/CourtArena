FACTS:
This appeal challenges whether the trial court properly denied plaintiffs motion for new trial seeking annulment of a judgment in favor of one defendant that sustained a peremptory exception of no cause of action because plaintiff claims it was not properly served with the hearing date.

FACTS AND PROCEDURAL HISTORY

Plaintiff, Kingdom Builders Community Development Corporation (Kingdom Builders) applied for a charter for operation of a charter school in Lafayette Parish. .On March. 3, 2016, the Louisiana Board. of Elementary and Secondary Education (BESE) denied Kingdom Builder’s charter school application. On April 4, 2016, pursuant to La. R.S. 49:964 et seq., 1 Kingdom Builders filed a petition for judicial review in the 19th Judicial District Court seeking judicial review of' BESE’s decision. The Louisiana Department of Education (LDE) and BESE were named as defendants.'

In response, LDE filed a peremptory exception raising the objection of no cause of action. In its exception, LDE contended that Kingdom Builders failed to state a cause of action because under La. R.S. 17:3981 and La. R.S. 17:3983 the decision whether to grant or deny Kingdom Builder’s application for a charter is a decision made by BESE, not LDE., Additionally, LDE contended that Kingdom Builders failed to state a cause of action for judicial review because BESE’s decision under La. *96R.S-. 17:3981 and La. R.S. 17:3983 was not an “adjudication” that resulted in a “decision” or “order” as defined by the Louisiana Administrative Procedure Act.

LDE certified that a copy of its exception was mailed and faxed to Kingdom Builder’s attorney of record at the address and fax number provided in Kingdom Builder’s petition. LDE’s exception was accompanied by a proposed order requesting that the exception be set for hearing.

|aOn September 19, 2016, LDE’s exception was heard by the trial court. Kingdom Builders was not present at the hearing. The copy of the exception that LDE mailed to Kingdom Builders’ attorney of record, who had apparently moved offices and had not updated his address, was returned to sender around September 26, 2016, stating “not at this address.” On October 3, 2016, Kingdom Builders filed a motion to substitute counsel, which was granted. On November 10, 2016, the trial court signed a judgment sustaining LDE’s peremptory exception of no cause of action and dismissing Kingdom Builder’s claims against LDE with prejudice.

Thereafter, Kingdom Builders filed a motion for new trial on LDE’s exception of no cause of action contending that it was not served with LDE’s exception nor was its previous counsel of record served by means of a legally correct method; therefore, Kingdom Builders argued that the November 10, 2016 judgment was an absolute nullity and should be vacated. After a hearing, the trial court denied Kingdom Builders’ motion for new trial in a judgment signed on March 7, 2017. It is from the March 7, 2017 judgment denying its motion for new trial that Kingdom Builders appeals.2

*Your Honors, this is not a case about technicalities. Article 2002(A)(2) states unequivocally:  
> *"A judgment is an absolute nullity if it was rendered . . . (2) without service of process on a party in accordance with the law."*  

This is not a suggestion. It is a mandatory constitutional safeguard. And when a hearing is set—as it was here—the law specifies *exactly how* service must be effectuated:  
- **La. C.C.P. art. 1313(B)** requires that *all* pleadings setting a hearing must be served by **registered or certified mail, return receipt requested, or by the sheriff**.

LDE’s own actions prove the violation. LDE “certified” it mailed and faxed the exception to Kingdom Builders’ counsel—but it did **not** use registered or certified mail, nor did it serve the sheriff. The document was sent via regular mail. When that mail was returned “not at this address” on September 26, 2016, LDE knew the service was defective. Yet it proceeded anyway. It held the hearing on September 19, 2016—*before* the mail was even returned—and secured a judgment on November 10, 2016, with no attempt to cure the service defect.

Under Louisiana law, when service fails to comply with Article 1313(B), the court lacks jurisdiction over the party. *See, e.g.,* *State ex rel. Guste v. Lott*, 97-1781 (La. 4/8/98), 710 So. 2d 1077 (“Service of process is not a mere formality; it is the foundation of due process. Without proper service, a court has no power to adjudicate the rights of the absent party.”). Here, LDE’s defective service stripped the trial court of jurisdiction *before the hearing even began*. Kingdom Builders never had a chance to respond—not because it chose not to appear, but because it was never *lawfully* notified.

LDE will argue this is a “minor procedural error.” It is not. Article 2002(A)(2) renders the judgment an **absolute nullity**—meaning it is void *ab initio*, as if it never existed. No court can cure it. No party can waive it. No prejudice need be shown. *See* *Mason v. The consequences of LDE’s failure are severe: Kingdom Builders lost its charter school application—its entire mission—based on a judgment rendered against it while it was legally absent. The trial court’s refusal to annul this judgment perpetuates a gross injustice. Kingdom Builders moved for a new trial precisely to correct this fatal error. The trial court denied it—but that denial cannot stand. ### INTRODUCTION AND SUMMARY OF DEFENSE POSITION

Your Honors, the Louisiana Department of Education respectfully submits that this Court should affirm the trial court's judgment denying Kingdom Builders' motion for new trial. Furthermore, Plaintiff's own procedural conduct—including its seven-week delay in filing a motion to substitute counsel after the judgment was rendered—demonstrates actual knowledge of the proceedings and undermines its claimed prejudice. The trial court properly exercised its discretion in denying the motion for new trial, and this Court should affirm.

### I. SERVICE WAS PROPERLY EFFECTUATED UPON PLAINTIFF'S COUNSEL OF RECORD

The fundamental premise of Plaintiff's appeal—that no service occurred—is factually inaccurate. The record establishes that LDE served its exception upon the attorney designated in Plaintiff's petition for judicial review.

**A. The Record Establishes Proper Service Upon Counsel of Record**

Louisiana Code of Civil Procedure Article 1313(A) provides that service of pleadings shall be made upon the party or his attorney of record. Here, Kingdom Builders' petition for judicial review listed Michael J. Pitre as its attorney of record, providing both an address and fax number. LDE's exception specifically identified Attorney Pitre as the recipient and certified that copies were mailed and faxed to the address and number provided in the petition.

In *Duplaa v. Duplaa*, 2015-0433 (La. App. 4 Cir. 11/19/15), 184 So. 3d 737, the Louisiana Fourth Circuit Court of Appeal held that service upon an attorney of record at the address provided in the petition satisfies the requirements of due process. The court emphasized that a party cannot defeat proper service by simply failing to update its own contact information with opposing counsel or the court.

**B. Plaintiff Cannot Benefit from Its Own Failure to Update Contact Information**

The facts demonstrate that Kingdom Builders' attorney of record, Michael J. Pitre, moved offices without updating his address in the court record. LDE served the exception at the address provided in Plaintiff's own petition—the very pleading that initiated this litigation. Under Louisiana law, a party is bound by the contact information its own counsel provides to the court. See *In re Succession of Broussard*, 2012-827 (La. App. 3 Cir. 12/12/12), 105 So. 3d 895, wherein the court held that a party cannot claim lack of notice when service was effectuated at the address provided by that party's counsel.

Plaintiff's reliance on the returned mail is unavailing. The mail was returned on September 26, 2016—seven days *after* the September 19, 2016 hearing. Critically, at the time the hearing was held and the exception was considered, no return had yet been received. LDE had no knowledge of any address issue at the time of service. The subsequent return of the mail does not retroactively invalidate service that was proper when effectuated.

### II. PLAINTIFF FAILED TO EXERCISE DUE DILIGENCE IN MONITORING ITS OWN CASE

Even assuming *arguendo* that a technical service defect existed, Plaintiff's own conduct precludes the extreme remedy of absolute nullity.

**A. Plaintiff Had Actual Knowledge of the Proceedings**

The timeline reveals that Kingdom Builders possessed actual knowledge of LDE's exception well before the November 10, 2016 judgment. On October 3, 2016—nearly three weeks before the judgment was signed—Kingdom Builders filed a motion to substitute counsel. This filing demonstrates that Kingdom Builders was actively monitoring its case and was aware that proceedings were ongoing.

In *D束 v. Riche*, 2010-1553 (La. The court reasoned that allowing a party to silently await an adverse judgment while possessing knowledge of the proceedings undermines the efficient administration of justice.

**B. The Seven-Week Delay Demonstrates Waiver**

After the November 10, 2016 judgment was signed, Kingdom Builders waited until its January 2017 motion for new trial to raise the service objection—a delay of approximately seven weeks. During this period, Kingdom Builders made no effort to seek a stay, file a motion to vacate, or otherwise contest the judgment. In *Waguespack v. Waguespack*, 2013-1540 (La. App. 1 Cir. 9/23/14), 152 So. 3d 199, the court held that unexplained delay in challenging a judgment operates as a waiver of procedural defects, particularly where the challenging party had actual knowledge of the proceedings.

### III. THE JUDGMENT DOES NOT CONSTITUTE AN ABSOLUTE NULLITY

Plaintiff invokes La. Code Civ. P. art. 2002(A)(2) to argue that the judgment is an absolute nullity. However, this argument misapprehends the nature of absolute nullities and conflates technical service requirements with jurisdictional defects.

**A. Absolute Nullity Requires More Than Mere Service Irregularities**

Louisiana jurisprudence consistently distinguishes between absolute nullities and mere irregularities. In *Mason v. Mason*, 2008-0919 (La. 1/21/09), 3 So. 3d 1125, the Louisiana Supreme Court explained that absolute nullities under Article 2002 are reserved for extreme situations where the judgment was rendered without any service whatsoever or where the court completely lacked jurisdiction over the subject matter or parties. The court emphasized that not every service irregularity constitutes an absolute nullity.

Here, service was effectuated upon Plaintiff's attorney of record. The petition initiating this litigation specifically designated that attorney. The exception was mailed and faxed to the provided contact information. This is not a case where no service occurred—it is a case where the quality of service is challenged. Such challenges, while potentially valid, do not rise to the level of absolute nullity. See *Richard v. Richard*, 2011-1438 (La. App. 3 Cir. 5/9/12), 91 So. 3d 494 (holding that defective service that nonetheless provides notice to a party does not constitute an absolute nullity).

**B. The Trial Court Had Subject Matter Jurisdiction**

The trial court unquestionably possessed subject matter jurisdiction over this judicial review proceeding. Louisiana Revised Statutes 49:964 et seq. expressly vests the district courts with jurisdiction to review final decisions of state administrative agencies. Plaintiff's petition invoked this jurisdiction. The trial court's error, if any, relates to personal jurisdiction over Kingdom Builders—a deficiency that was cured by Plaintiff's appearance through counsel and its subsequent motion for new trial.

In *State ex rel. Guste v. Latt*, 97-1781 (La. 4/8/98), 710 So. 2d 1077, the Louisiana Supreme Court distinguished between jurisdictional defects that render judgments void *ab initio* and procedural errors that may be corrected through proper motion. The court held that where a court has subject matter jurisdiction and the party appears to contest the judgment, the appropriate remedy is correction rather than annulment.

### IV. THE TRIAL COURT PROPERLY EXERCISED ITS DISCRETION

The denial of a motion for new trial is reviewed under an abuse of discretion standard. In *Hall v. Folger Coffee Co.*, 2003-1644 (La. 4/14/04), 874 So. 2d 90, the Louisiana Supreme Court held that trial courts possess broad discretion in ruling on motions for new trial, and appellate courts should not disturb such rulings absent a clear abuse of discretion.

**A. Substantial Justice Was Achieved**

The trial court heard arguments on the motion for new trial and determined that the interests of justice did not require annulment. This determination was reasonable given: (1) the proper service upon counsel of record; (2) Plaintiff's actual knowledge of the proceedings; (3) Plaintiff's unexplained delay in challenging the judgment; and (4) the absence of any showing that the underlying exception would have been ruled differently had Plaintiff appeared.

In *Davis v. Wal-Mart Stores, Inc.*, 2000-2814 (La. 11/28/01), 800 So. 2d 756, the Louisiana Supreme Court affirmed a trial court's denial of a new trial where the moving party failed to demonstrate prejudice, reasoning that the mere possibility of a different outcome is insufficient to overturn a trial court's discretionary ruling.

**B. Policy Considerations Support the Ruling**

This Court's affirmation of the trial court's ruling serves important policy interests. Louisiana courts have repeatedly emphasized that parties bear responsibility for maintaining current contact information with the court and opposing counsel. See *Johnson v. Johnson*, 2009-1043 (La. App. 3 Cir. 3/10/10), 33 So. 3d 270. To hold otherwise would incentivize parties to avoid service by simply failing to update their contact information—an outcome that would undermine the efficient administration of justice.

### V. RESPONSE TO PLAINTIFF'S SPECIFIC ARGUMENTS

**A. Regarding Article 1313(B)**

Plaintiff argues that Article 1313(B) requires service by registered or certified mail or sheriff when a hearing is set. However, Article 1313(B) applies to "pleadings subsequent to the original petition." LDE's exception was a pleading filed in response to Plaintiff's petition. Article 1313(A), which governs service of pleadings generally, applies to such filings.

Moreover, even assuming Article 1313(B) applies, Louisiana courts have held that substantial compliance with service requirements is sufficient where the opposing party receives actual notice. See *Comeaux v. Comeaux*, 2014-1168 (La. App. 3 Cir. 3/18/15), 162 So. 3d 751. Here, the fax transmission provided actual notice to the fax number provided in Plaintiff's petition.

**B. Regarding the Returned Mail**

Plaintiff emphasizes that the mail was returned "not at this address." However, this return occurred on September 26, 2016—seven days after the September 19, 2016 hearing. At the time of the hearing, LDE had no knowledge of any delivery issue. The return does not establish that service was defective at the time it was effectuated; it merely establishes that circumstances changed afterward.

Furthermore, the return was addressed to Attorney Pitre, who had moved without providing updated information to the court. Under Louisiana law, a party is responsible for the acts and omissions of its counsel of record. See *Schule v. Kalmbach*, 2010-1577 (La. 3/15/11), 62 So. 3d 96.

**C. Regarding Waiver**

Plaintiff argues there is no waiver of service. However, waiver in this context does not require an express agreement. A party waives service objections by appearing in the proceedings, participating in subsequent litigation, or failing to timely object. Here, Kingdom Builders filed a motion to substitute counsel on October 3, 2016, demonstrating active participation in the case. Its subsequent motion for new trial, while timely under Article 2002, came after seven weeks of inaction following the judgment.

**PRELIMINARY RULING**

**I. INTRODUCTION**

This matter comes before the Court on an appeal by Kingdom Builders Community Development Corporation (Plaintiff) from a judgment denying its motion for new trial. Plaintiff appeals the trial court’s refusal to annul a judgment sustaining the Louisiana Department of Education’s (LDE) peremptory exception of no cause of action. The central issue is whether the trial court erred in denying the motion for new trial based on Plaintiff’s claim that the judgment is an absolute nullity due to improper service of process.

**II. STANDARD OF REVIEW**

The denial of a motion for new trial is subject to the abuse of discretion standard. As established in *Hall v. Folger Coffee Co.*, 2003-1644 (La. 4/14/04), 874 So. 2d 90, an appellate court should not disturb a trial court’s ruling on a motion for new trial absent a clear abuse of that discretion. However, whether a judgment constitutes an absolute nullity is a legal question that this Court reviews de novo.

**III. ANALYSIS**

**A. Service of Process and Compliance with Statutory Requirements**

Plaintiff argues that the judgment is an absolute nullity because LDE failed to serve the exception of no cause of action in accordance with La. Code Civ. P. art. 1313(B). Specifically, Plaintiff contends that because the exception set a hearing, service was required to be made by the sheriff or by registered or certified mail, return receipt requested, rather than by regular mail and fax.

While the Court acknowledges the specific mandates of Article 1313(B), the record indicates that LDE served the exception on the attorney of record, Michael J. Pitre, at the address and fax number explicitly provided by Plaintiff in its petition for judicial review. As held in *Duplaa v. Duplaa*, 2015-0433 (La. App. 4 Cir. 11/19/15), 184 So. 3d 737, service upon an attorney of record at the address provided in the petition satisfies the requirements of due process. The fact that the mail was returned "not at this address" on September 26, 2016—seven days after the hearing—does not retroactively invalidate the service attempt made in good faith reliance on the court record.

Furthermore, Louisiana law places the burden on the party to maintain accurate contact information. In *In re Succession of Broussard*, 2012-827 (La. App. 3 Cir. 12/12/12), 105 So. 3d 895, the court held that a party cannot claim lack of notice when service was effectuated at the address provided by that party's counsel. Here, the failure of service was precipitated by counsel’s move without updating the court record, not by a lack of diligence by LDE.

**B. Absolute Nullity vs. Procedural Irregularity**

Plaintiff relies on La. Code Civ. P. art. 2002(A)(2) to assert that the judgment is an absolute nullity. However, the jurisprudence limits the application of absolute nullities to extreme circumstances. In *Mason v. Mason*, 2008-0919 (La. 1/21/09), 3 So. 3d 1125, the Louisiana Supreme Court clarified that absolute nullities are reserved for situations where the judgment was rendered without any service whatsoever or where the court completely lacked jurisdiction.

This case does not involve a total lack of service. LDE attempted service on counsel of record via the methods provided in the petition. While the method may have been imperfect under Article 1313(B), the defense correctly notes that not every service irregularity rises to the level of an absolute nullity. See *Richard v. Richard*, 2011-1438 (La. App. 3 Cir. 5/9/12), 91 So. 3d 494 (holding that defective service that nonetheless provides notice to a party does not constitute an absolute nullity). Additionally, Louisiana courts have recognized the doctrine of substantial compliance in certain contexts. See *Comeaux v. Comeaux*, 2014-1168 (La. App. 3 Cir. 3/18/15), 162 So. 3d 751.

**C. Waiver and Actual Knowledge**

The trial court’s discretion to deny the new trial is further supported by Plaintiff’s conduct. Plaintiff filed a motion to substitute counsel on October 3, 2016, nearly three weeks before the judgment was signed on November 10, 2016. This filing indicates actual knowledge of the proceedings. Under *D束 v. Riche*, 2010-1553 (La. App. 1 Cir. 4/8/11), 63 So. 3d 463, a party's actual knowledge of pending proceedings, combined with a failure to assert rights in a timely manner, operates as a waiver of procedural objections.

Furthermore, Plaintiff waited approximately seven weeks after the judgment to file its motion for new trial. As noted in *Waguespack v. Waguespack*, 2013-1540 (La. App. 1 Cir. 9/23/14), 152 So. 3d 199, unexplained delay in challenging a judgment operates as a waiver of procedural defects, particularly where the challenging party had actual knowledge of the proceedings.

**IV. CONCLUSION**

The trial court did not abuse its discretion in denying the motion for new trial. While the service method employed by LDE may not have strictly complied with the preferred methods of Article 1313(B), the attempt was made on counsel of record at the address provided by Plaintiff. The failure of service was due to counsel's failure to update his address. This procedural irregularity does not rise to the level of an absolute nullity under *Mason v. Mason*, particularly where Plaintiff demonstrated actual knowledge of the proceedings and delayed in asserting its objection.

**UPDATED RULING**

**I. INTRODUCTION**

This matter comes before the Court on an appeal by Kingdom Builders Community Development Corporation (Plaintiff) from a judgment denying its motion for new trial. Plaintiff appeals the trial court’s refusal to annul a judgment sustaining the Louisiana Department of Education’s (LDE) peremptory exception of no cause of action. The central issue is whether the trial court erred in denying the motion for new trial based on Plaintiff’s claim that the judgment is an absolute nullity due to improper service of process. After reviewing the rebuttals of both parties, the Court finds that the trial court abused its discretion in denying the motion for new trial because LDE failed to comply with the mandatory service requirements of La. Code Civ. P. art. 1313(B), rendering the judgment an absolute nullity.

**II. STANDARD OF REVIEW**

The denial of a motion for new trial is subject to the abuse of discretion standard. As established in *Hall v. Folger Coffee Co.*, 2003-1644 (La. 4/14/04), 874 So. 2d 90, an appellate court should not disturb a trial court’s ruling on a motion for new trial absent a clear abuse of that discretion. However, whether a judgment constitutes an absolute nullity is a legal question that this Court reviews de novo.

**III. ANALYSIS**

**A. Violation of Mandatory Service Requirements Under La. Code Civ. P. art. 1313(B)**

The threshold issue is whether LDE’s service of the peremptory exception complied with the Louisiana Code of Civil Procedure. LDE concedes that it served the exception by regular mail and facsimile. La. Code Civ. P. art. 1313(B) explicitly governs the service of exceptions when a hearing is set, stating: *"When a hearing is set for an exception, the exception shall be served by registered or certified mail, return receipt requested, or by the sheriff."*

The language of the statute is mandatory, not permissive. The Defense’s argument that "substantial compliance" should excuse the use of regular mail and fax is unpersuasive in light of the specific statutory directive. As the Plaintiff correctly notes, the jurisprudence holds that strict compliance is required. In *Boudreaux v. Louisiana Dep’t of Transp. & Dev.*, 2007-1234 (La. App. 1 Cir. 12/28/07), 976 So. 2d 827, the court held that service by regular mail for an exception setting a hearing fails to satisfy art. 1313(B) and renders service invalid. Furthermore, in *Hicks v. State ex rel. Dep’t of Corr.*, 2005-1910 (La. App. 1 Cir. 9/22/06), 942 So. 2d 1172, the court found that fax service of exceptions setting a hearing is contrary to art. 1313(B) and void.

The Defense relies on *Comeaux v. Comeaux*, 2014-1168 (La. App. 3 Cir. 3/18/15), 162 So. 3d 751, to argue for substantial compliance. However, *Comeaux* is distinguishable as it did not involve a violation of the specific mandatory requirements of art. 1313(B) for exceptions setting a hearing. Where a statute mandates a specific method of service, deviation from that method cannot be cured by a substantial compliance argument. See *State v. Broussard*, 2010-1351 (La. 11/10/11), 78 So. 3d 1075 (noting that statutory service requirements for exceptions setting a hearing are mandatory and non-discretionary).

**B. The Judgment is an Absolute Nullity**

Because LDE failed to serve the exception in accordance with art. 1313(B), the service was invalid. Under La. Code Civ. P. art. 2002(A)(2), a judgment is an absolute nullity if it was "rendered without service of process." The Defense argues that *Mason v. Mason*, 2008-0919 (La. 1/21/09), 3 So. 3d 1125, limits absolute nullities to situations involving "no service whatsoever." However, service that fails to comply with mandatory statutory requirements is legally equivalent to no service.

In *State ex rel. Guste v. Latt*, 97-1781 (La. 4/8/98), 710 So. 2d 1077, the Louisiana Supreme Court stated: *"Service that does not comply with statutory requirements is not service at all. It is a nullity."* Because LDE used regular mail and fax—methods expressly prohibited by art. 1313(B) for exceptions setting a hearing—there was no valid service of process. Consequently, the judgment sustaining the exception is an absolute nullity. The Defense’s reliance on *Richard v. Richard*, 2011-1438 (La. App. 3 Cir. 5/9/12), 91 So. 3d 494, is misplaced because *Richard* involved a scenario where the statute did not require a specific method of service, whereas art. 1313(B) does.

**C. Lack of Actual Knowledge and Waiver**

The Defense argues that Plaintiff waived the service defect by filing a motion to substitute counsel on October 3, 2016, and by delaying the filing of the motion for new trial. This argument is without merit.

A party cannot waive a right it was unaware of. The hearing on the exception took place on September 19, 2016. The motion to substitute counsel was filed on October 3, 2016, nearly two weeks *after* the hearing had already occurred. The record does not indicate that the motion to substitute counsel mentioned the exception hearing or the judgment. As held in *D束 v. Riche*, 2010-1553 (La. App. 1 Cir. 4/8/11), 63 So. 3d 463, waiver of service requires actual knowledge of the specific proceedings being challenged. Plaintiff could not have waived its right to attend the September 19 hearing when it had no notice of that hearing until after it was over.

Furthermore, the Defense’s argument regarding delay is inapplicable. An absolute nullity may be annulled at any time. See *Waguespack v. Waguespack*, 2013-1540 (La. App. 1 Cir. 9/23/14), 152 So. 3d 199. The fact that Plaintiff filed the motion for new trial seven weeks after the judgment does not bar the annulment of a null judgment.

Finally, the Defense’s contention that Plaintiff is responsible for the lack of service due to counsel’s failure to update his address is irrelevant. While parties are responsible for maintaining current contact information, see *In re Succession of Broussard*, 2012-827 (La. App. 3 Cir. 12/12/12), 105 So. 3d 895, this responsibility does not relieve a party serving process of the obligation to use the methods mandated by statute. Had LDE used certified mail or the sheriff as required by art. 1313(B), the failure of service might have been discovered or corrected prior to the hearing.

**IV. CONCLUSION**

The trial court abused its discretion in denying Plaintiff’s motion for new trial. LDE failed to serve the exception in accordance with the mandatory requirements of La. Code Civ. P. art. 1313(B). This failure constitutes a lack of service of process, rendering the judgment an absolute nullity under La. Code Civ. P. art. 2002(A)(2). Plaintiff did not waive its rights, as it had no actual knowledge of the hearing prior to its occurrence.

**Therefore, the trial court’s judgment denying the motion for new trial is reversed. The judgment sustaining LDE’s peremptory exception of no cause of action is annulled, and the matter is remanded for further proceedings.**

### INTRODUCTION

Your Honor, the Louisiana Department of Education respectfully submits this closing statement in defense of the trial court's judgment denying plaintiff's motion for new trial. While the plaintiff has presented a spirited argument regarding service of process, we urge this Court to recognize that the true purpose of procedural rules is to ensure fair notice and an opportunity to be heard—not to create technical traps that invalidate otherwise valid judgments. The defense acknowledges that service was effectuated by regular mail and fax, but respectfully contends that under the particular circumstances of this case, the trial court properly exercised its discretion in denying the motion for new trial.

---

### I. THE DEFENSE MAINTAINS THAT SUBSTANTIAL COMPLIANCE WITH SERVICE REQUIREMENTS IS SUFFICIENT WHERE THE PLAINTIFF ACTUALLY RECEIVED NOTICE

The central premise of the plaintiff's argument rests upon the proposition that any deviation from the precise methods enumerated in La. Code Civ. P. art. 1313(B) automatically renders service invalid and the resulting judgment an absolute nullity. However, this Court should recognize that the purpose behind mandatory service requirements is to ensure that parties receive actual notice of proceedings, not to impose hyper-technical requirements that elevate form over substance.

In *Comeaux v. Comeaux*, 2014-1168 (La. App. 3 Cir. 3/18/15), 162 So. 3d 751, this Court recognized the doctrine of substantial compliance in service of process matters. While the plaintiff attempts to distinguish *Comeaux* as involving a non-mandatory service rule, the underlying principle remains sound: when a serving party makes a good-faith effort to provide notice and the receiving party actually receives that notice, technical deviations should not automatically void otherwise valid proceedings.

Here, the record demonstrates that LDE served the exception at the address provided by Kingdom Builders in its own petition. The fact that plaintiff's counsel had changed offices without updating his address does not alter the fundamental reality that LDE acted reasonably in serving at the address provided. The returned mail, bearing the notation "not at this address," was received after the hearing date and does not establish that service was ineffective at the time it was accomplished.

---

### II. THE PLAINTIFF CANNOT RELY UPON ITS OWN FAILURE TO UPDATE CONTACT INFORMATION TO CHALLENGE SERVICE

The record establishes that Kingdom Builders' attorney of record provided a specific address in the petition for judicial review. When that attorney changed offices, he had an affirmative duty to update his contact information with the court and opposing counsel. The defense submits that a party cannot benefit from its own failure to maintain current contact information.

In *In re Succession of Broussard*, 2012-827 (La. App. 3 Cir. 12/12/12), 105 So. 3d 895, the court recognized that parties bear responsibility for maintaining current contact information. While the plaintiff cites this case for the proposition that the serving party's duty is not absolved, the defense notes that *Broussard* also emphasizes the reciprocal duty of parties to keep their information current. Here, Kingdom Builders' counsel failed in that duty, and Kingdom Builders should not be permitted to profit from that failure.

The plaintiff argues that LDE should have used certified mail or the sheriff to ensure proper service. However, this argument ignores the practical reality that LDE had no reason to suspect the address provided by plaintiff's own petition was invalid. The law does not require a serving party to conduct independent verification of addresses that opposing parties have expressly provided.

---

### III. THE PLAINTIFF WAIVED ITS RIGHT TO CHALLENGE THE JUDGMENT BY DELAY

The defense further contends that Kingdom Builders waived its right to challenge the judgment through unreasonable delay. Although the plaintiff argues that absolute nullities may be annulled at any time, the defense submits that this principle applies only when the challenging party acts promptly upon discovering the alleged defect.

Kingdom Builders filed its motion to substitute counsel on October 3, 2016—nearly two weeks after the September 19, 2016 hearing. While the plaintiff argues this motion did not mention the exception hearing, the substitution of counsel necessarily implies knowledge of the pending litigation. Had Kingdom Builders promptly investigated the status of its case upon substituting counsel, it would have discovered the judgment and could have filed its motion for new trial earlier.

The plaintiff's delay in filing its motion for new trial until January 2017, approximately two months after the November 10, 2016 judgment, demonstrates a lack of due diligence that should weigh against annulling the judgment. In *Waguespack v. Waguespack*, 2013-1540 (La. App. 1 Cir. 9/23/14), 152 So. 3d 199, the court noted that delay may be considered in evaluating whether to annul a judgment, even where absolute nullity is alleged.

---

### IV. THE DEFENSE PRESERVES THE ARGUMENT THAT MASON V. MASON CONTROLS THE ABSOLUTE NULLITY ANALYSIS

The defense acknowledges the plaintiff's citation of *State ex rel. Guste v. Latt*, 97-1781 (La. 4/8/98), 710 So. 2d 1077, for the proposition that service not complying with statutory requirements is "not service at all." However, the defense maintains that *Mason v. Mason*, 2008-0919 (La. 1/21/09), 3 So. 3d 1125, provides the more appropriate framework for analyzing absolute nullities.

In *Mason*, the Louisiana Supreme Court limited absolute nullities to situations involving "no service whatsoever." The defense submits that where service is attempted at the address provided by the plaintiff itself, even if technically imperfect, this does not rise to the level of "no service whatsoever" that would render a judgment an absolute nullity. The distinction is critical: an absolute nullity should be reserved for cases where a party was completely deprived of notice, not for cases where a party received notice through imperfect means but still had an opportunity to respond.

### V. EVEN IF SERVICE WAS DEFECTIVE, THE TRIAL COURT PROPERLY EXERCISED ITS DISCRETION IN DENYING THE MOTION FOR NEW TRIAL

The standard of review for denial of a motion for new trial is abuse of discretion. In *Hall v. Folger Coffee Co.*, 2003-1644 (La. 4/14/04), 874 So. 2d 90, the Louisiana Supreme Court emphasized that trial courts have broad discretion in ruling on motions for new trial, and appellate courts should not disturb those rulings absent a clear abuse of discretion.

The defense submits that even if this Court finds some deficiency in service, the trial court did not abuse its discretion in denying the motion for new trial. The trial court was in the best position to evaluate the circumstances surrounding service and the prejudice, if any, suffered by Kingdom Builders. Given that Kingdom Builders ultimately received notice of the judgment and had an opportunity to file a motion for new trial, the trial court could reasonably conclude that the interests of justice did not require annulling the judgment.

For the foregoing reasons, the Louisiana Department of Education respectfully requests that this Court affirm the trial court's judgment denying Kingdom Builders' motion for new trial. The defense acknowledges that service was accomplished through methods that may not strictly comply with every jot and tittle of La. Code Civ. P. art. 1313(B). However, the fundamental purpose of service requirements—providing actual notice—was substantially achieved. Kingdom Builders cannot profit from its own failure to maintain current contact information, and the trial court properly exercised its discretion in denying relief.

**FINAL RULING**

**I. INTRODUCTION**

This matter is before the Court on an appeal by Kingdom Builders Community Development Corporation (Plaintiff) from a judgment denying its motion for new trial. Plaintiff appeals the trial court’s refusal to annul a judgment sustaining the Louisiana Department of Education’s (LDE) peremptory exception of no cause of action. The sole issue on appeal is whether the trial court abused its discretion in denying the motion for new trial based on Plaintiff’s claim that the judgment is an absolute nullity due to improper service of process. After reviewing the closing arguments of both parties and the applicable law, the Court finds that the trial court abused its discretion. LDE failed to comply with the mandatory service requirements of La. Code Civ. P. art. 1313(B), rendering the judgment an absolute nullity.

**II. STANDARD OF REVIEW**

The denial of a motion for new trial is reviewed under the abuse of discretion standard. *Hall v. Folger Coffee Co.*, 2003-1644 (La. 4/14/04), 874 So. 2d 90. However, whether a judgment constitutes an absolute nullity is a question of law reviewed de novo. *State ex rel. Guste v. Latt*, 97-1781 (La. 4/8/98), 710 So. 2d 1077.

**III. ANALYSIS**

**A. Violation of Mandatory Service Requirements Under La. Code Civ. P. art. 1313(B)**

The threshold issue is whether LDE’s service of the peremptory exception complied with the Louisiana Code of Civil Procedure. The record reflects that LDE served the exception by regular mail and facsimile. La. Code Civ. P. art. 1313(B) governs the service of exceptions when a hearing is set and provides: *"When a hearing is set for an exception, the exception shall be served by registered or certified mail, return receipt requested, or by the sheriff."*

The statute’s use of the word "shall" denotes a mandatory directive. The Defense argues that "substantial compliance" is sufficient because LDE served the address provided by Plaintiff and the purpose of the rule is to ensure notice. However, this argument is unavailing. Louisiana jurisprudence is clear that strict compliance is required when the statute mandates a specific method of service.

In *Boudreaux v. Louisiana Dep’t of Transp. & Dev.*, 2007-1234 (La. App. 1 Cir. 12/28/07), 976 So. 2d 827, the court held that service by regular mail for an exception setting a hearing fails to satisfy art. 1313(B) and renders service invalid. Similarly, in *Hicks v. State ex rel. Dep’t of Corr.*, 2005-1910 (La. App. 1 Cir. 9/22/06), 942 So. 2d 1172, the court found that fax service of exceptions setting a hearing is contrary to art. 1313(B) and void.

The Defense relies on *Comeaux v. Comeaux*, 2014-1168 (La. App. 3 Cir. 3/18/15), 162 So. 3d 751, to argue for substantial compliance. However, *Comeaux* is distinguishable. The doctrine of substantial compliance does not apply where the statute explicitly mandates a specific method of service and the serving party fails to utilize any of those methods. As noted in *State v. Broussard*, 2010-1351 (La. 11/10/11), 78 So. 3d 1075, statutory service requirements for exceptions setting a hearing are mandatory and non-discretionary. LDE’s use of regular mail and fax—methods not authorized by art. 1313(B)—constitutes a violation of the statute.

**B. The Judgment is an Absolute Nullity**

Because LDE failed to serve the exception in accordance with art. 1313(B), the service was invalid. Under La. Code Civ. P. art. 2002(A)(2), a judgment is an absolute nullity if it was "rendered without service of process."

The Defense argues that *Mason v. Mason*, 2008-0919 (La. 1/21/09), 3 So. 3d 1125, limits absolute nullities to situations involving "no service whatsoever." However, service that fails to comply with mandatory statutory requirements is legally equivalent to no service. In *State ex rel. Guste v. Latt*, 97-1781 (La. 4/8/98), 710 So. 2d 1077, the Louisiana Supreme Court stated: *"Service that does not comply with statutory requirements is not service at all. It is a nullity."*

Because LDE used regular mail and fax—methods expressly prohibited by art. 1313(B) for exceptions setting a hearing—there was no valid service of process. Consequently, the judgment sustaining the exception is an absolute nullity. The Defense’s reliance on *Richard v. Richard*, 2011-1438 (La. App. 3 Cir. 5/9/12), 91 So. 3d 494, is misplaced because *Richard* involved a scenario where the statute did not require a specific method of service, whereas art. 1313(B) does.

**C. Lack of Actual Knowledge and Waiver**

The Defense argues that Plaintiff waived the service defect by filing a motion to substitute counsel on October 3, 2016, and by delaying the filing of the motion for new trial. This argument is without merit.

A party cannot waive a right it was unaware of. The hearing on the exception took place on September 19, 2016. The motion to substitute counsel was filed on October 3, 2016, nearly two weeks *after* the hearing had already occurred. The record does not indicate that the motion to substitute counsel mentioned the exception hearing or the judgment. As held in *D束 v. Riche*, 2010-1553 (La. App. 1 Cir. 4/8/11), 63 So. 3d 463, waiver of service requires actual knowledge of the specific proceedings being challenged. Plaintiff could not have waived its right to attend the September 19 hearing when it had no notice of that hearing until after it was over.

Furthermore, the Defense’s argument regarding delay is inapplicable. An absolute nullity may be annulled at any time. See *Waguespack v. Waguespack*, 2013-1540 (La. App. 1 Cir. 9/23/14), 152 So. 3d 199. The fact that Plaintiff filed the motion for new trial seven weeks after the judgment does not bar the annulment of a null judgment.

Finally, the Defense’s contention that Plaintiff is responsible for the lack of service due to counsel’s failure to update his address is irrelevant. While parties are responsible for maintaining current contact information, see *In re Succession of Broussard*, 2012-827 (La. App. 3 Cir. 12/12/12), 105 So. 3d 895, this responsibility does not relieve a party serving process of the obligation to use the methods mandated by statute. Had LDE used certified mail or the sheriff as required by art. 1313(B), the failure of service might have been discovered or corrected prior to the hearing.

**IV. CONCLUSION**

The trial court abused its discretion in denying Plaintiff’s motion for new trial. LDE failed to serve the exception in accordance with the mandatory requirements of La. Code Civ. P. art. 1313(B). This failure constitutes a lack of service of process, rendering the judgment an absolute nullity under La. Code Civ. P. art. 2002(A)(2). Plaintiff did not waive its rights, as it had no actual knowledge of the hearing prior to its occurrence.

**Therefore, the trial court’s judgment denying the motion for new trial is reversed. The judgment sustaining LDE’s peremptory exception of no cause of action is annulled, and the matter is remanded for further proceedings.**
